6. NEPRA Distribution Code Chapter 7 — Insulation Coordination & Protection Requirements

The NEPRA Distribution Code 2019, Chapter 7 (Protection) and Chapter 8 (Insulation Coordination) impose specific requirements on 11kV switchgear connected to the distribution network in Pakistan.

6.1 Insulation Coordination (Chapter 8, IEC 60071-1 basis)

  • Rated lightning impulse withstand voltage (LIWV): Minimum 75kVp (peak) for 12kV class equipment per NEPRA Distribution Code Table 8.2. This matches IEC 62271-1 Standard Insulation Level (SIL) for Um = 12kV. Do not accept equipment rated at only 60kVp.
  • Rated short-duration power-frequency withstand voltage: 28kV (rms) 1-minute for Um = 12kV class.
  • Pakistan surge arrester coordination: Install ZnO surge arresters (11kV, 10kA, Class 2) at the cable entry to the RMU or VCB panel per NEPRA Distribution Code Clause 8.4. See SPD / surge protection guide for arrester selection details.

6.2 Protection Relay Requirements (Chapter 7)

  • Overcurrent (51) and Earth Fault (51N): Mandatory on all 11kV feeders connected to DISCO networks. IDMT characteristic — typically Normal Inverse (NI) per IEC 60255-151 for Pakistan DISCO grading studies.
  • Time-grading: WAPDA TSG-1 protection grading standard specifies a 0.4s CTI (Coordination Time Interval) between successive relay stages on 11kV feeders. Typical settings: incomer relay TMS 0.3–0.5; feeder relay TMS 0.1–0.2; PSOC (protection setting override certificate) required for grid-connected captive plants.
  • Single-phase earth fault sensitivity: Solidly earthed 11kV systems (LESCO, K-Electric) require ≥5% In minimum earth fault current detection. High-resistance earthed systems (some rural DISCOs) — use sensitive earth fault (SEF) relay, Io pickup ≤0.05A primary.
  • Auto-reclose (79): Permitted on 11kV overhead line feeders only; not permitted on underground cable feeders per NEPRA Distribution Code Clause 7.9.2. Confirm feeder type before specifying auto-reclose VCB.

8. Captive Power: Generator Feeder VCB + Directional OC Relay

⭐ Pakistan First on e-commerce: Directional OC/EF relay coordination for Pakistan captive power generator VCB — NEPRA regulation 2015 Clause 12.4 compliance.

Pakistan's energy situation has driven a surge in captive power generation. Any captive generator (gas/diesel) connected to the national grid at 11kV requires an interconnection VCB with specific protection functions that differ fundamentally from a simple feeder VCB.

8.1 Why Directional Relays (67/67N) are Mandatory

On a radial feeder from DISCO, a standard IDMT relay (51/51N) is adequate — fault current flows only from the source toward the load. On a captive power interconnection, current can flow in either direction: WAPDA feeds the factory during grid periods, and the generator feeds the factory (and potentially exports to the grid) during captive operation. A standard non-directional OC relay will trip incorrectly on load current during periods of heavy generator export. A directional relay (67) only trips when fault current flows in the specified tripping direction.

8.2 NEPRA Captive Power Regulation 2015 — Clause 12.4 Requirements

  • Dedicated VCB at the Point of Common Coupling (PCC) with the DISCO 11kV bus
  • Overcurrent protection (67/67N) with directional element — tripping direction set toward generator
  • Under-voltage (27) and over-voltage (59) protection at PCC
  • Under-frequency (81U) and over-frequency (81O): Pakistan grid nominal 50Hz; 81U set ≤47.5Hz, 81O set ≥51.5Hz per NEPRA Grid Code
  • Anti-islanding protection: ROCOF ≥0.5 Hz/s pickup (adjustable) AND vector shift ≥12° — NEPRA 2026 captive mandate for generators >1MW
  • Intertrip to generator main CB on DISCO supply restoration: prevents parallel operation outside NEPRA control

8.3 LESCO 2025 Protection Grading Study — Captive Plant Coordination

LESCO's Protection Coordination Study (2025 revision) requires captive plant 11kV VCBs to clear faults within 100ms for phase faults (IDMT stage time dial ≤0.05) and 150ms for earth faults at the generator terminals. This is significantly faster than a standard feeder relay. The implication: a standard IDMT relay with TMS 0.3 will NOT meet LESCO clearance time — specify a numerical relay with Definite Time Override (DTO) high-set stage at 50ms.

  1. ☐ Fault level confirmed from DISCO SCL study — not assumed. Request the Short Circuit Level (SCL) certificate from the DISCO protection department. Add 20% margin.
  2. ☐ Withdrawable or fixed? — Withdrawable mandatory for all industrial applications with >PKR 10,000/hr downtime cost. Fixed acceptable for standby/backup feeders only.
  3. ☐ 45°C derating declared on nameplate — Specify "Rated current at 45°C ambient" in purchase order.

  6. ☐ Cable gland IP rating — IP68 for underground cables; IP54 minimum for indoor panels on overhead cable entry.
  7. ☐ Coastal/corrosion spec — Karachi DHA/Clifton/SITE: SS316L tank (RMU), IP54 panel, conformal coat, EPDM gaskets, 80W anti-condensation heater.
  8. ☐ PSQCA / IEC certificate — Request IEC 62271-100 type test certificate from an accredited lab (KEMA, CESI, DNV, or equivalent). PSQCA mark required for any equipment sold retail in Pakistan.
  9. ☐ Spare parts confirmed — Trip coil, closing coil, spring set, vacuum interrupter part numbers confirmed with local distributor. Delivery time >8 weeks from China on some items — stock critical spares locally.
  10. ☐ HS code and import duty verified — Verify HS 8537.20.90 vs 8537.10.10 classification with clearing agent before LC opening.

12. 7 Costly Mistakes Pakistani Buyers Make with VCB/RMU

  1. Specifying fault level without a DISCO study
    Assuming 16kA is adequate everywhere in Pakistan. K-Electric SITE zone: 35kA actual. Undersized VCB explodes on a close-on-fault event — documented case Faisalabad 2024, PKR 4.8M panel replacement + 11-day shutdown.
  2. Buying a fixed VCB for an industrial application
    Fixed VCBs require full shutdown for maintenance. In Pakistan where WAPDA load-shedding already causes 3–8 forced outages/day, a scheduled maintenance shutdown loses another 4–8 hours. Withdrawable VCBs cost 20–40% more but pay back within one maintenance cycle in a busy factory.
  3. Using standard IDMT relay (51/51N) for a captive power interconnection
    A non-directional relay on a captive plant 11kV feeder will trip on generator export current, causing nuisance trips and potential generator damage. Always use 67/67N for any bidirectional current flow point.
  4. Not specifying SS316L for Karachi coastal RMU
    Standard carbon-steel epoxy RMU at coastal Karachi: 3–5 year paint failure, busbar corrosion, dielectric degradation. Replacement cost PKR 700,000–1,200,000. SS316L cost premium: PKR 80,000–150,000. ROI: immediate.
  5. Omitting anti-condensation heaters
    Pakistan switchgear rooms experience 40–60°C temperature swings between summer afternoon and monsoon night. Without heaters, condensation forms on cold metal surfaces — causing insulation tracking faults, relay misoperation, and busbar arcing. Anti-condensation heater cost: PKR 3,000–8,000. Busbar replacement after tracking fault: PKR 150,000–800,000.
  6. Accepting a 40kA VCB when 25kA is needed
    Over-specifying also costs money — a 40kA withdrawable VCB costs PKR 350,000 more than a 25kA unit. On IESCO Islamabad suburban feeders (10–18kA), a 20kA VCB is fully adequate. Request the DISCO SCL study; do not assume worst-case everywhere.
  7. Specifying auto-reclose on underground cable feeders
    Auto-reclose is beneficial on overhead lines (temporary arc faults clear on reclosure). On underground XLPE cables, auto-reclose onto a cable fault causes a second, larger fault, destroying the cable and potentially the VCB. NEPRA Distribution Code Clause 7.9.2 prohibits auto-reclose on underground cable feeders. Disable or omit auto-reclose function for all cable-fed applications.

Q5: What NEPRA requirements apply to VCB for captive power generators in Pakistan?

NEPRA Captive Power Regulation 2015 Clause 12.4 requires a dedicated VCB at PCC with directional OC (67) and EF (67N) relays. Trip within 100ms on islanding detection. Anti-islanding: ROCOF ≥0.5 Hz/s AND vector shift ≥12°. Under/over voltage (27/59) and under/over frequency (81U/81O) protection mandatory. A protection setting coordination certificate (PSOC) is required from the DISCO before interconnection approval.

Q6: Why is VCB preferred over SF6 in Pakistan for indoor switchgear?

VCBs are preferred for Pakistani indoor applications: no SF6 gas handling; maintenance-free vacuum interrupter (10,000+ ops); no pressure monitoring in 45°C+ ambient; lower TCO. SF6 remains preferred for RMUs and outdoor AIS where hermetic sealing is critical. See Table 1 for full technology comparison.

Q7: How does Pakistan's 45°C ambient affect VCB derating?

IEC 62271-1 rates VCBs at 40°C. At 45°C (Pakistan standard summer): derate continuous current 5–9%. A 630A VCB at 45°C in Lahore: ~573–593A effective. At 50°C (Multan peak): 542–561A. Always select one size up or specify "Ith at 45°C" on the nameplate. See Table 6 for city-by-city derating.

Q8: What is the difference between a fixed and withdrawable VCB for Pakistan applications?

Fixed VCBs require power shutdown for maintenance. Withdrawable VCBs can be racked out without de-energising the busbars. In Pakistan with 3–8 WAPDA load-shedding events/day, withdrawable VCBs are essential for any factory where downtime cost exceeds PKR 10,000/hr. All WAPDA DISCO substations use withdrawable VCBs. For industrial applications: always specify withdrawable.
