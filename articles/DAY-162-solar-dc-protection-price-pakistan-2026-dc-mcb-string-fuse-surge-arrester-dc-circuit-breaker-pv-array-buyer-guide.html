1. Why DC Protection Is Different — The Zero-Crossing Problem

When an AC circuit breaker opens, the current naturally passes through zero 100 times per second (50 Hz × 2). The arc self-extinguishes at that zero-crossing. In a DC circuit there is no zero-crossing — the arc sustains indefinitely until the arc voltage exceeds the supply voltage or the gap becomes large enough.

Key takeaway: Every protection device on the DC side of a PV system — MCB, MCCB, isolator, fuse — must carry a specific DC voltage and current rating and must be tested to the relevant DC standard. Marking "600V" on an AC device does not make it safe for 600V DC.

2. DC MCBs & DC MCCBs — Ratings, Types & Brands

2.1 What Makes a DC MCB Different

  • Series poles: A 500V DC MCB wires two poles in series (2P-1) to achieve the required contact gap. A 1000V DC MCB uses four poles in series (4P-1). Never connect a 2P DC MCB across 1000V single-polarity.
  • Arc chutes with permanent magnets: DC MCBs contain blow-out magnets (arc-blowing coils) to rapidly extend and cool the arc.
  • Standard: IEC 60898-1 (up to 125A, PV applications up to 1500V DC), IEC 60947-2 for industrial DC MCCBs.

2.3 Key Specifications to Check Before Buying

  • Ics (service breaking capacity): Must exceed the DC short-circuit current available at the installation point (typically 3–8 kA for PV systems).
  • Polarity: Some DC MCBs are unipolar (one polarity only) — confirm +/− marking. Most 2P or 4P series wired are bipolar.
  • Temperature derating: On Pakistani rooftops (panel/combiner box ambient 55–70°C), apply IEC 60947-2 temperature derating curves. A 32A MCB derated to 25A at 60°C.
  • IP rating: Minimum IP40 for indoor panel; IP54 for rooftop combiner box; IP65 for exposed rooftop disconnect.

3. String Fuse Holders & gPV Fuse Links

3.1 Why String Fuses Are Needed

When two or more PV strings are connected in parallel, a shaded or failed string can receive reverse current from the healthy strings. If the reverse current exceeds the string cable rating, the cable insulation can burn inside the conduit. String fuses protect each string against this reverse current scenario.

IEC 60269-6 defines the gPV (general purpose PV) fuse class — specifically designed for DC PV circuit protection with:

  • Rated voltage up to 1500V DC
  • High breaking capacity for DC (25–50 kA)
  • Low melting I²t to protect PV cables
  • Current-limiting action on fault

4. DC Surge Protective Devices (SPD Type II) for PV Arrays

4.1 Why DC SPD Is Critical in Pakistan

Pakistan's Punjab, KPK and GB regions experience 20–60 lightning strikes per km² per year (Keraunic Level 20–60). A single nearby lightning strike induces 2–10 kV transients on PV array cables. Without a DC SPD:

  • Inverter IGBT transistors fail (replacement cost PKR 25,000–80,000)
  • Monitoring string input modules fail
  • Panel junction boxes and bypass diodes fail
  • Fire risk from sustained insulation breakdown at the cable entry point

4.3 DC SPD Configuration for PV

  • +/− to PE (ground) mode: The most common configuration. Two MOV (metal oxide varistor) legs: one from +DC to PE, one from −DC to PE. Protects against common-mode surges.
  • +/− to +/− (differential mode): Protects against differential surges between the two DC conductors. Used in addition to common-mode SPD for high-risk sites.
  • Backup fuse: Every DC SPD requires a backup fuse (or MCB) sized per manufacturer data sheet — typically 32A–63A. Without a backup fuse, a failed SPD (short-circuit mode) can sustain a DC arc.
  • Remote indication: Commercial DC SPDs include a visual indicator window (green = OK, red = failed) and a remote alarm contact. Connect to monitoring system or panel alarm circuit.

5. DC Load Break Isolators & PV Disconnects

5.1 Purpose & Standards

A DC isolator (load break switch/disconnector) provides a visible, lockable off point for PV array maintenance and emergency shutdown. It differs from an MCB in that its primary function is isolation, not overcurrent protection — though it must be capable of breaking the full load current.

Standards:

  • IEC 60947-3 — Low-voltage switchgear: Switches, disconnectors, switch-disconnectors and fuse-combination units
  • Utilisation category DC-21B: switching of resistive loads (applies to PV array DC output)
  • Utilisation category DC-23B: switching of motor loads (applies to tracking systems)

All LESCO, IESCO, FESCO, MEPCO grid-tie solar approvals require a visible, externally accessible DC disconnect mounted within 1.8 metres of the inverter, lockable in the OFF position, clearly labelled "PV DC ISOLATOR". 10. Installation & Wiring Best Practices

  1. DC cable sizing: Size DC cables for Isc × 1.25 (NEC / IEC 60364-7-712). Minimum 4 mm² for most residential strings; 6–10 mm² for multi-string combiner to inverter runs. Use PV-rated solar cable (TÜV 2PfG 1169 or EN 50618 — UV-resistant, double insulated, 1500V DC rated).
  2. Polarity marking: Label all DC conductors — red for +DC, black for −DC. Never reverse polarity to a string inverter (immediate MOSFET failure).
  3. Ground fault protection: On ungrounded (floating) PV systems, install a ground fault detection relay (GFDI) or use an inverter with built-in ground fault monitoring. Grounded (−DC grounded) systems require a fuse in the grounded conductor only where code requires, but consult local DISCO.
  4. Torque settings: Over-tightening combiner bus connections causes micro-cracking in fuse holders and MCB terminals. Use a calibrated torque screwdriver: fuse terminal 2–3 Nm, MCB terminal 2.5–4 Nm per manufacturer data.
  5. Thermal management in combiner boxes: On Pakistani rooftops, combiner box internal temperature can reach 65–75°C. Apply 10% upward current derating per 10°C above 40°C per IEC standards. Use ventilated or thermally conductive enclosures where possible.
  6. Earthing the PV frame: Every panel frame must be bonded to the main earthing system (PE busbar in combiner box → main earth terminal → earthing electrode). Use 4 mm² green-yellow cable or green-yellow lugs on the frame mounting bolts. Ref: Earthing System Design Pakistan 2026 Buyer Guide.

13. FAQs

Can I use a standard AC MCB for DC protection in a solar PV system?

No. AC MCBs are designed to interrupt current at the natural zero-crossing of an AC waveform. DC current has no zero-crossing, so an AC MCB cannot reliably extinguish a DC arc and may weld its contacts shut, creating a fire hazard. Always use devices specifically rated and marked for DC voltage (e.g., 600V DC or 1000V DC per IEC 60947-2 or IEC 60898-1 DC).

What fuse rating should I use for a 400W solar string in Pakistan?

For a typical 400W monocrystalline panel with Isc ≈ 10A, the string fuse should be rated at 1.25 × Isc for the string. For a single string: 1.25 × 10 = 12.5A — use a 15A gPV fuse (IEC 60269-6). For 2 parallel strings each at 10A: fuse per string = 15A; combiner output fuse = 25A. Never upsize beyond 2 × Isc without manufacturer confirmation.

Is a DC surge arrester required by NEPRA for rooftop solar in Pakistan?

NEPRA SRO 892(I)/2015 (Net Metering Regulations) mandates grid-compliant inverters and protective relays but does not explicitly itemise SPD Type II DC. However, IEC 62109-1 (safety of power converters for PV) and standard insurance policies in Pakistan require Type II DC SPD (IEC 61643-11) on the PV array DC input side. For any system >10kW or in lightning-prone regions (Punjab/KPK foothills), DC SPD is effectively mandatory to maintain warranty on the inverter and comply with insurer requirements.

What is the difference between a string fuse and a DC MCB for PV protection?

A string fuse (gPV, IEC 60269-6) is a passive overcurrent device sized per string Isc — it protects individual strings against reverse current from parallel strings. A DC MCB (IEC 60898-1 or 60947-2) is manually switchable, provides overcurrent AND short-circuit protection for the entire array output or sub-array, and can serve as a local DC disconnect. For systems with 2+ parallel strings, use string fuses at combiner level PLUS a DC MCB at inverter input. A single-string system only needs the DC MCB.

What DC isolator rating do I need for a 10kW on-grid inverter in Pakistan?

For a typical 10kW on-grid inverter with Voc(max) ≈ 800V DC and Isc(array) ≈ 15A, use a DC load break isolator rated ≥ 1000V DC, ≥ 25A, with IP54 weatherproofing for rooftop installation. IEC 60947-3 Section 5.3 (utilisation category DC-21B/DC-23B) governs load break isolators. All Pakistan grid-tied solar installations require a visible, lockable DC disconnect within 1.8m of the inverter per PEPCO/DISCO grid connection requirements.
