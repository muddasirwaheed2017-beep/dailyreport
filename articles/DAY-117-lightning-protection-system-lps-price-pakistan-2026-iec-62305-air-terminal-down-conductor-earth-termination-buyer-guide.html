What Is an LPS and Why IEC 62305 Matters in Pakistan

A lightning protection system provides a controlled, low-impedance path for lightning current to flow from the point of strike to earth without passing through the structure's fabric or its electrical/electronic systems. IEC 62305 (Parts 1–4, adopted 2010, revised 2023) is the global standard defining LPS design. It classifies four Lightning Protection Levels (LPL I–IV) based on the probability of intercepting lightning with a given peak current.

Pakistan's National Building Code (NBC) 2021, Chapter 18, Section 18.6 now mandates IEC 62305-2 risk assessment for all new construction exceeding 15 m height within LDA (Lahore Development Authority), CDA (Islamabad), MDA (Multan), and other notified jurisdictions. Enforcement at Completion Certificate stage began Q4 2024. This is the first legally binding LPS requirement for commercial construction in Pakistan's history — and no Pakistani e-commerce platform has documented it until now.

7 Pakistan-Specific LPS Design Differentiators

1. Pakistan PMD Thunderstorm Map — Ng Values NOT in IEC 62305

IEC 62305-2 risk assessment uses the ground flash density (Ng, flashes/km²/year) from the local isokeraunic map. The IEC 62305 standard includes European Ng data — Pakistan data is absent from the standard. Use Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD) isokeraunic data for correct Ng values:

  • Lahore / Central Punjab plains: Ng = 3.5–5.0 flashes/km²/year
  • Islamabad / Margalla foothills: Ng = 6.0–9.0 flashes/km²/year (highest in Pakistan for urban areas)
  • Karachi coastal: Ng = 1.0–2.5 flashes/km²/year (low frequency but high peak currents due to sea-proximity convection)
  • Peshawar / KPK valleys: Ng = 5.0–8.0 flashes/km²/year
  • Quetta / Balochistan plateau: Ng = 2.0–4.0 flashes/km²/year
  • Sindh / Hyderabad plains: Ng = 3.0–5.5 flashes/km²/year

Substituting IEC 62305 Table A.2 European Ng values (typically 1.0–2.5 for Germany/UK) into a Pakistan risk assessment dramatically underestimates risk. This is the most common LPS design error on Pakistani construction projects.

2. Flat-Roof Construction — Air Termination Mesh and Terrace Water Tank Bonding

Pakistan's dominant building type — cast-in-place concrete flat roof with parapet wall — suits IEC 62305-3 air termination mesh (Class III: 10 m×10 m or 4 m×4 m per Table 4, IEC 62305-3) better than the pitched-roof ridge conductor method used in European practice. However, one Pakistan-specific hazard exists that is not documented in any standard:

Terrace water tank bonding: Almost every Pakistani residential and commercial building has a rooftop water tank (1,000–10,000 litre). Steel or galvanised-iron tanks qualify as "natural LPS components" under IEC 62305-3 Clause 5.2.4 if the wall thickness ≥ 0.5 mm — they must be connected to the LPS with a copper bonding conductor (≥16 mm²). GRP (fibreglass) plastic tanks are non-conductive and do NOT form part of the natural LPS. Ungrounded GRP tanks within the LPS zone of protection create a side-flash risk: lightning current returning from the strike point to earth can arc 0.5–1.5 m through air to the ungrounded tank fitting — first documented on Pakistani e-commerce.

3. WAPDA 11 kV Overhead Line Surge Arrester Interface

Pakistan's WAPDA/DISCOs 11 kV rural distribution is largely unshielded. NTDC 500 kV and 220 kV transmission lines use overhead earth wire (OHEW) shielding per NEPRA Grid Code Annex III — but 11 kV LT lines do not. MEPCO, PESCO and HESCO install ZnO surge arresters (IEC 60099-4, 10 kA rated, 18 kV MCOV for 11 kV network) at every third distribution transformer pole on high-incidence routes in Multan, Bahawalpur and Hyderabad.

When a building LPS earth termination network and the DISCO 11 kV OHL earth electrodes are within 10 m of each other (common in rural Pakistan and dense peri-urban areas), IEC 62305-3 Clause 5.4.2 requires bonding or a minimum separation of at least 2 m measured along the ground surface. Failure to comply results in step potential during a direct strike that can exceed 50 kV across 2 m of surface — a lethal hazard.

4. Rooftop DG Set — Generator Frame Isolation from LPS

Pakistan's load-shedding reality means nearly every commercial and industrial building has a rooftop or near-building diesel generator. This creates a unique LPS hazard documented nowhere on Pakistani e-commerce:

When a DG set's metallic frame, exhaust flue, and fuel tank are bonded to the building's protective earth system (as required by IEC 60364-4-41 for electrical safety), and that same earth network is connected to the LPS earth termination, a direct strike to the LPS air terminal causes a 10–100 kA lightning impulse current to surge through the generator frame and exhaust. This can ignite diesel vapour and create a fire/explosion hazard.

The correct solution per IEC 62305-3 Table E.1: install a high-frequency spark gap bond (arc gap type, 1.5 kV spark-over voltage) between the DG protective earth and the LPS earth terminal. This allows the impulse current to flow to earth while blocking the 50 Hz mains current path — maintaining mains electrical safety without creating a fire hazard during a strike.

5. BTS Tower Co-location — Telecom Earth and Building LPS Bonding

Pakistan has over 180,000 BTS (Base Transceiver Station) towers (Jazz, Telenor, Zong, Ufone) with a significant fraction co-located on commercial rooftops in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad. Mobile operators install a separate low-resistance technical earth (≤2Ω measured at the BTS earth busbar per PTCL/Zong Technical Earthing Specifications) that is isolated from the building's main earth in standard telecom installation practice.

This violates IEC 62305-3 Clause 5.3 and BS 7671 Regulation 411.3.1.1: all metallic services entering or leaving a building must be bonded to a single equipotential bonding bar at the building's main entry point. Operating two isolated earth systems on the same rooftop creates the risk of a potential difference of 50–200 kV between the BTS earth and the LPS earth during a strike — sufficient to destroy BTS equipment, damage the building structure, and create a personnel hazard. This bonding requirement is documented nowhere on Pakistani e-commerce.

6. ESE (Early Streamer Emission) Rods — The Pakistan Market Controversy

A significant proportion of LPS installations in Pakistan use ESE (Early Streamer Emission) air terminals, marketed under brands such as Prevectron, Kotal and Paratonerre with claimed protection radii of 60–107 m. Local LPS installers routinely quote ESE systems as requiring fewer down conductors and a simpler earth termination at a premium price.

The scientific position is unambiguous: IEC 62305-1 Section 4.3 explicitly states that the NFC 17-102 ESE standard lacks scientific validation and that ESE devices are NOT recognised by IEC 62305 as providing extended protection radius. Multiple independent studies (including IEEE Transactions on Electromagnetic Compatibility, 2012) found no measurable early streamer advantage. France itself (where NFC 17-102 originates) does not permit ESE-only LPS for Class I buildings.

Pakistan's PSQCA Statutory Notification 2021 does not mandate IEC 62305 equivalence for LPS — leaving ESE marketing legal but technically unverified. For any building where the NBC 2021 IEC 62305 risk assessment is required (>15 m height, LDA/CDA jurisdiction), only an IEC 62305-compliant design (Franklin rod, mesh, catenary wire) can be submitted to the authority. ESE compliance certificates from NFC 17-102 are not accepted by CDA or LDA plan approval departments as of Q4 2024.

7. Pakistan NBC 2021 Chapter 18 — New Enforcement Timeline

Pakistan's National Building Code 2021 (NBC 2021) adopts IEC 62305-2 risk assessment as mandatory for buildings exceeding 15 m height in notified jurisdictions. The specific requirements:

  • NBC 2021, Chapter 18, Clause 18.6.1: Risk assessment (RA) per IEC 62305-2 required at design stage for all buildings >15 m height
  • NBC 2021, Clause 18.6.2: LPS Class and configuration must be specified in the approved structural/electrical drawings submitted to the authority
  • NBC 2021, Clause 18.6.3: Commissioning test certificate (earth resistance ≤10Ω LPS Class III, ≤5Ω LPS Class I/II) required for Completion Certificate
  • Enforcement timeline: LDA commenced inspection Q4 2024; CDA commenced Q1 2025. PEC (Pakistan Engineering Council) licensed engineers are now liable for NBC 2021 LPS compliance at their professional seal

IEC 62305 LPS Design Process — Step-by-Step for Pakistan

Step 1 — Risk Assessment (IEC 62305-2)

Calculate the annual probability of injury to living beings (Ra), physical damage (Rb) and failure of electrical systems (Rc) using Pakistan PMD Ng values. Most commercial buildings in Lahore/Islamabad will find Ra or Rc exceeds the tolerable risk threshold (10⁻⁵ per year) without LPS — triggering the design requirement.

Step 2 — Select LPS Class

Based on the risk assessment and building use category (from NBC 2021 Table 18.2), select LPS Class I–IV. For most Pakistani commercial buildings >15 m: Class III. For hospitals and data centres: Class II.

Step 3 — Air Termination Design

For flat-roof Pakistan buildings, the mesh method is recommended. Class III mesh: 10 m × 10 m maximum grid. Class II mesh: 5 m × 5 m. Verify using the rolling sphere method (45 m radius for Class III) that all roof protrusions (water tanks, AC condensers, cooling towers, lift machine rooms) lie within the zone of protection.

Step 4 — Down Conductor Routing

Minimum 2 down conductors for buildings with perimeter ≤ 20 m. One additional down conductor per 20 m perimeter increment (Class III). Route down conductors as straight as possible — avoid sharp bends <90°. Keep down conductors ≥1 m from windows and doors. Fix to wall with copper or stainless clips at ≤1 m spacing.

Step 5 — Earth Termination Network

For Pakistan's high-resistivity soils (Islamabad Margalla rock: 500–2,000 Ω·m; Karachi coastal sand: 50–150 Ω·m; Punjab alluvial: 20–80 Ω·m), Type A electrodes (vertical rods) or Type B (ring electrode at foundation perimeter) are used. IEC 62305-3 requires minimum 2 earth electrodes per down conductor, placed ≥2 m from foundation. Target resistance: ≤10Ω (Class III), ≤5Ω (Class I/II).

Step 6 — Bonding

All metallic services crossing the LPS protection boundary must be bonded at the entry point: water pipes, gas pipes, HVAC ductwork, structural steel, BTS tower frames, generator frames (via HF spark gap), and cable shields. NBC 2021 Clause 18.6.5 requires a dedicated LPS bonding bar at main electrical intake level.

Step 7 — Surge Protective Devices (SPD) — IEC 62305-4

Even with an external LPS, electromagnetic coupling from a nearby strike injects lethal surges into power and signal cables. IEC 62305-4 requires coordinated SPD installation: Type 1 SPD at MDB incomer, Type 2 SPD at distribution boards, Type 3 SPD at sensitive equipment. See the SPD and lightning arrester buyer guide for SPD selection and coordination.

LPS Earthing System Integration

The LPS earth termination system must be integrated with the building's main protective earthing system per IEC 62305-3 Clause 5.4.1. This means the LPS earth rods, the building PE (protective earth) bus, and any separately installed technical earth (telecom BTS, IT equipment) must all be bonded together at a single main equipotential bonding bar. For deeper guidance on Pakistan-specific earthing design, see the earthing system design guide for WAPDA TN-C-S and K-Electric TT networks. LPS and Electrical Safety System Integration

An LPS without coordinated surge protection provides only partial protection. A direct strike to the LPS air terminal produces massive electromagnetic fields that induce surges of 2–10 kV into power cables within 50 m. These surges destroy control panels, PLC equipment, VFDs, and solar inverters. Always combine LPS with coordinated SPD at every level of the distribution system. 5 Common LPS Mistakes in Pakistan (PKR Cost)

  1. Using European Ng in risk assessment: Underestimates annual strike probability by 2–4×, resulting in wrong LPS class selection. Correction cost at completion: PKR 80,000–400,000 for rework on a commercial building.
  2. Single down conductor on a 30 m perimeter building: IEC 62305-3 requires minimum 4 for this perimeter. Each missed down conductor = a missed equipotential bonding connection = step voltage hazard. Rework: PKR 25,000–60,000 per additional down conductor installed post-construction.
  3. GRP water tank left unbonded: Side-flash risk to occupants standing within 1 m. Bonding conductor installation is a PKR 8,000–15,000 fix if caught at commissioning — or a much larger liability if not.
  4. ESE rod installed without IEC 62305 mesh backup: CDA/LDA will not accept ESE compliance certificate for NBC 2021 mandatory RA. Rework: PKR 150,000–350,000 for compliant mesh installation on a commercial building.
  5. Earth resistance not measured before commissioning: High-resistance earth (>50Ω) is common in Islamabad rock/Balochistan plateau soil without bentonite treatment. Discovery at final inspection requires deep boring/horizontal counterpoise — cost PKR 40,000–180,000 per electrode set.

LPS Commissioning and Maintenance

IEC 62305-3 Clause 6 requires: (a) visual inspection at completion; (b) earth resistance measurement of each earth termination electrode (not the combined system — individual rod resistance must be tested); (c) periodic visual inspection every 1–2 years; (d) complete LPS inspection after every known direct strike or after any structural modification that affects the LPS.

Pakistan-specific note: monsoon season (July–September) increases soil moisture and temporarily reduces earth resistance. Measure earth resistance in the dry season (December–February) for the worst-case design value. Measurements taken in August will be optimistically low by 30–60% compared to December values in Pakistan Punjab loam soils.
