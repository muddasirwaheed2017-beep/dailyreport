Electrical Design Software & Load Calculation Pakistan 2026 — Cable Sizing, Short-Circuit, kVAr & LESCO Load Application Buyer Guide

Getting the electrical design right before construction starts saves significant rework costs in Pakistan, where LESCO, MEPCO, GEPCO, HESCO and other DISCOs all require stamped load calculations before approving a new industrial or commercial connection. This guide covers the complete workflow: how to calculate connected and demand loads, how to size cables per IEC 60364-5-52, how to run a symmetrical fault study, how to size kVAr for NEPRA power-factor compliance, and what software Pakistani engineers are actually using in 2026 — with current PKR prices for licences and recommended hardware.

1. Why Formal Load Calculations Matter in Pakistan

Under the Pakistan Electricity Rules 1937 (amended) and NEPRA's Connection Standards, any industrial or commercial consumer above 5 kW must submit a load schedule with the connection application. DISCOs verify:

  • Sanctioned load vs connected load — the tariff category (A1/A2/B1/B2/C1/C2/C3 industrial, B-III/IV commercial) is tied to your approved sanctioned load in kVA.
  • Protection coordination — the DISCO incoming breaker/fuse must discriminate with the consumer's incomers. Wrong cable sizing causes nuisance tripping or, worse, catastrophic failure under fault.
  • Power factor penalty — NEPRA's Fuel Price Component surcharge mechanism includes a PF penalty below 0.85 lagging. An APFC panel sized from a proper kVAr calculation avoids this penalty.
  • Tariff metering accuracy — undersized current transformers (CT ratio mismatch) or inaccurate demand estimation leads to billing disputes with LESCO/MEPCO.

2.3 Power Factor Correction

Once design kW and PF are known:

kVAr required = kW × (tan φ₁ − tan φ₂)

Where:
  φ₁ = existing power factor angle (e.g., 0.75 → φ₁ = 41.4°, tan φ₁ = 0.882)
  φ₂ = target power factor angle (e.g., 0.95 → φ₂ = 18.2°, tan φ₂ = 0.329)

Example: 500 kW load at 0.75 PF → target 0.95:
  kVAr = 500 × (0.882 − 0.329) = 500 × 0.553 = 276.5 kVAr → select 275 kVAr APFC panel

3. Cable Sizing per IEC 60364-5-52

3.1 The Six-Step Sizing Procedure

  1. Determine design current (Ib) — from load kW, voltage, PF and efficiency.
  2. Select installation method — IEC 60364-5-52 reference methods: A1 (in conduit in wall), B1 (conduit on wall), C (clipped direct), D1 (underground duct), E (free air). Each has its own base ampacity table.
  3. Apply derating factors (Ca × Cg × Cc × Cs):
    • Ca — ambient temperature derating (IEC Table B.52.14; Pakistan summer peak: 45–50°C in Karachi/Multan, derating factor ~0.71 at 50°C for 70°C PVC cable)
    • Cg — grouping derating (IEC Table B.52.17; 3 circuits in flat touching = 0.70)
    • Cc — conductor temperature correction
    • Cs — soil thermal resistivity correction (for underground cables)
  4. Tabulated current It ≥ Ib / (Ca × Cg) — select cable size from IEC Table B.52.4 (copper XLPE) or B.52.3 (copper PVC).
  5. Voltage drop check — IEC 60364-5-52: ≤4% for final circuits, ≤2% LV distribution switchboards, ≤5% total. Formula: ΔV = √3 × Ib × L × (R cosφ + X sinφ) for 3-phase.
  6. Fault current withstand check — IEC 60364-4-43 / IEC 60364-5-52 Annex D: minimum cross-section for fault energy let-through.

3.2 Pakistan-Specific Cable Sizing Notes

  • Ground temperature: 30°C default (IEC) vs typical 35°C in Pakistan plains — always use local ambient in derating.
  • Soil thermal resistivity (ρT): Pakistan dry soil 2.5 K·m/W (vs IEC default 1.0 K·m/W) — underground cables derate significantly; add one size as rule of thumb.
  • WAPDA/DISCO approved cables: LT armoured XLPE 3.5-core or 4-core per WAPDA spec. See LT Armoured Cable Pakistan 2026 guide for approved specifications and PKR prices.
  • Voltage level: Pakistan LV system is 400 V L-L / 230 V L-N (50 Hz). 11 kV is the dominant MV level for industrial consumers.

4. Short-Circuit Current Calculation

4.1 Per-Unit (Zpu) Method for LV Bus Fault

The symmetrical three-phase fault current at the LV bus of a distribution transformer is the most critical value for breaker rating and cable withstand verification.

Base kVA = transformer rated kVA (e.g., 1,000 kVA)
Base voltage = LV voltage (e.g., 415 V)
Base impedance: Zbase = V² / kVA = 415² / 1,000,000 = 0.172 Ω

Transformer impedance: Zpu(transformer) = %Z / 100 (e.g., 5% → Zpu = 0.05)
Utility source impedance: Zpu(source) ≈ kVA(transformer) / kVA(fault MVA × 1000)
  e.g., LESCO 11kV fault level 250 MVA → Zpu(source) = 1,000 / 250,000 = 0.004 pu

Total Zpu = Zpu(source) + Zpu(transformer) = 0.004 + 0.05 = 0.054 pu

Isc(3ph) = Ibase / Zpu
  Ibase = kVA / (√3 × kV) = 1,000,000 / (1.732 × 415) = 1,390 A
  Isc = 1,390 / 0.054 = 25,740 A ≈ 25.7 kA

→ Select MCCBs and ACBs with a minimum 25 kA rated short-circuit capacity (Icu)

For sub-distribution boards (SDB) downstream, add cable impedance (R + jX per metre from IEC or cable manufacturer data) to reduce available fault current. Refer to our MCCB Pakistan 2026 guide for Icu ratings and recommended applications.

6. LESCO/DISCO Load Application Process

6.1 Required Documents for Industrial Connection Application

  1. Load Schedule (signed by Licensed Electrical Contractor / Electrical Engineer) — connected load per phase, demand load, PF, diversity factor applied.
  2. Single Line Diagram (SLD) — from metering point to main distribution board; must show transformer kVA, protection scheme, cable sizes, earthing system.
  3. Property documents — allotment letter or sale deed for industrial plots (SITE, MITE, Korangi, Sundar IEP, etc.).
  4. NTN/CNIC of applicant
  5. NOC from NEPRA — for connections above 500 kW (varies by DISCO).
  6. EI Certificate — Electrical Inspectorate approval for the internal wiring before connection is energised. (Refer to our Industrial Electrical Installation Standards guide for complete EI inspection requirements.)

6.2 Sanctioned Load vs Connected Load

DISCOs sanction a load in kVA. Your tariff category is determined by this sanctioned load:

  • B-III / B-IV: Commercial (B-III <5 kW, B-IV >5 kW)
  • C-1: Industrial <70 kW
  • C-2: Industrial 70–500 kW
  • C-3: Industrial >500 kW, peak-off-peak ToU tariff applies
  • H-1 / H-2: High tension (11 kV connection) — typically >1 MVA

Caution: Over-applying sanctioned load to get a higher tariff bracket can result in a minimum demand charge even in months of low consumption. Under-applying results in penalties when actual MD exceeds sanctioned load (MD tariff surcharge).

7. Panel Layout Design Integration

Once load calculations are complete, the next step is translating them into a panel layout. Key inputs that feed from the load calculation into the panel design:

  • Design current (Ib) per circuit → busbar ampacity, MCB/MCCB rating selection
  • Short-circuit current at each board → Icu rating, Type 2 coordination verification
  • Cable sizes → gland sizes, cable entry planning, cable duct fill calculation
  • kVAr sizing → APFC panel section within MDB or separate panel

9. Commissioning Handover Documentation

A proper electrical design package for LESCO submission and project handover includes:

  1. Load schedule (Excel/PDF, signed and stamped)
  2. Single line diagram (AutoCAD or manually drawn, A1 size minimum)
  3. Cable schedule (origin, destination, length, size, type, installation method)
  4. Short-circuit study results (Isc at each board, equipment Icu vs available Isc)
  5. Coordination study (TCC curves or tabulated discrimination criteria)
  6. Earthing design calculation (resistance target, electrode configuration)
  7. kVAr sizing calculation (PF, kW, kVAr required, capacitor bank BOM)
  8. Lighting design (DIALux or lux calculation sheet, per PSQCA lighting standards)
  9. Panel layout drawings (component placement, busbar arrangement, cable entry)
  10. As-built drawings (after installation, before EI Inspectorate visit)

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is ETAP required for LESCO submissions or will Excel calculations be accepted?
A: LESCO and most Pakistani DISCOs accept hand calculations or Excel spreadsheets if signed by a licensed electrical engineer (registered with PEC under Electrical Engineer category). ETAP or Amtech reports are accepted but not mandatory except for projects above 500 kW where a formal protection coordination study may be requested.

Q: What voltage drop limit does LESCO enforce?
A: LESCO follows WAPDA construction standards which reference IEC 60364 — max 4% VD in final circuits, 2.5% in distribution, 5% total end-to-end. In practice for industrial connections, a total 5% VD at full load is the accepted limit in DISCO review.

Q: How do I get the 11 kV fault level at my site from LESCO?
A: Submit an official application to LESCO's Technical Services / New Connection department requesting "System Fault Level Data at proposed connection point." Processing time is typically 2–4 weeks. Alternatively, a licensed LESCO-approved electrical consultant may obtain this data through LESCO's technical liaison channel.

Q: Our factory has large VFDs — does this affect cable sizing?
A: Yes, VFDs generate current harmonics (primarily 5th and 7th). High harmonic content causes neutral conductor overloading in 3-phase 4-wire systems. Size the neutral conductor at 130–173% of the phase conductor for drives constituting >20% of total load, per IEC 60364-5-523 annex guidance.
