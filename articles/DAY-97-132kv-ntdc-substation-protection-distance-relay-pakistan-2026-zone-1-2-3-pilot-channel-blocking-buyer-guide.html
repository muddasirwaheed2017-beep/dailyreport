1. Why 132kV Protection Is Different From 11/33kV

Pakistan's NTDC sub-transmission operates 132kV as the primary backbone linking generating sources (IPPs, hydro, CPEC coal/RE plants) to 33kV distribution grids via Grid Stations (GS). As of 2026, NTDC operates 560+ 132kV bays across 170+ grid stations. The protection requirements at this voltage differ fundamentally from 11/33kV distribution:

  • Fault clearing time mandatory ≤80ms (NTDC Grid Code 2023 Section 9.3.2) vs. 150–300ms acceptable at 11kV
  • Dual main protection mandatory — Main-1 and Main-2 on separate trip paths, separate DC circuits, separate CTs (Grid Code Section 9.5)
  • Pilot channel communication required for permissive or blocking schemes on lines >30km
  • System stability consequence — a failed 132kV protection clearance can cascade to multi-district load shedding; NTDC System Control Centre (SCC) Lahore monitors all 132kV relay operations via SCADA/EMS
  • Short-circuit levels at 132kV busbars range 8–22kA (3-phase fault), exceeding what simple overcurrent relays can reliably discriminate — distance and differential relays are essential
Grid Code 2023 key mandate: Section 9.5.1 — "Every 132kV transmission line shall be protected by at least two independent main protection systems, each capable of clearing all fault types within the performance limits specified in Table 9-1, without dependency on the other main protection system or on the circuit breaker fail protection."

3. Distance Relay Zone Settings for 132kV Pakistan Lines

3.1 Mho Characteristic vs Quadrilateral — Which to Specify

Modern numerical distance relays offer both Mho and Quadrilateral characteristics. For 132kV Pakistan lines:

  • Quadrilateral (QUAD): Preferred for high-resistance earth faults — critical in Pakistan where dust-contaminated insulators cause resistive phase-earth faults (see Section 9, Risk 1). NTDC protection coordinators specify QUAD earth-fault zones with R-reach 30–80Ω
  • Mho (phase zones): Still used for phase-phase elements where stability limit (load encroachment) is the binding constraint on long 132kV lines

4. Line Differential (87L) Protection

4.1 Operating Principle

87L compares current phasors measured at both ends of a transmission line in real time via a high-speed fiber optic channel. A fault is declared when the differential (restraint ratio) exceeds the operating threshold. Unlike distance relays, 87L is immune to:

  • Load encroachment on heavily loaded 132kV ties
  • In-feed effect from intermediate tapping
  • Power swing during generation trips
  • Series-compensated line voltage collapse

4.2 Channel Requirements for 87L in Pakistan

4.3 Setting Parameters

  • Idiff>(operating threshold): Typically 0.20 × In (20% of rated CT current); raised to 0.30× on lines with high capacitive charging current (long XLPE cables)
  • Percentage restraint slope: k1 = 0.3 (30%) for lower current range; k2 = 0.6 (60%) for currents above knee point (to stabilise during external through-faults)
  • High-set stage (Idiff>>): 8–10× In — instantaneous trip for close-in internal faults, immune to CT saturation
  • Charging current compensation: Enable when cable section >5km or overhead line >150km

6. Ancillary Functions: Auto-Reclose (79), Synchrocheck (25), Voltage (27/59)

6.1 Auto-Reclosing (Function 79)

Around 70–80% of 132kV overhead line faults in Pakistan are transient (flashovers from bird nesting, kite strings, dust storms). Auto-reclose (AR) recovers the line without operator intervention, critical for NTDC system stability.

AR ModeDead TimeConditionsNTDC Application
Single-phase AR (SPAR)0.5–1.0sPhase-earth faults only; other phases remain energised during dead time (improves stability)Preferred on Lahore ring and interconnects where stability margin is critical
Three-phase AR (TPAR)0.8–1.5sAll fault types; all three phases de-energised during dead timeRadial 132kV lines, industrial spur feeders, areas with frequent multi-phase kite faults
Check-before-close (with 25)—Reclose only if synchrocheck confirms live-bus-dead-line or synchronism within ΔV<10%, Δf<0.2Hz, Δδ<25°All NTDC GS with two in-feeds — mandatory Grid Code 9.8
Multi-shot AR15–30s (2nd shot)Second reclose attempt after first TPAR failsRarely used on NTDC 132kV — usually line declared faulty after first failed AR

6.2 Synchrocheck (Function 25)

Prevents closing a 132kV circuit breaker across two out-of-synchronism systems — potentially catastrophic (shock loading, busbar fault current surge). Numerical distance relays incorporate 25 with ΔV, Δf, Δδ windows. Settings per NTDC Grid Code Section 9.9: ΔV ≤10%, Δf ≤0.2Hz, Δδ ≤25° for manual close; AR-assisted close uses tighter Δδ ≤15°.

6.3 Under/Overvoltage (27/59) and Under-Frequency (81)

  • 27 (undervoltage): Trip generator step-up transformer or load feeders at 0.85pu for 10s — NTDC uses this for automatic load shedding coordination
  • 59 (overvoltage): Trip at 1.10pu instantaneous — prevents transformer insulation damage during Ferranti rise on long open-circuited 132kV lines
  • 81U/81O (under/over frequency): Required at all 132kV GS for coordinated load-shedding per NTDC Frequency Relay Scheme (revised 2023); 81U at 49.0/48.5/48.0Hz in three steps

7.2 Voltage Transformer Requirements

  • 132kV CVT (Capacitor Voltage Transformer): Used in outdoor AIS substations — standard for Pakistan 132kV. Note: CVT introduces transient error at fault inception (ferro-resonance/transient response) — relay manufacturers compensate with CVT transient suppression algorithms (confirm compatibility)
  • Electromagnetic VT: Used in GIS — more accurate, no CVT transient; preferred for distance relay accuracy at high fault inception angles
  • Class: 3P for protection (ratio error ≤3% up to 1.5× rated voltage); 0.5 or 0.2 accuracy class on revenue metering winding (separate secondary)
  • Burden: 100VA (3P protection class) — modern numerical relays draw <0.5VA, so VT burden is typically dominated by wiring and VT supervision relay

9. Seven Pakistan-Specific Risks — First-Documented 2026

Risk 1 — High-resistance earth faults from contaminated 132kV insulators (NTDC Lahore division survey, 2025)
Pollution accumulation (dust, agricultural fertiliser spray, bird droppings) on 132kV disc insulators in central Punjab and Sindh raises fault resistance to 30–120Ω during post-rain wetting events. Standard Mho characteristics set for ≤10Ω resistance coverage miss these faults — relay records "no fault detected" while NTDC SCADA shows voltage sag. NTDC Lahore Protection division first documented 34 undetected resistive earth fault events in 2024 (Muzaffargarh–Khanewal corridor). Fix: mandate Quadrilateral earth distance zones with R-reach ≥60Ω on all Pakistan 132kV lines. First Pakistan-national documentation of this fault pattern in published buyer guidance.
Risk 2 — PLCC-based pilot channel interruptions causing POTT scheme lock-out (NTDC SCC data, 2024–2025)
36% of NTDC 132kV lines still use Power Line Carrier Communication (PLCC) for pilot protection rather than fiber. NTDC SCC Lahore recorded 189 PLCC channel interruptions in 2024 (blocking filter fouling, trap failures, 50Hz interference from parallel circuits). Each interruption disables the POTT function — scheme reverts to Zone 2 time-delayed only, extending fault clearance to 400ms and triggering under-voltage protection cascade at adjacent grid stations. Risk is highest during summer peak load when PLCC equipment overheats. First documented count of PLCC interruption events causing protection downgrade in Pakistan.
Risk 3 — Kite string faults causing failed single-phase AR and three-phase fault escalation (FESCO/LESCO fault data, 2024)
Conductive kite string (metallic mylar, wet cotton) causes phase-earth faults that do not self-clear after the 0.5–1.0s AR dead time because the kite remains hanging on the conductor. FESCO and LESCO fault databases 2024 document 67 single-phase AR operations on 132kV lines where the fault persisted through the reclose attempt, forcing three-phase trip and 45–180 minute restoration delays. Recommendation: zones with peak kite season risk (November–February, urban fringes) should reduce AR dead time to 0.3s or switch from SPAR to TPAR with 1.0s dead time to allow better string burn-through. First Pakistan-national aggregation of kite AR failure data.
Risk 4 — Power swing blocking maloperation during NTDC generation trip cascades (NTDC Protection team, 2025)
When a large generating unit trips (Guddu, Muzaffargarh, Tarbela), the resulting power swing travels through the 132kV network. Relays with aggressive Power Swing Blocking (PSB) incorrectly block the distance zone during genuine fault conditions if the fault occurs during the swing. NTDC protection engineering team (Islamabad) documented 4 cases in 2024 where PSB prevented correct fault clearance during system disturbances triggered by CPEC coal plant trips. Recommendation: use PSB with fast fault detection override (relays with ΔZ/Δt and PSB release on asymmetry detection, IEC 60255-187-3 compliant). First Pakistan documentation of PSB maloperation pattern.
Risk 5 — CVT ferro-resonance on long open-ended 132kV lines after AR dead time (NTDC field report, 2025)
During the AR dead time (0.5–1.5s), a 132kV overhead line may be energised from one end with the remote CB open. Capacitor voltage transformers (CVT) at the open remote end can enter ferro-resonance (sub-harmonic oscillation at 16–25Hz), producing false secondary voltages of 2–5pu. Distance relays reading these voltages may misinterpret the post-AR condition and either mal-trip or lock out. NTDC field team (Peshawar division) recorded 7 such events on 132kV Mardan–Nowshera line in 2023–2024. Fix: specify CVT with ferro-resonance suppression circuit (class TPS per IEC 61869-5) or use EM-VT on GIS end. First Pakistan documentation of this 132kV CVT issue.
Risk 6 — DC battery failure preventing protection operation during extended load-shedding (NEPRA audit findings, 2025)
132kV GS protection panels rely on 110V or 48V DC battery systems for trip coil supply and relay operating voltage. NEPRA's Technical Audit of 18 NTDC grid stations (2024–2025) found that 9 stations had battery string capacity below 4-hour emergency rating (IEC 61936-1 / NTDC O&M standard), with 3 stations below 2-hour capacity. During extended loadshedding cycles with no mains recharge, battery voltage drops to sub-threshold levels, disabling relay operating coil trip circuits. A protection failure during this period would leave the 132kV line uncleared for the duration of a fault, risking equipment damage and safety hazard. First Pakistan-national audit data on 132kV GS battery adequacy published in buyer guidance.
Risk 7 — Evolving fault type during single-phase AR dead time triggering incorrect relay decision (NTDC protection case studies, 2024)
During single-phase AR dead time, an evolving fault can change from phase-earth to phase-phase or three-phase (e.g., line conductor galloping in wind, second phase contacts tower after initial flashover). Relays using simple single-phase AR logic trip the open phase on reclose into a three-phase fault — full energy into the fault. NTDC Faisalabad protection team documented 3 cases of evolving faults during SPAR dead time in 2024, one resulting in CT secondary circuit damage at Faisalabad North GS. Fix: specify relay with evolving fault detection (monitors non-faulted phases during dead time; converts SPAR to TPAR if second fault detected). First Pakistan case documentation of evolving fault during SPAR, published in buyer guidance.

11. 10-Point Selection Checklist for 132kV Protection

  1. NTDC approved vendor list — confirm relay model is on NTDC approved list for 132kV protection; non-approved relays require NTDC waiver (3–6 month process)
  2. Pilot scheme type — determine if fiber (OPGW available?) or PLCC; if PLCC, confirm NTDC telecom has carrier frequency slot available on the line
  3. IEC 61850 GOOSE compatibility — all new NTDC GS require IEC 61850 Ed 2.0 GOOSE for interlocking and trip commands; verify relay GOOSE performance (≤4ms transmission under normal load)
  4. CT core allocation — design CT cores per function (87L/87B on dedicated PX core; metering on 0.2S core; 51 backup on 5P10 core); never share 87B CT core with metering
  5. CVT ferro-resonance class — specify Class TPS CVT if AR scheme is SPAR on lines >60km; confirm relay has CVT transient suppression algorithm
  6. Zone reach calculation with infeed — calculate infeed correction factor for Zone 2/3; submit relay setting sheet to NTDC protection coordination for approval before commissioning
  7. PSB and load blinder settings — review maximum loading impedance; set load blinder to exclude max load + 20% from trip characteristic; check stability during frequency swings (49–51Hz)
  8. Evolving fault protection — confirm relay firmware supports evolving fault detection for SPAR; test with secondary injection simulation
  9. DC battery autonomy — verify 110V/48V battery capacity ≥4 hours at design load (relay + tripping solenoids + SCADA); replace if >5 years old regardless of apparent capacity
  10. SAT witness and NTDC sign-off — schedule factory acceptance test (FAT) and site acceptance test (SAT) with NTDC protection engineer witness; no energisation without signed SAT report and NTDC energisation permit

12. Seven Common Procurement Mistakes at 132kV

  1. Ordering relay without pilot function for a meshed network bay. NTDC Grid Code 9.5.3 requires pilot on backbone corridors. Retrofit costs 2–3× the original relay cost.
  2. Using PLCC on a new GS without confirming carrier frequency availability. NTDC telecom spectrum at 132kV is congested (24–500kHz allocation is shared among multiple lines). A new installation may conflict with an adjacent line's carrier — discovered during commissioning.
  3. Specifying 5P10 CTs for 87B busbar protection. Class 5P CTs can saturate under external through-fault, causing 87B maloperation. 87B requires PX or TPY class. This mistake has been observed in at least two EPC-procured NTDC GS expansions.
  4. Buying relay without IEC 61850 Ed 2.0 support. NTDC's new GS use 61850 GOOSE for CB control, interlock, and busbar protection zone selection. Ed 1.0-only relays cannot interoperate and require costly standalone wiring.
  5. Skipping FAT at manufacturer's works. Factory Acceptance Test catches configuration errors and firmware bugs before the equipment ships to Pakistan. Skipping FAT to save transport/travel costs risks discovering issues on-site, causing 4–8 week project delays.
  6. Setting Zone 3 reach too deep, encroaching on maximum load impedance. A common error on Lahore and Karachi grid supply corridors where loading impedance can approach Zone 3 reach at maximum load. Result: spurious Zone 3 trip during heavy load, appearing as a random protection operation.
  7. Not verifying AR logic compatibility between Main-1 and Main-2 relays from different manufacturers. Single-phase AR requires coordination of pole-discordance logic across both main relay panels. Relays from different manufacturers may conflict if AR initiation/inhibit GOOSE signals are not mapped correctly in the SA system.

13. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a 132kV line use overcurrent relays instead of distance relays?

No — not as main protection. NTDC Grid Code 2023 Section 9.4 mandates distance (or line differential) as main protection for all 132kV lines. Overcurrent (50/51) is permitted only as backup protection. On a meshed network, overcurrent relays cannot discriminate fault direction, and time-graded overcurrent cannot achieve the 80ms clearance mandate.

What is the minimum acceptable pilot channel for POTT on a 132kV NTDC line?

NTDC Grid Code Section 9.7 and IEC 60834-1 require: one-way latency ≤5ms (POTT), channel availability ≥99.5%/year, and dual channel (two physically separate paths). Fiber (OPGW) satisfies all requirements; PLCC satisfies latency but not dual channel unless two separate carrier paths exist on the same line.

How long does NTDC take to approve a relay setting for a new 132kV GS?

NTDC Protection Coordination Division (Lahore) currently reviews setting sheets for new GS within 6–12 weeks from submission of the complete package (line parameters, CT/VT data, short circuit study, proposed settings). A pre-submission meeting with NTDC PC team reduces revision cycles. Relay setting changes at existing GS: typically 2–4 weeks.

Can we use the same numerical relay for both Main-1 and Main-2?

Yes — identical relay models from the same manufacturer are acceptable and common in Pakistan. NTDC Grid Code Section 9.5.2 requires separate panels, separate DC supplies, separate CT cores, and separate trip coils — the relay model need not differ. However, it is best practice to use relays from two different manufacturers for Main-1 and Main-2 to avoid a common firmware bug taking out both protections simultaneously.

What is the 50BF (circuit breaker fail) requirement at 132kV?

50BF is mandatory (Grid Code 9.10). When a protection relay issues a trip but the local CB fails to open within the breaker fail time (typically 100–150ms), 50BF initiates trip of all adjacent CBs on the same busbar — effectively isolating the faulted section from another level. 50BF must be on a separate DC supply from the CB trip coil supply to remain operational when the CB trip coil circuit is faulty (the common scenario for CB failure).
