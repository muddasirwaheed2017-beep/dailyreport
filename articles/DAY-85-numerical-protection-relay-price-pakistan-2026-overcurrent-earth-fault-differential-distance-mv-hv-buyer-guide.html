1. What Is a Numerical Protection Relay?

A numerical protection relay (also called a digital protection relay or microprocessor-based relay) is an electronic device that continuously monitors current, voltage, power flow, and frequency on a medium-voltage (MV) or high-voltage (HV) electrical system. When a fault or abnormal condition exceeds the relay's set thresholds, it issues a trip signal to the associated circuit breaker — typically a 11kV Vacuum Circuit Breaker (VCB) — to isolate the fault within milliseconds, protecting equipment worth crores of rupees and preventing hazards to personnel.

Unlike older electromechanical relays (IDMT disc relays, attracted armature relays) and static analog relays, numerical relays offer:

  • Multi-function in one chassis — one relay replaces 3–5 separate electromechanical relays (overcurrent + earth fault + auto-reclose + measurement + event log)
  • Self-monitoring — continuous hardware self-test detects internal relay failure and flags a watchdog alarm
  • Event logging & fault records — stores last 100–1000 fault events with timestamp, fault current magnitude, and pre-fault waveform (disturbance recorder)
  • Easy setting — settings via front-panel keypad or PC software (USB/RS485), not physical plug-and-socket links
  • Communication — Modbus RTU, IEC 61850 GOOSE, DNP3 for SCADA integration
  • Accurate measurement — metering accuracy ±1% (replaces separate meters)
🚨 Pakistan-Specific Warning: In Pakistan, protection relay selection is subject to DISCO (LESCO, K-Electric, FESCO, IESCO) Type Approval. Always confirm your relay model is on the relevant DISCO's Approved Vendor List (AVL) before purchase. Installing a non-AVL relay will result in DISCO rejection during pre-energization inspection and the relay must be replaced at full cost.

3. Types of Numerical Protection Relays

3.1 Overcurrent & Earth Fault Relay (OC/EF) — ANSI 50/51/51N

The most common relay in Pakistan's 11kV systems. Provides IDMT overcurrent and instantaneous overcurrent plus earth fault protection. Usually includes 3× phase CT inputs and 1× residual earth CT input (or calculated 3I₀). Used for all outgoing 11kV feeders supplying industrial consumers, distribution transformers, and cable feeders.

3.2 Multi-Function Feeder Protection Relay (50/51/51N/67/79/46/27/59)

Combines overcurrent, earth fault, directional, auto-reclose, voltage, and frequency protection in one chassis. These relays are now the Pakistani DISCO standard for new 11kV feeder installations — they eliminate the need for separate auto-reclose relays, directional relays, and voltage relays. Recommended for any new 11kV switchgear installation (consumer substations, bulk supply points, ring main units).

3.3 Transformer Differential Relay (87T)

Provides unit protection for power transformers by comparing current entering and leaving the transformer. Trips only for internal faults (inter-turn, inter-winding, earth) while remaining stable for external faults and magnetising inrush. Required by all Pakistani DISCOs for transformers ≥1 MVA. Must be configured with correct CT ratio compensation and vector group matching (e.g. Dyn11 = 30° phase shift compensation).

3.4 Busbar Differential Relay (87B)

Protects 11kV/33kV busbars by comparing the algebraic sum of all feeder currents (which should be zero in steady state). Used in main substations, switching stations, and large industrial plants with multiple 11kV feeders. Requires extremely fast operation (<20ms) to prevent busbar fault from propagating.

3.5 Distance Relay (21)

Measures the impedance of the protected line and trips when the measured impedance falls within a defined zone (Z1 = 80% of line length, Z2 = 120%, Z3 = 150%). Used on 11kV/33kV overhead transmission and sub-transmission lines. Less common in Pakistan for 11kV consumer substations; mainly used by WAPDA/DISCOs for their own network protection.

3.6 Motor Feeder Protection Relay (50/51/51N/46/49/66)

Specialist relay for high-voltage motor feeders (3.3kV, 6.6kV, 11kV motors). Adds thermal overload (49), start inhibit (66 starts-per-hour), and negative sequence (46) to the standard overcurrent functions. Note: this is distinct from the LT motor protection relay used for 415V motors.

5. IDMT Curves — IEC 60255-151 Pakistan DISCO Standard

IDMT (Inverse Definite Minimum Time) is the operating characteristic where relay operating time decreases as fault current increases. IEC 60255-151 defines the standard curve families used in Pakistani DISCO protection coordination studies:

Curve TypeIEC 60255-151 Namek (constant)α (exponent)Pakistan DISCO Use
SIStandard Inverse0.140.02Most common — LESCO, FESCO, IESCO 11kV standard
VIVery Inverse13.51.0Cable feeders, K-Electric preferred for close-in discrimination
EIExtremely Inverse80.02.0Fuse coordination, short cable runs
LILong-Time Inverse1201.0Motor starting coordination, not typical in DISCO specs
DTDefinite Time——Grading with fixed-time relays, busbar protection backup

The operating time formula (IEC 60255-151):
t = TMS × [k / ((I/Is)^α − 1)]
where: t = operating time (seconds), TMS = Time Multiplier Setting, I = measured fault current, Is = pick-up current (PS% × CT primary), k, α = curve constants from table above.

💡 Pakistan DISCO Coordination Rule: A grading margin of minimum 0.4 seconds between consecutive relay operating times (upstream minus downstream) is the standard adopted by LESCO and most Pakistani DISCOs, accounting for: relay overshoot time (40–100ms), breaker operating time (50–80ms), CT error (up to 10%), relay timing error (IEC class E up to 5%), and safety margin (150–200ms).

7.2 Voltage Transformer (VT) Requirements

For relays with voltage inputs (27, 59, 67, 21, 81):

  • Secondary voltage: 110V AC (line-to-line) standard in Pakistan (57.7V line-to-neutral)
  • Accuracy class: 3P (protection class, IEC 61869-3)
  • Fusing: HV primary fuse on each VT plus LV secondary MCB on each phase — if VT fuse blows, relay sees "loss of VT" (ANSI 60) and should block directional/voltage functions
  • Earthing: VT star-point earthed at one point only (secondary neutral earth) — double earthing causes circulating current and CT saturation-like effects

11. Six Expensive Mistakes Pakistani Installers Make with Protection Relays

  1. Buying a relay not on the DISCO AVL.
    Checking the AVL takes 10 minutes. Not checking it costs the full relay price + project delay. Every Pakistani DISCO maintains a current AVL — obtain it from their P&C section before purchasing. Some DISCOs update their AVL annually.
  2. Using a measuring-class CT (0.5, 1, 3) instead of protection-class (5P, PS).
    Measuring-class CTs saturate at 1.5–3× rated current. Under a fault, the CT output is distorted and the relay either fails to operate in time or misoperates. Protection-class CTs must not saturate up to rated accuracy limit factor (ALF) × rated current.
  3. Commissioning without DISCO settings approval.
    Installing arbitrary relay settings is a DISCO violation in Pakistan. The DISCO protection engineer must approve the Plug Setting (PS%), Time Multiplier Setting (TMS), and Instantaneous Setting (IS) before energisation. Non-approved settings = DISCO can require de-energisation and protection redesign at the consumer's cost.
  4. No battery autonomy calculation for DC auxiliary supply.
    Sizing the battery based on "what fits in the panel" rather than calculating relay burden + VCB trip coil power × required standby hours. An undersized battery fails to trip the VCB during a prolonged WAPDA/DISCO outage + subsequent fault — the most dangerous scenario.
  5. Incorrect vector group compensation for 87T differential relay.
    A transformer with Dyn11 vector group introduces a 30° phase shift between HV and LV currents. The relay must compensate for this in software (or historically via external CTs with delta/star winding). Incorrect setting causes the relay to misoperate on transformer energisation inrush or fail to operate on a real internal fault.
  6. Open-circuiting a live CT secondary during maintenance.
    Removing a relay from a live panel without first short-circuiting the CT secondary causes the CT to develop kilovolt-level voltage on the secondary — lethal to personnel, destructive to CT insulation. Always use CT secondary shorting plugs before disconnecting any relay. Never assume the panel is de-energised: verify with a live-line indicator on the 11kV side.

FAQ — Numerical Protection Relay Pakistan 2026

Q: What is the price of a numerical protection relay in Pakistan 2026?

Numerical protection relay prices in Pakistan (2026) range from PKR 25,000–55,000 for a basic single-function overcurrent/earth fault relay (ANSI 51/51N), PKR 55,000–1,80,000 for a multi-function feeder protection relay (50/51/51N/67/79/46), PKR 1,20,000–2,80,000 for a transformer differential relay (87T), and PKR 1,80,000–4,50,000 for a distance relay (21). All prices include 18% GST and are based on September 2026 import rates.

Q: What ANSI relay function codes do I need for an 11kV feeder panel in Pakistan?

For a standard 11kV outgoing feeder VCB panel in Pakistan, minimum relay functions are: ANSI 51 (IDMT overcurrent), 51N (IDMT earth fault), 50 (instantaneous OC), 50N (instantaneous EF). DISCO specs also require 79 (auto-reclose) for overhead-line feeders. For transformer/incomer feeders, add 87T (differential). Confirm exact requirements from your DISCO's P&C department.

Q: What is IDMT and why does it matter for Pakistani DISCOs?

IDMT (Inverse Definite Minimum Time) means relay operating time decreases as fault current increases, ensuring downstream fuses/breakers clear before upstream ones — called discrimination or selectivity. Pakistani DISCOs use IEC 60255-151 Standard Inverse (SI) or Very Inverse (VI) curves and issue Protection Relay Settings Letters specifying Plug Setting, TMS, and IS. Never commission without DISCO settings approval.

Q: Can I use a relay without a battery backup (auxiliary DC supply) for an 11kV panel?

No. An 11kV panel numerical relay requires a dedicated auxiliary DC supply (110V DC or 48V DC from VRLA battery with charger). During a fault, the 11kV AC supply may collapse — the relay must still trip the VCB using stored DC energy. Using AC auxiliary alone fails this fundamental safety requirement.

Q: What CT ratio should I use with a protection relay for an 11kV feeder?

Calculate full-load current: I = kVA / (√3 × 11kV). Select CT primary ratio to the next standard size above FLC. Use protection class CT (5P20 or PS, IEC 61869-2) — not measuring class. CT secondary must match relay input (1A or 5A). ALF ≥ 10 required for overcurrent relays. CT VA rating must exceed relay burden + lead burden to avoid saturation.

Q: What is the difference between a protection relay and a motor protection relay?

An MV protection relay (this guide) is for 11kV/33kV circuit breakers protecting MV feeders, transformers, and busbars — IEC 60255 compliant, high CT input levels, complex coordination requirements. A motor protection relay is for 415V/690V LT motors — IEC 60947-4-1, handles thermal overload, phase failure, thermistor input. Do not interchange.

Q: Do Pakistani DISCOs require IEC 61850 communication for 11kV protection relays?

As of 2026, IEC 61850 is mandatory for new grid substation protection relays (132kV+) but optional for most 11kV consumer substations. LESCO and K-Electric are progressively requiring IEC 61850 GOOSE for new 11kV feeder relays in their own network. For industrial 11kV consumer substations, Modbus RTU/RS485 is still accepted — verify with your DISCO P&C department during the protection approval stage.

Q: What commissioning tests are required for a protection relay before 11kV energization?

Mandatory commissioning tests: (1) Relay injection test — IDMT curve, pick-up current, TMS, instantaneous; (2) CT polarity and continuity test; (3) Trip circuit continuity — relay contact to VCB trip coil; (4) VCB trip test — <80ms; (5) Auto-reclose functional test (if fitted); (6) DC battery voltage and autonomy verification; (7) Event log review. A Protection Commissioning Test Report (PCTR) signed by installer and DISCO P&C engineer is required before energization.
