9. Performance Ratio (PR) — Calculation and Pakistani Benchmarks

Performance Ratio = (Actual kWh output) ÷ (Irradiance in kWh/m² × System kWp × 1 m²/kWp)

PR strips out weather effects to give a system health score. A new, well-installed system should achieve PR 0.78–0.84 in Pakistan's climate.

11. Installation Wiring for Monitoring Equipment

Five installation rules that prevent monitoring hardware failures in Pakistan's field conditions:

  1. RS485 cable — use twisted pair, shielded (STP Cat5e or dedicated Belden 9842): Unshielded RS485 in the same tray as AC power cables causes Modbus CRC errors. Run RS485 in a separate cable tray or at minimum 300 mm separation from power cables.
  2. Termination resistors at both ends of RS485 bus: 120Ω resistor at the master (logger) and at the last slave (furthest inverter). Missing termination is the single most common cause of RS485 dropouts in multi-inverter plants — accounts for 61% of communication faults (field survey, 50 C&I sites, 2024).
  3. Logger enclosure IP54 minimum: Dust ingress destroys SIM card readers and SD card slots within 18 months in open-panel configurations. Use an IP65 enclosure for roof-mounted loggers.
  4. Logger power supply on UPS circuit: Logger must stay powered during load shedding to maintain data log continuity. A 12V 7Ah battery with solar trickle charge sustains a 4G logger for 48 hrs during prolonged outage — cost under PKR 3,500.
  5. Earthing the logger chassis: Logger RS485 ground must share the common DC earth reference with the inverters. Floating logger chassis causes common-mode noise that corrupts Modbus frames — particularly on long RS485 runs in rural Pakistan where earth quality is variable. See our Earthing System Design Guide.

14. FAQs

Q1: Does my WiFi inverter app satisfy NEPRA net-metering requirements?

For systems below 10 kW: yes — the DISCO bi-directional meter is the official record. Your app export log is supplementary. For 10–50 kW commercial: NEPRA recommends independent logging but does not mandate it yet — retain your inverter CSV nonetheless. For 50 kW+: an IEC 61724-compliant datalogger is mandatory under TSDG 2023.

Q2: Which 4G SIM is best for a solar datalogger in rural Punjab / KPK?

Jazz (Warid merged) has the widest 4G rural coverage as of 2024, particularly in central Punjab and southern KPK. Telenor is strong in urban corridors. Zong covers northern areas (AJK, GB) better than others. Buy a SIM from each carrier and test signal strength at the site before committing to a logger with a fixed SIM slot — some loggers accept any standard Nano/Micro SIM.

Q3: Can I monitor a Growatt inverter on FusionSolar?

No — FusionSolar only accepts Huawei inverters. For multi-brand monitoring on a single platform, use SolarmanBusiness (supports 50+ brands via Modbus RTU/TCP) or openWB on a local Raspberry Pi if you need brand-agnostic monitoring.

Q4: My datalogger shows string 2 at 85% of string 1 current. Is this a fault?

A persistent >10% imbalance between strings with identical panel count, orientation, and tilt is an actionable fault. First check: (a) Is string 2 receiving any shading between 10:00–14:00? (b) Are all fuses in the string combiner intact? (c) Are all MC4 connectors fully latched? A 15% imbalance corresponds to roughly 1 degraded panel (bypass diode open) or one partially corroded connector. IV-curve trace the string to locate the fault cell or junction.

Q5: How do I prove my solar system's yield if the DISCO meter reading is wrong?

Your independent datalogger CSV log (timestamped, 15-min intervals) is your legal evidence. File a formal meter test request with your DISCO — under NEPRA Consumer Protection Rules 2023, the DISCO must test the meter within 15 working days of a written complaint. Retain your datalogger logs for the full 5-year net-metering contract period. Without your own log, reconciliation disputes default in DISCO's favour.
