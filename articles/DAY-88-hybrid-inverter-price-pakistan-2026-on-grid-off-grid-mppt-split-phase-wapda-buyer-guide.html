1. On-Grid vs Off-Grid vs Hybrid — Real Differences

These three inverter topologies solve fundamentally different problems. Choosing the wrong one costs PKR 50,000–300,000 in wasted capacity or missed savings.

1.1 On-Grid (Grid-Tied) Inverter

An on-grid inverter converts DC power from solar panels to AC power synchronised with the WAPDA grid. It has no battery interface and produces zero output during a grid outage — by law, to protect WAPDA linemen from back-feed during maintenance.

  • Best for: Homes with reliable grid supply (less than 4 hours loadshedding/day) wanting to reduce bills via NEPRA net metering
  • Cannot do: Backup power during outages; battery storage
  • WAPDA anti-islanding requirement: Must disconnect within 2 seconds of grid loss (IEEE 1547 / IEC 62116 compliance mandatory)
  • Grid compliance certificate: Required from NEPRA for net metering application
  • Pakistan grid match: Single-phase 220V/50Hz for residential; three-phase 415V/50Hz for commercial >5kW

1.2 Off-Grid Inverter

An off-grid inverter operates entirely independent of the WAPDA grid. It charges batteries from solar panels (via MPPT or PWM charge controller) and inverts battery DC to AC for loads. Grid connection is absent or disconnected by design.

  • Best for: Remote sites with no grid (tubewells, agricultural pumps, rural clinics); extreme loadshedding areas (>16 hours/day)
  • Cannot do: Export excess solar to grid; synchronise with WAPDA for net metering credit
  • Battery dependency: System dies when batteries are depleted if solar is insufficient
  • Pakistan application: Widely used for agricultural pump control — see our Tubewell Pump Control Panel Guide for panel integration

1.3 Hybrid Inverter (Multi-Mode)

A hybrid inverter combines all three capabilities: MPPT solar charging, battery interface, and bi-directional grid connection. It is the most complex and most capable topology.

Operating modes (in priority order for most configurations):

  1. Solar priority: PV feeds loads first → surplus charges battery → remaining surplus exports to grid (if net metering active)
  2. Battery priority: Battery discharges to feed loads when PV insufficient, before drawing from grid
  3. Grid backup: Grid supplements when both PV and battery are insufficient
  4. Grid-charge mode: Grid charges battery during off-peak tariff hours (configurable — important for UR-1 TOU tariff strategy)

Pakistan-specific hybrid advantage: With UR-1 peak tariff at PKR 68–72/kWh (18:00–22:00) and off-peak at PKR 18–22/kWh (22:00–06:00), a correctly programmed hybrid can charge batteries from cheap off-peak grid power and discharge during the expensive peak window — a pure arbitrage strategy independent of solar generation.

2. MPPT Specifications That Matter in Pakistan

Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) efficiency headlines are mostly marketing. The specifications that actually affect Pakistan performance are different.

2.1 PV Input Voltage Range

MPPT trackers have a minimum start voltage (Vstart) and a maximum operating range (Vmpp range). Pakistan's summer morning temperatures of 25–40°C affect panel open-circuit voltage (Voc) and Vmpp significantly.

PV String Voltage Behaviour in Pakistan Climate

Pakistan design rule: Size string voltage so winter Voc × string count ≤ 95% of inverter Max PV Voltage. Size Vmpp at 60°C cell temperature ≥ inverter MPPT minimum × 1.10 buffer.

2.2 Number of MPPT Trackers

Rooftops in Pakistan are rarely one unshaded plane. A home in DHA Lahore may have panels on the main roof (south-facing) and a boundary wall (east-facing). Two MPPT trackers allow each string to be optimised independently — critical for avoiding the "one shaded panel drags the whole string" problem.

Practical rule: If panels face more than one direction or experience different shading profiles, specify a minimum of 2 independent MPPT trackers.

2.3 MPPT Efficiency vs Euro Efficiency

Manufacturers quote peak MPPT efficiency (e.g. 99.9%) which occurs only at full load under ideal irradiance. Euro Efficiency is the weighted average across real partial-load conditions and is the correct comparison metric for Pakistan's variable irradiance profile.

Efficiency Metrics Comparison

Peak MPPT EfficiencyFull irradiance, full loadLow — occurs <15% of operating hours
Euro EfficiencyWeighted 5%–100% load profileHigh — representative of annual yield
CEC EfficiencyUS climate weightingMedium — hotter bias, closer to Karachi coastal conditions

Minimum acceptable Euro Efficiency for Pakistan: ≥96.5% for string inverters ≤10kW; ≥97.0% for central inverters >10kW.

2.4 MPPT Start-Up Irradiance

Inverters that start tracking at lower irradiance levels (e.g. 50 W/m² vs 150 W/m²) generate more energy during morning ramp-up and evening ramp-down. In Karachi (high irradiance, low cloud cover) the difference is modest. In Lahore or Islamabad during monsoon (heavy cloud, reduced irradiance hours), an inverter with 50 W/m² start-up can deliver 8–12% more annual yield compared to a 150 W/m² threshold inverter.

3. WAPDA 220V/50Hz Compliance — Non-Negotiable Rules

Pakistan's grid operates at nominal 220V AC / 50Hz single-phase (and 415V / 50Hz three-phase). However, actual grid voltage in most DISCOs fluctuates between 170V and 260V — a ±23% swing far exceeding IEC 60038 standard tolerances. Your inverter must handle this without tripping unnecessarily or over-charging batteries.

3.1 Grid Voltage Window Settings

Most hybrid inverters allow configuration of grid voltage acceptance windows. For Pakistan:

Critical: An inverter with a fixed 196V–253V window will trip 3–8 times daily on many Pakistani feeders. Always verify the inverter allows field-adjustable voltage windows before purchase.

3.2 Frequency Synchronisation & Anti-Islanding

For NEPRA net metering compliance, the inverter must include certified anti-islanding protection. The most common methods are:

  • Active frequency drift (AFD): Standard, suitable for most single-inverter installations
  • Sandia frequency shift (SFS): Required where multiple inverters are connected on the same feeder — prevents mutual cancellation of AFD signals
  • Passive methods only: Not accepted by NEPRA for net metering applications

3.3 Three-Phase Inverter Balancing

For three-phase hybrid inverters (5kW and above), Pakistan's commercial consumers must verify phase imbalance limits. WAPDA DISCOs limit single-phase contribution from three-phase inverters to no more than 5kW per phase on LT feeders. Oversized single-phase loading causes neutral current problems in WAPDA's 3-wire distribution systems.

See our 3-Phase Energy Meter Guide for metering compliance with three-phase solar hybrid systems.

3.4 Earthing Requirements

NEPRA DG/NEM Policy 2025 mandates a dedicated earthing system for the solar inverter installation, separate from the building's main earth. Minimum requirements: 1× copper-bonded earth rod (1.8m), 25mm² copper earthing conductor, earth resistance <10Ω verified by earth tester.

Inverters with built-in EMI filters (Class B, per IEC 61000-3-2) are required in areas where smart meters are installed.

5. Sizing Methodology — 4 Pakistan Examples

Step 1: Calculate daily consumption (kWh)

Sum all loads × hours/day. Use 30-day WAPDA bill average for actual baseline.

Step 2: Identify backup priority loads

Separate essential (fans, LED lights, router) from heavy (AC, water heater, welder). Only essential loads need battery backup — sizing for heavy loads multiplies battery cost 5–10×.

Step 3: Calculate solar PV array size

Pakistan average peak sun hours: Karachi 5.8 PSH, Lahore 5.0 PSH, Islamabad 4.7 PSH, Quetta 6.2 PSH, Multan 5.5 PSH.

Formula: Array kWp = (Daily kWh ÷ PSH) ÷ System efficiency (0.75–0.80)

Step 4: Select inverter kW rating

Inverter AC output must equal or exceed peak simultaneous load. Add 20% headroom for starting currents (motor loads, compressors).

8. VFD Compatibility & Motor Load Considerations

Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs) are increasingly common in Pakistan for motor speed control — air compressors, pumps, HVAC systems. Running a VFD on a hybrid inverter EPS output requires careful compatibility verification.

8.1 Why VFDs Cause Problems on Inverter EPS Output

Most hybrid inverters produce a modified sine wave or pure sine wave at their EPS output. VFDs are highly sensitive to:

  • Total Harmonic Distortion (THD): EPS output THD must be <3% for VFD operation. Modified sine wave inverters (THD 20–40%) will cause VFD overcurrent faults or damage
  • Inrush current: VFDs draw 3–6× rated current at start-up. Inverter EPS must handle this without voltage sag below ±5% of 220V
  • Ground fault detection: VFDs use RCD ground fault protection internally. Floating output of some inverters causes nuisance RCD trips

Specification requirements for VFD loads on hybrid inverter EPS:

  • Pure sine wave output (not modified sine wave) — THD <3% at full load
  • EPS output current rating ≥ 3× VFD rated input current (for inrush)
  • Inverter must have a neutral-referenced output (not floating neutral)

For VFD selection and sizing for motor loads, see our comprehensive VFD & Variable Frequency Drive Price Guide for Pakistan.

8.2 Air Conditioner Inverter Technology Compatibility

Inverter-technology air conditioners (variable speed compressors) present a different challenge. Their built-in inverter draws variable current depending on compressor speed. Most hybrid inverter EPS outputs handle inverter ACs well, provided:

  • AC start-up inrush is within EPS surge rating (typically 2× rated output for 100ms)
  • AC's built-in EMI filter does not conflict with inverter's frequency control
  • AC does not use active PFC (Power Factor Correction) that fights the inverter's voltage waveform

9. Seven Common Inverter Selection Mistakes in Pakistan

  1. Undersizing for AC loads: A 5kW inverter running two 1.5-ton ACs (each 1.5–2kW) plus a refrigerator, fans, and lighting simultaneously hits 6–7kW peak. Size to measured peak, not average load.
  2. Ignoring grid voltage window adjustability: Buying an inverter with fixed ±10% voltage window on Pakistani feeders that swing ±20% means constant tripping. Verify the window is field-adjustable before purchase.
  3. Buying on-grid only in a loadshedding area: On-grid inverters produce zero output during loadshedding — the exact time most people in Pakistan need power. If your area has >4 hours/day loadshedding, a hybrid with battery is essential.
  4. Specifying battery voltage mismatch: 48V LiFePO4 packs cannot be mixed with inverters designed for 100V or 200V high-voltage battery inputs. Always confirm battery voltage compatibility with the inverter model's specification sheet.
  5. Skipping earthing documentation: NEPRA net metering applications are rejected without a proper earthing test report. Install the earthing system before commissioning and get a certified earth resistance reading.
  6. Not configuring TOU tariff charging strategy: Hybrid inverters shipped from factory default to "solar priority" charging. Without manually configuring "grid charge at off-peak" mode, buyers miss the PKR tariff arbitrage benefit entirely.
  7. Placing inverter in an unventilated room: At 45°C ambient room temperature (common in Pakistani summers), a hybrid inverter at full load runs near its thermal limit. Most inverters derate output by 1–2% per °C above 40°C. An unventilated room can cause 20–40% power derating. Install in a shaded, ventilated location or provide forced cooling.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a hybrid inverter without solar panels initially?

Yes. A hybrid inverter connected to batteries and the grid — without solar panels — functions as an intelligent UPS with load-shedding backup and tariff arbitrage capability. You can add solar panels later when budget permits. The inverter's MPPT trackers will activate automatically when panels are connected.

Can I connect a generator to a hybrid inverter?

Most hybrid inverters accept a generator input on their AC-in terminals. However, generators must produce stable 220V/50Hz ±2% to avoid inverter faults. Cheap "non-inverter generators" in Pakistan often have poor frequency stability — verify generator output quality before connecting. For automatic switchover between generator and WAPDA, an external ATS may be needed depending on inverter model.

What MCB/circuit breaker is required between the inverter and my distribution board?

The inverter AC output should connect through a dedicated MCB or RCBO at the main distribution board. The MCB should be sized at 125% of inverter rated output current. For a 5kW single-phase inverter (output current ~22.7A), use a 32A MCB minimum. See our MCB Buyer Guide for Pakistan and RCBO Guide for circuit protection selection.

Does my hybrid inverter qualify for NEPRA net metering automatically?

No. You must apply to your DISCO (LESCO, HESCO, IESCO, etc.) for a net metering connection agreement. The application requires: your inverter's NEPRA approval certificate, a single-line diagram prepared by a licensed electrical consultant, earthing test report, and payment of metering fees. Processing time varies: 30–90 days in most DISCOs.

What is the warranty standard I should expect?

International standard: 5 years for the inverter unit. Extended warranties of 10 years are available from some manufacturers at additional cost. Pakistan-based service centres and spare parts availability are more critical than warranty duration — verify local support before purchase.

Can I use the hybrid inverter EPS output to run a welder or power tools?

Welding machines (especially arc welders) draw massive inrush currents (5–15× rated) with extremely poor power factor. Standard hybrid inverter EPS outputs cannot support welding. VFD-based welding inverters are lighter on the grid but still require careful matching to inverter surge capacity. Most installer warranties are voided if welding machines are connected to the EPS output without explicit engineering approval.
