V-Belt, Timing Belt & Pulley Drive Price Pakistan 2026 — Z, A, B, C, SPZ, SPA, SPB Section Selection, Tensioning Guide & Buyer Guide

V-belts and timing belts are the most common mechanical power transmission elements in Pakistan's industrial and agricultural sectors — yet premature belt failure is one of the top three maintenance complaints at every Faisalabad textile mill, Karachi SITE factory, and Punjab tubewell installation. The causes are consistent: wrong cross-section selected, over-tensioned or under-tensioned installation, misaligned pulleys, and belt quality that degrades rapidly in Pakistan's 45–50°C summer ambient conditions.

This guide covers V-belt cross-section selection (classical Z, A, B, C and narrow SPZ, SPA, SPB, SPC), PKR 2026 price tables, timing belt sizing for CNC and servo applications, poly-V belt selection for high-speed auxiliary drives, pulley alignment and deflection-force tensioning method, and a Pakistan-specific failure mode guide based on field patterns at industrial sites across Punjab and Sindh.

1. Why V-Belts Fail Early in Pakistan

Pakistan's industrial environment creates specific belt failure accelerators that differ from temperate climates:

  • High ambient temperature: Rubber compound softens above 60°C belt surface temperature. In 45°C Karachi summer ambient, a loaded V-belt running in a poorly ventilated enclosure easily reaches 70–80°C surface temperature — above the threshold of standard rubber compound. Result: cracking from the base cord outward, not from the outer surface as in normal wear.
  • Load-shedding restart shock: Across-the-line DOL restart after a 4-hour power cut imposes 5–7× running torque as a shock load on the belt. A belt sized for steady-state load fails within 200 start cycles under repeated DOL shock. Pakistan textile mills on 12-hour load-shedding schedules have measured 3–4× shorter belt life vs comparable mills on stable power.
  • Abrasive dust: Cement, flour, cotton, and coal dust embed in belt compound, accelerating sidewall wear and reducing grip on sheave groove walls. High-dust environments require enclosed sheave guards and accelerated inspection intervals.
  • Counterfeit and sub-specification belt: Hall Road Lahore and wholesale markets carry belt with incorrect cord material (polyester substituted for aramid), under-filled cross-section (measured 0.5–1 mm undersized), and non-standard compound hardness. These fail in 20–30% of rated life.
  • Wrong tensioning: Over-tensioning is the most common installer error — it accelerates bearing wear, cord fatigue, and sheave groove wear simultaneously. Under-tensioning causes slippage, heat, and glaze failure. Neither defect is visible without measurement.

3. V-Belt Section Selection Procedure — Pakistan Field Method

When a motor and driven machine are already selected and belt drives need sizing:

  1. Design power = Motor kW × Service factor
    Pakistan service factors: Centrifugal pump = 1.0; Positive displacement pump = 1.2; Compressor = 1.2–1.4; Fan/blower = 1.0–1.2; Agitator = 1.1–1.4; Crusher = 1.5–2.0
    Pakistan add-on for DOL restarts ≥4/day: +0.15 to 0.25 (same load-shedding restart factor as shaft coupling sizing guide)
  2. Select section from design power vs small sheave speed chart — most belt manufacturer catalogues include this chart. At 1,450 RPM (4-pole motor common in Pakistan): SPA starts at ~2 kW, SPB at ~5 kW, SPC at ~15 kW design power.
  3. Calculate transmission ratio: N1/N2 = D2/D1. Avoid ratios above 7:1 in a single stage — use a gear reducer for higher ratios.
  4. Select minimum small sheave diameter — using catalogue recommended minimum for section. Running a B-belt on a 75 mm sheave halves belt life vs rated.
  5. Calculate number of belts = Design power / (Power per belt from catalogue × correction factors). Always add 1 spare capacity belt position for Pakistan dusty environments.

7. Timing Belt (Synchronous Belt) — When to Choose Over V-Belt

Timing belts (toothed / synchronous belts) eliminate slip entirely and maintain precise speed ratio — essential for CNC, servo-driven axes, and any application where timing between driver and driven shaft must be maintained. V-belts slip by design (2–3% in normal operation, up to 8% under overload); this makes them unsuitable for:

  • CNC router X/Y/Z axes driven by stepper or servo motors — see stepper motor driver guide for motor sizing
  • Servo-driven packaging machines requiring repeatable position
  • Cam-driven machinery where timing advance/retard must not drift
  • Encoder-coupled drives where slip would create position error

Choose V-belt over timing belt when: shock loads are present (timing belt teeth shear under shock; V-belt slips as torque fuse), cost is primary constraint, alignment is poor (timing belt requires accurate pulley alignment; V-belt is more forgiving), or drive ratio need not be exact.

10. Poly-V Belt (Ribbed Belt / Multi-Rib Belt) — High-Speed Applications

Poly-V belts (also called micro-V, ribbed belt, or PJ/PK/PL section) combine the flexibility of a flat belt with the positive groove engagement of a V-belt. They operate at belt speeds up to 50 m/s vs 25–30 m/s for classical V-belts, making them ideal for:

  • High-speed grinder spindle drives (3,000–6,000 RPM)
  • Alternator drives on industrial diesel gensets
  • Washing machine drum drives (Pakistan domestic market)
  • Air conditioning compressor drives on small vehicles and industrial chillers

Poly-V sections (ISO 9981):

  • PH — 1.6 mm rib pitch, very light duty (household appliances)
  • PJ — 2.34 mm pitch, most common domestic and light industrial
  • PK — 3.56 mm pitch, automotive, industrial machinery
  • PL — 4.70 mm pitch, heavy industrial, compressors
  • PM — 9.40 mm pitch, very heavy duty industrial

PKR 2026 price range: PJ ribbed belts (domestic/appliance): PKR 400–1,200 depending on rib count and length. PK industrial (alternator/compressor): PKR 800–3,500. PL heavy industrial: PKR 2,500–9,000.

11. Pulley Alignment — Pakistan Field Procedure

Pulley misalignment is the second most common cause of early V-belt failure in Pakistan after wrong tensioning. Two types:

  • Angular misalignment: Pulley faces not parallel — belt runs on one flange, causes edge wear and cordline fatigue.
  • Parallel (offset) misalignment: Pulley centrelines not in the same plane — belt twists, causing uneven stress and early delamination.

Field alignment method (no laser tool):

  1. Place a long straightedge (steel rule, spirit level, or 1.5 m pipe) across both pulley faces at 3 contact points.
  2. Both faces must touch the straightedge simultaneously at all 3 points — no gap, no rocking. This confirms both parallel and angular alignment simultaneously for pulleys in the same horizontal plane.
  3. Check shaft parallelism with a vernier caliper: measure centre-to-centre distance at the drive side and slack side. Difference > 1 mm per metre of centre distance indicates angular shaft misalignment.
  4. For vertical drives, use a plumb bob + spirit level on each shaft.

Pakistan field note: Welded motor base plates in Pakistan fabrication shops are rarely flat. A 0.5 mm plate twist creates 1–2 mm pulley offset at the pulley face. Always shim the motor base feet to remove twist before aligning pulleys.

12. V-Belt Tensioning — Deflection-Force Method (Pakistan Field)

Correct belt tension is the single most impactful maintenance parameter. The deflection-force method is the most practical for Pakistan field conditions without a belt tension gauge:

  1. Measure the free span length (t) — the distance between the point where the belt leaves one sheave and the point where it contacts the other, in millimetres.
  2. Deflection target: 16 mm per metre of free span (1.6% deflection) at the midpoint of the free span.
  3. Apply a perpendicular force at the midpoint and measure deflection. The force required to achieve the 1.6% deflection target is the tension reference force.
  4. Reference force by section (new belt, good condition):
    • A section: 25–35 N (2.5–3.5 kg force)
    • B section: 35–55 N (3.5–5.5 kg force)
    • C section: 55–80 N (5.5–8 kg force)
    • SPA: 30–45 N
    • SPB: 45–70 N
  5. Re-check after 4–8 hours of running — new belts bed into sheave grooves and tension drops. Re-tension to the reference force and check again after 24 hours.

Pakistan warning: Many installers judge tension by pressing the belt with a thumb — this is not a reliable method. Thumb pressure corresponds to approximately 50–80 N, which over-tensions A and B section belts. Over-tensioned belts fail in bearing overload and cord fatigue, not in slippage.

14. V-Belt Drive vs VFD — Pakistan Energy Efficiency Decision

For variable-speed applications (fans, pumps, compressors), the comparison is:

  • Fixed speed V-belt drive with throttle/damper: Motor runs at full speed, output is mechanically throttled. Efficiency: poor at partial load (60–70% of design).
  • V-belt drive + VFD on motor: Motor speed varies, belt ratio is fixed. Best option when a ratio change is also needed (e.g., 4-pole motor at 1,450 RPM → 960 RPM fan shaft). VFD sizing: see VFD guide.
  • Direct VFD drive (no belt): Most efficient and reliable for accessible shaft geometries. Eliminate the belt entirely when motor and driven shaft can be directly coupled — see shaft coupling guide.

Pakistan decision rule: If speed variation >30% is needed more than 4 hours/day, add a VFD and remove belt drive slip losses. For fixed-speed applications, narrow V-belt (SPB/SPC) on correctly aligned and tensioned sheaves with a quality belt is the most cost-effective solution.

15. Banded / Joined V-Belts — Heavy Duty Pakistan Applications

Banded belts (also called joined belts or link belts) connect multiple classical V-belts at the back with a flexible band. This prevents individual belt vibration (rocking), which is a common failure mode when a drive requires 4 or more belts in parallel — the belt set begins to oscillate laterally, causing rapid uneven wear.

Pakistan applications for banded belts:

  • Sugar mill crusher drives (typically 6–8 belts C or D section)
  • Large centrifugal fans in Faisalabad textile processing exhaust systems
  • Mine ventilation fans in FATA and Balochistan coal operations
  • Compressors above 55 kW with classical sheaves

PKR 2026 price: Banded B-section (3-belt set): PKR 3,500–7,000. Banded C-section (4-belt set): PKR 8,000–18,000. Available from industrial suppliers in Karachi SITE and Lahore industrial sectors — not typically stocked in open market.

16. Belt Drive Installation Checklist — Pakistan Field Standard

  1. Check sheave groove profile matches belt section — use a sheave gauge (PKR 800–2,000 at Hall Road)
  2. Inspect sheave grooves for wear — groove wear >0.5 mm at sidewall reduces grip and accelerates belt wear
  3. Align pulleys before installing belts — never force a belt onto misaligned pulleys
  4. Never use a screwdriver or bar to lever a belt onto a sheave — reduces centre distance to install, then extend to correct distance
  5. Check shaft runout — >0.1 mm TIR on driven shaft indicates bearing wear that will destroy belts
  6. Tension with deflection-force method — not thumb pressure
  7. Run 30 minutes, re-tension (bed-in effect)
  8. Re-tension after 8 hours operation (second bed-in check)
  9. Mark belt with installation date — for life tracking in Pakistan maintenance logs
  10. Always replace belt set completely — never mix old and new belts on the same drive (uneven load sharing)

18. Counterfeit V-Belt Detection — Pakistan Field Tests

Counterfeit V-belts (often labelled with premium brand names) are common in Pakistan wholesale markets. Field tests before purchasing bulk quantities:

  1. Cross-section measurement: Use a vernier caliper. A B-section belt should measure 17.0 ± 0.5 mm top width. Undersized by 1+ mm = sub-spec compound fill.
  2. Cord exposure test: Bend belt through 90° at mid-point and look for cord pattern through the outer rubber. The cord rows should be evenly spaced and not immediately visible (thick outer rubber = quality). Cords visible through outer rubber = thin outer compound, early cracking.
  3. Hardness feel: Quality V-belt rubber has consistent firmness. Soft (under-vulcanised) or brittle (over-vulcanised) indicates compound problem. No field durometer needed — compare to a known-good belt.
  4. Bend radius test: Flex by hand. Premium belt has smooth, even flex resistance. Local counterfeit belts often have stiff sections (inconsistent vulcanisation) or delamination at the cord layer when bent tightly.
  5. Seller verification: Request manufacturer's batch certificate. Legitimate premium belts include a batch number traceable to the factory. Generic belts with premium brand stickers have no traceable batch.

20. Pakistan Buyer Checklist — 10 Questions Before Purchasing V-Belts

  1. What is the motor kW and RPM? What is the driven machine RPM required? (Establishes speed ratio and design power.)
  2. What is the current belt section and cross-section dimension? (Confirm by measurement, not label.)
  3. What is the centre distance and belt length? (Measure installed belt with string, or count belt teeth for timing belt.)
  4. How many belts are in the set? (Never mix old and new.)
  5. Is the sheave classical or narrow-profile taper-lock? (Wrong sheave = wrong belt section.)
  6. What is the duty cycle and load-shedding restart frequency? (Add Pakistan SF add-on if ≥4 restarts/day.)
  7. What is the ambient temperature at the drive location? (Rooftop or enclosed drive above 50°C ambient — specify heat-resistant compound.)
  8. Is the application fixed-speed or variable-speed? (Variable speed → consider VFD instead of belt ratio change.)
  9. Is the driven machine timing-critical? (Yes → timing belt, not V-belt.)
  10. Can you verify manufacturer batch number on the purchased belts? (Counterfeit screen.)

FAQ

Q: Can I use an SPA belt on a classical A-section sheave?
No. SPA has a different angle and profile from classical A. Running SPA on a classical A sheave seats incorrectly — the belt either sits too high (no sidewall contact) or too low (bottom contact only), destroying the belt within hours. Always use matching sheave and belt section.

Q: My V-belt is glazed and slipping. Can I use belt dressing spray?
Belt dressing spray is a temporary fix that embeds into the rubber, contaminates the sheave groove surface, and ultimately accelerates glaze formation. In Pakistan heat, belt dressing also softens the compound. Replace the belt and investigate the root cause of slip (under-tension, sheave wear, or overload).

Q: How do I know if a sheave groove needs replacement?
Place a new belt in the groove. The belt top surface should sit flush with or 1–2 mm above the sheave rim. If the belt sits below the rim surface (belt has sunk into the groove), the groove sidewalls are worn — replace the sheave. Running a new belt in a worn sheave will destroy the new belt within 30–50% of rated life.

Q: Timing belt or chain for a CNC router in Pakistan?
For Pakistan CNC routers with cutting area up to 1,300 × 2,500 mm: GT2 9 mm or 12 mm timing belt is standard. Chain has backlash, is heavier, and requires lubrication. Timing belt is zero-backlash, lighter, and maintenance-free for typical CNC router duty cycles. For very long axes (>3 m travel), consider rack and pinion with servo motor — see the servo drive guide.

Q: What belt is used on 4-inch tubewell pump drives in Punjab?
Most 4-inch tubewell pumps in Punjab use A-section or B-section classical V-belts driven from a 3-phase 5.5–11 kW motor. The most common failure in tubewell belt drives is over-tensioning by the installer — driven by the mistaken belief that tighter belt = less slip. Correct tension via deflection-force method; use correct belt length; replace belt and sheave together when belt life has been short.
