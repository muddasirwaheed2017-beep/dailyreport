CPO vs EMSP: The Two-Role Structure

International best practice separates two roles that Pakistani operators often conflate:

  • CPO (Charge Point Operator): Owns/operates the physical charging infrastructure. Manages OCPP chargers. Responsible for uptime and power delivery.
  • EMSP (E-Mobility Service Provider): Manages EV driver accounts, payment, and roaming authorisation. Provides the driver-facing app and RFID cards.

In Pakistan's early market, most players are CPO+EMSP combined. As the market matures (2027+), separation will occur — a hotel CPO will partner with a national EMSP rather than build its own driver app.

5. EMSP & OCPI Roaming in Pakistan

OCPI (Open Charge Point Interface) is the protocol that enables a driver registered with one EMSP to charge on any CPO's network — just like international credit card roaming. It is the difference between isolated charging islands and a national EV charging network.

OCPI Key Modules for Pakistan CPOs

  • Locations/EVSEs: Publish your charger data to the national hub so drivers can see your site in any app.
  • Sessions: Real-time session data for roaming billing reconciliation.
  • CDRs (Charge Detail Records): The invoice unit for inter-operator settlement — typically PKR 2–4/kWh roaming fee to EMSP.
  • Tokens: Authorise drivers from other networks on your chargers without individual account setup.
  • Tariffs: Publish dynamic pricing so EMSP can show correct cost to drivers before they plug in.

Vendor lock-in trap: Charger-bundled CPMS platforms often use proprietary extensions to OCPP that prevent migration to a third-party CPMS without replacing hardware. For any deployment above 10 EVSE, insist on a standard OCPP 2.0.1-only communication contract with the charger vendor — test on a neutral CPMS before accepting delivery.

Key CPMS Selection Criteria for Pakistan

  1. Payment gateway: Must support JazzCash, Easypaisa, HBL Pay, and standard Visa/Mastercard. OCPP defines charging; payment is CPMS-layer. Confirm before purchase.
  2. 4G/SIM connectivity: Pakistan's fibre reliability on commercial premises is 92%+ in Tier-1 cities but drops to 65% in Tier-2. CPMS must handle offline operation and queue sessions for sync when connectivity returns.
  3. Urdu/Pashto interface: Driver-facing mobile app. English is acceptable for urban premium sites; Urdu essential for Tier-2 market penetration.
  4. FBR integration: Pakistan's tax digitalisation push means CPMS must generate FBR-compliant invoices with NTN, STRN, and HSN code for electricity supply.
  5. WAPDA demand charge management: The CPMS must have configurable site power limits and load balancing profiles that account for PKR demand charges, not just capacity limits.
  6. Data sovereignty: NTRC's draft Data Localisation for Critical Infrastructure (2026) may require Pakistani CPO session data to be stored on Pakistan-hosted servers. Plan for local data hosting capability.

12. FAQ

What is OCPP and why does it matter for CPOs in Pakistan?
OCPP (Open Charge Point Protocol) is the international standard that connects EV chargers to a central management system. For CPOs in Pakistan, OCPP 2.0.1 enables remote monitoring, billing, roaming with other networks, and compliance with NTRC's forthcoming mandatory standards. Without OCPP compliance, a charger is an isolated island — no remote management, no roaming revenue, no NTRC certification from 2027.
How much does CPMS (Charge Point Management Software) cost in Pakistan?
CPMS costs in Pakistan vary: open-source self-hosted (PKR 150,000–500,000 setup), cloud SaaS (PKR 8,000–35,000/month per 10 charge points), and white-label enterprise platforms (PKR 2,500,000–8,000,000 one-time). Cloud SaaS is recommended for most Pakistani CPOs starting with 5–50 charge points. Total Year 1 CPMS cost for a 6-EVSE site on SaaS: approximately PKR 300,000–420,000.
What is the WAPDA/LESCO commercial tariff for EV charging businesses in Pakistan?
WAPDA's EV-3 commercial tariff for CPOs (2026) is PKR 42–58/kWh depending on time-of-use and demand charges. LESCO adds a PKR 850/kW/month demand charge component. CPOs typically sell at PKR 85–120/kWh to end users, achieving 45–65% gross margin. LESCO FPA surcharges (PKR 4–8/kWh) must be absorbed via a pricing buffer.
Is OCPI roaming available in Pakistan yet?
OCPI roaming is in early stages in Pakistan as of 2026. NTRC's EV charging framework (2025) mandates roaming capability for public fast-charger networks by 2027. Early bilateral roaming agreements between PARCO EV and charge2go are operational on OCPI 2.2. Build your CPMS with OCPI 2.2 from day one — it adds minimal cost but future-proofs your network for the roaming mandate.
What is the minimum viable CPO setup in Pakistan?
A minimum viable CPO in Pakistan requires: 2–4 AC chargers (22kW) + 1 DC fast charger (60–120kW), cloud CPMS subscription, WAPDA EV-3 tariff connection, payment gateway (JazzCash/Easypaisa + card), and NTRC registration. Total initial investment: PKR 8–15 million for a 4-charger urban site. Expected payback at 25% utilisation: 18–24 months.
Can I use solar power for my EV charging site in Pakistan?
Yes, and it significantly improves CPO economics. A 50–100kWp rooftop solar system (cost PKR 5–9M) reduces WAPDA energy cost by 30–50% and partially insulates against LESCO demand charges. BESS integration further enables peak shaving. For solar DC protection equipment (DC MCBs, combiner boxes, surge arresters), plan this into the site electrical design from day one.
