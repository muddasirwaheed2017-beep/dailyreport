What Is a Digital Motor Protection Relay? (Pakistan Context)

A digital motor protection relay replaces 3–4 separate devices with one unit that continuously monitors the motor's electrical environment and trips the contactor or MCCB the moment any parameter exceeds the programmed threshold. Where a separate thermal OL relay only responds to current magnitude, a digital relay also tracks phase balance, earth fault current, starting conditions, and in many units, direct winding temperature via thermistor (PTC) input.

In Pakistan, three WAPDA supply characteristics make digital motor protection more important than in most countries:

  • Voltage asymmetry: WAPDA 11kV lines regularly show 2–5% voltage unbalance during peak summer load. IEC 60034-26 states that 2% voltage unbalance produces approximately 8% increase in rotor heating. At 4% unbalance, rotor temperature rise doubles. Negative-sequence protection catches this; a thermal OL relay does not.
  • Load-shedding thermal cycling: 8–16 hours of daily load-shedding in summer 2025 means motors in textile and food processing plants start and stop 4–6 times more per day than IEC Class F insulation is designed for. Trip-class-aware starting current supervision (locked-rotor detection) is critical. A basic thermal OL relay's thermal model does not adequately track repeated starting thermal accumulation.
  • 45°C ambient in Pakistan motor rooms: IEC motor ratings assume 40°C ambient. At 45°C, the motor is already operating 5°C above nameplate. A digital relay with external PTC thermistor input directly measures winding temperature and trips before the insulation sees thermal damage — regardless of what the current is doing.

5 Protection Functions a Digital Motor Protection Relay Combines

1. Thermal Overload with True Thermal Memory

Unlike a bimetallic relay, a digital relay's microprocessor-based thermal model accumulates heat from starting currents and remembers the motor's thermal state across power cycles. When WAPDA load-shedding interrupts power for 45 minutes and the motor restarts, the relay knows the motor was already at 60% thermal capacity — a bimetallic relay reset to zero when the contactor opened. Pakistan Class selection guidance:

  • Class 10: Centrifugal pumps, fans, belt conveyors — fast-starting loads.
  • Class 20: Compressors, reciprocating pumps, loaded conveyors — medium starting duration.
  • Class 30: Crushing mills, rubber mixers, heavy grinding — long starting time under full load.

2. Phase Failure and Voltage Asymmetry (Negative-Sequence Protection)

A digital relay measures the negative-sequence component of the current, which rises disproportionately with voltage unbalance. The IEC 60255 negative-sequence threshold is typically settable from 5–40% of positive-sequence current. For Pakistan WAPDA supply, set the asymmetry threshold at 15–20% (corresponding to approximately 4–5% voltage unbalance). This trips the motor before rotor heating becomes destructive — the phase failure relay in a 3-device setup typically requires complete phase loss (100% loss), missing the partial unbalance failure mode entirely.

3. Earth Fault Protection

Better digital relays include a 3-core CT summation input or Rogowski coil input for earth fault detection. Pakistan-specific setting consideration: WAPDA earthing systems have significant stray earth currents in industrial areas, particularly in older textile/food processing zones. Earth fault threshold must be set high enough to avoid nuisance tripping from stray currents (typically 0.1–0.2 × FLC minimum), but the protection exists where a separate ELR or a basic thermal OL provides nothing.

4. Stall and Locked Rotor Detection

If a motor fails to accelerate to full speed within the programmed starting time (typically 10–30 seconds, application-dependent), the relay trips. This catches: mechanical jam (textile yarn tangle, pump impeller clog, bearing seize), single-phase start with insufficient starting torque, and incorrect star-delta transition leaving the motor in partial rotation. A thermal OL relay eventually trips a stalled motor but only after significant winding damage has occurred; the locked-rotor detection in a digital relay trips within 3–5 seconds of stall detection.

5. Thermistor (PTC) Input — Pakistan High-Ambient Application

For motors installed in 45–50°C enclosures (many Pakistan cotton gin and textile air-handling applications), a PTC thermistor embedded in the motor winding measures actual insulation temperature. The digital relay's thermistor input trips at the PTC's resistance threshold (typically 3.3kΩ for standard PTC elements). This catches cases where current is normal but ambient temperature is pushing winding temperature into the insulation damage zone — a condition that no current-based relay can detect.

Negative-Sequence Protection — Pakistan WAPDA Voltage Asymmetry Settings

Voltage asymmetry is the leading silent killer of Pakistan industrial motors. The IEC formula for voltage unbalance percentage (VU%) is: VU% = (Max deviation from average) / (Average of 3 line voltages) × 100. For a typical Pakistan industrial feeder with 360V / 395V / 380V (summer peak): average = 378V, max deviation = 18V, VU% = 4.76%.

IEC 60034-26 Table 1 gives approximate rotor temperature rise increase:

  • 1% VU: ~2% additional rotor heating
  • 2% VU: ~8% additional rotor heating
  • 3% VU: ~18% additional rotor heating
  • 4% VU: ~32% additional rotor heating
  • 5% VU: ~50% additional rotor heating

Pakistan setting recommendation: Set the negative-sequence current threshold at 20–25% for WAPDA-supplied motors (corresponding to approximately 4–5% voltage unbalance). Set the delay to 5–10 seconds to ride through momentary switching transients. This setting prevents nuisance tripping during normal feeder fluctuations while catching sustained asymmetry from a failed WAPDA distribution transformer tap or a loose 11kV termination.

Earth Fault Sensitivity — Pakistan-Specific Setting

Pakistan industrial earthing systems, particularly in older textile mill and food processing zones in Faisalabad, Lahore, and Karachi, carry significant stray earth currents from poorly maintained neutral earthing systems on the WAPDA LV network. Setting earth fault threshold too sensitively causes nuisance tripping. Pakistan-specific earth fault setting guidance:

  • Motors in well-maintained earthed premises: Set earth fault threshold at 10–15% of motor FLC. This catches genuine winding-to-frame faults without responding to stray currents.
  • Motors in older industrial zones with poor earthing infrastructure: Set threshold at 20–30% of FLC. Pair with time delay (0.5–1.0 second) to ensure trip is sustained fault, not transient.
  • Motors on WAPDA TN-C supply (combined neutral/earth): Set threshold at 30–40% of FLC with 1.0-second delay. TN-C neutrals carry load unbalance current which appears as earth current in the CT summation circuit.

For correct earth fault detection, use a core-balance CT (CBCT) around all 3 phase conductors (and the neutral if present). Do NOT use 3 individual CTs in summation — Pakistan sites with harmonic loads (VFDs, UPS, switching power supplies) will produce false summation earth currents from CT manufacturing tolerances at triplen harmonic frequencies. See the ELR buyer guide for CBCT sizing and installation.

Digital Motor Protection Relay Wiring in Pakistan MCC

Standard wiring for a mid-range digital motor protection relay in a Pakistan MCC feeder:

  • Current input: 3× secondary CT outputs (5A secondary standard for large motors, 1A secondary for motors above 160kW). Wire CT secondaries in a closed loop through the relay — never open-circuit a CT secondary while the motor is running.
  • Auxiliary supply: 110VAC or 24VDC (relay model-dependent). In Pakistan MCC with 24VDC SMPS, use 24VDC auxiliary supply. In older MCCs with only 110VAC control bus, use 110VAC auxiliary supply version. Do not mix.
  • Trip output: Use relay's NC (normally closed) contact in series with the contactor coil circuit. This means protection relay failure (relay supply fails) trips the motor — fail-safe. Most Pakistan panel builders wire NO contacts — incorrect and unsafe.
  • MODBUS connection: RS485 two-wire (A+, B−) with 120Ω termination resistor at the last relay on the bus. Pakistan panels with multiple relays on one RS485 bus: max 32 devices per segment without repeater.
  • Thermistor connection: PTC thermistor connects to dedicated relay terminals (typically labelled T1, T2). The relay applies a small test current (less than 1mA) — do not use for anything else. If no thermistor is installed, connect a 100Ω resistor across T1-T2 terminals to prevent nuisance trips.

For SMPS auxiliary supply sizing in the MCC, see the DIN rail 24VDC SMPS buyer guide. For interposing relay requirements between protection relay trip output and PLC, see the interposing relay buyer guide.

7 Pakistan Mistakes in Motor Protection — PKR Repair Costs

  1. Setting thermal OL at 125% FLC "because the motor runs hot" — PKR 55,000–200,000 rewind cost. The correct OL setting is 100–110% of nameplate FLC. "Running hot" is a symptom to investigate (asymmetric supply, high ambient, under-voltage), not a reason to raise the trip threshold. Raising the threshold removes the protection.
  2. Using bimetallic thermal OL relay for Class 30 loads — PKR 40,000–120,000 repair. A bimetallic relay cannot model the sustained starting current of a heavy mechanical load. It either nuisance-trips during starting (if set too tight) or fails to catch overload (if set to avoid starting trips). Digital Class 30 setting solves this precisely.
  3. Ignoring 2–4% WAPDA voltage asymmetry — PKR 100,000–350,000 motor failure every 18–36 months. The failure is slow (rotor insulation degradation over months) and appears as "motor life is short in Pakistan" — the real cause is unbalanced WAPDA supply combined with no negative-sequence protection. Digital relay with asymmetry threshold set at 20% solves this permanently.
  4. Not setting a lockout (anti-restart) after phase failure trip — PKR 20,000–80,000 bearing and winding damage. After a phase failure trip, if WAPDA restores supply with the contactor still energised, the motor starts single-phase and draws 3× FLC until another trip. A digital relay with manual reset requirement prevents this restart. Phase failure relays with auto-reset are dangerous in Pakistan load-shedding environments.
  5. Wiring protection relay trip contact as NO instead of NC — zero protection on relay supply failure. If the control supply fails and the trip contact is wired normally-open, the motor runs unprotected. Pakistani MCC builders often wire NO "to save the extra wire run to close the circuit." This is the wrong choice for a protection device. Always wire the trip contact NC (fail-safe).
  6. Not installing a CBCT for earth fault — missing the most common Pakistan motor winding failure mode. Moisture-induced winding-to-frame faults are the most common cause of motor rewinds in Pakistan (humid monsoon season, flooded motor rooms, outdoor pump sets). A 3-device combination without a separate ELR and CBCT provides no earth fault protection. A digital relay with built-in earth fault detection via CT summation provides partial earth fault detection — a CBCT on a dedicated relay gives the most sensitive and Pakistan-appropriate protection.
  7. Using a single-phase timer to replace star-delta transition timing instead of the protection relay's starting supervision — PKR 35,000–90,000 motor damage. Pakistan panel builders often use a cheap single-phase timer for the star-delta transition. This timer has no knowledge of whether the motor has reached sufficient speed. A digital protection relay with motor speed estimator or current-based transition detection ensures the transition happens when the motor current drops to running level — preventing the destructive inrush from a premature transition. See the contactor buyer guide for correct star-delta interlock wiring.

Frequently Asked Questions — Motor Protection Relay Pakistan

What is the price of a digital motor protection relay in Pakistan in 2026?

Digital motor protection relay prices in Pakistan range from PKR 7,000–12,000 for entry-level (thermal OL + phase failure only) to PKR 18,000–32,000 for mid-range (thermal model + negative-sequence + earth fault + locked rotor + thermistor) to PKR 42,000–110,000 for full-featured MODBUS-capable relays. Mid-range at PKR 18,000–32,000 is recommended for most Pakistan industrial motors above 22kW.

What is negative-sequence protection and why is it important for Pakistan motors?

Negative-sequence protection detects voltage asymmetry (2–5% unbalance common on WAPDA industrial feeders) that causes 8–50% additional rotor heating. Bimetallic OL relays and most basic phase failure relays don't detect this level of unbalance. A digital relay with negative-sequence current measurement trips before the rotor heating becomes destructive — essential for Pakistan motors above 22kW.

Should I use a digital protection relay or separate thermal OL + phase failure relay + ELR?

For Pakistan motors below 22kW on stable WAPDA supply, a 3-device combination at PKR 8,000–16,000 is acceptable. For motors above 22kW, on feeders with voltage asymmetry, or with critical continuous duty, a mid-range digital relay at PKR 18,000–32,000 is strongly recommended. The digital relay provides negative-sequence protection, locked-rotor detection, thermal memory across power cycles, and PTC thermistor input — none of which the 3-device combination can provide.

What trip class should I set for a Pakistan centrifugal pump?

Set Class 10 for centrifugal pumps (tube-well pumps, HVAC chilled water, fire pumps). Class 10 allows up to 10 seconds at 7.2× FLC for starting. Use Class 20 for compressors with loaded start. Use Class 30 for heavy grinding mills, crushers, and sugar mill rollers.

How do I set earth fault protection for a Pakistan WAPDA supply?

For Pakistan industrial zones with older earthing, set earth fault threshold at 20–30% of motor FLC with 0.5–1.0 second delay to avoid WAPDA stray earth current nuisance trips. For well-maintained premises, 10–15% FLC with 0.2 second delay. Always use a CBCT — not 3 individual CTs in summation — on sites with harmonic loads (VFDs, UPS).

Can I use a motor protection relay with a VFD in Pakistan?

Most VFDs have built-in motor protection. An external relay on the VFD output will false-trip on harmonic currents. For VFD-motor Pakistan applications: rely on VFD's internal protection + MCCB on input side + motor PTC wired to VFD's thermistor input. See the VFD buyer guide for details.

What happens to the trip relay if WAPDA load-shedding interrupts power?

A bimetallic OL relay resets to zero when power is interrupted — thermal memory is lost. A digital relay stores its thermal model in NVRAM and retains the motor's thermal state across power interruptions. When supply restores, the relay knows the actual thermal capacity remaining — critical for Pakistan motors that start 4–6 times per day due to WAPDA load-shedding.

Why should the motor protection relay trip contact be wired NC (not NO)?

NC wiring is fail-safe: if the relay's auxiliary supply fails, the circuit de-energises and the contactor drops out, stopping the motor. NO wiring leaves the motor running unprotected if the relay supply fails. Always wire protection relay trip contacts NC in series with the contactor coil circuit.
