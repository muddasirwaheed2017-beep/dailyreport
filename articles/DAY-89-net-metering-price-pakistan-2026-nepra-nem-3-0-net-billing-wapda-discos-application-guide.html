Solar Net Metering Pakistan 2026: NEPRA NEM 3.0, Net Billing Transition, DISCO Application Process & PKR Economics — Complete Buyer Guide

Pakistan's solar net metering landscape changed fundamentally in 2023–2026. NEPRA's shift from NEM 2.0 (1:1 unit exchange) to NEM 3.0 / Net Billing (buy-low, sell-lower) slashed paper ROI for new applicants — but strategic system sizing, battery pairing, and TOU arbitrage still make grid-tied solar financially compelling. This guide covers everything: per-DISCO capacity limits, current PKR application fees, anti-islanding standards, bi-directional meter specs, UR-1 tariff economics, and the 7 steps every Pakistani applicant gets wrong.

1. Pakistan Net Metering History: NEM 1.0 → 2.0 → 3.0 / Net Billing

Pakistan's distributed generation (DG) framework has gone through three distinct phases:

Pakistan Net Metering Policy Evolution
EraNEPRA NotificationMechanismExport RateKey Change
NEM 1.0S.R.O. 892(I)/2015Net Metering (units banked)1:1 unit creditFirst residential solar framework
NEM 2.0S.R.O. 1170(I)/2019Net Metering (units banked)1:1 unit credit + annual settlementExtended to 1MW; commercial/industrial inclusion
NEM 3.0 / Net BillingNEPRA/R/TRF-434/2023 (April 2023)Net Billing (PKR settlement)Buy-back at avoided-cost rate (~PKR 10–11/kWh) vs import rate PKR 30–60/kWhEliminated 1:1 unit banking; export now compensated at wholesale avoided-cost rate

Critical NEM 3.0 transition rule: Existing NEM 2.0 licence holders were grandfathered at 1:1 unit credit for the remainder of their 7-year licence term. New applicants from July 2023 onward are automatically on Net Billing. This means two neighbours with identical 5kW rooftop systems can have dramatically different economics depending on their application date.

2026 status: NEPRA has confirmed no reversal to NEM 2.0. Net Billing is permanent policy. DISCOs are completing meter infrastructure upgrades for bi-directional metering rollout. Some DISCOs (notably K-Electric and LESCO) are processing new Net Billing applications; others (PESCO, HESCO) still have processing backlogs of 4–8 months.

Key insight: Under Net Billing, self-consumption is king. A system sized to maximise self-consumption (with or without a battery storage system) delivers 85–90% of NEM 2.0 economics. Oversized systems that export heavily are the poor performers under NEM 3.0.

4.2 Inverter Anti-Islanding Standard — Pakistan (IEC 62116, not UL 1741)

This is one of the most critical and most misunderstood technical requirements in Pakistani solar:

  • NEPRA requirement: IEC 62116:2014 (Island prevention measures for utility-interconnected photovoltaic inverters)
  • NOT acceptable: UL 1741 alone — this is the US standard and is not sufficient for NEPRA type-approval
  • Inverter approval body: Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority (PSQCA) or NEPRA type-approval letter
  • IEC 62116 key requirements for Pakistan DISCOs:
    • Anti-islanding detection time: ≤2 seconds for frequency shift methods; ≤150ms for voltage deviation (under/overvoltage)
    • Frequency thresholds: Trip at <47.5 Hz or >52.5 Hz (WAPDA nominal 50 Hz)
    • Voltage thresholds: Trip at <80% or >110% of 220V nominal
    • Reconnection delay after grid restoration: 5 minutes minimum

Many lower-cost inverters sold in Pakistan carry CE marking (which implies IEC 62116 compliance) but lack actual type-approval documentation. The DISCO's DG cell will ask for the type-approval certificate at inspection. Sourcing this document from your installer before purchase protects against rejection at commissioning.

4.3 Earthing Requirements for Grid-Tied Inverters

NEPRA DG regulations require dedicated earthing for the inverter AC output, separate from the building's existing earth. Minimum requirements:

  • Earth electrode resistance: ≤5Ω (residential); ≤2Ω (commercial >20kW)
  • Conductor: 10mm² minimum copper for inverter chassis earth
  • Separate AC combiner with SPD (Type 2, 40kA) before inverter AC output

5. UR-1 TOU Tariff & Net Billing Economics: 2026 PKR Calculations

Understanding NEPRA's UR-1 TOU (Time-of-Use) tariff structure is essential for sizing a Net Billing system correctly. The tariff creates three import price bands:

NEPRA UR-1 TOU Tariff — Residential Rates (FY2026, PKR/kWh incl. surcharges)
PeriodHours (Mon–Sat)Energy Charge (PKR/kWh)Solar Generation Match
Off-Peak23:00–07:00PKR 22–28 (low consumption slabs)No solar generation — nighttime
Shoulder07:00–09:00 & 17:00–19:00PKR 30–40Morning ramp-up (07:00–09:00 moderate solar); evening (17:00–19:00 declining solar

Sunday/HolidayAll dayOff-peak rates applySolar generation wasted if no self-consumption or battery

TOU Self-Consumption Strategy: 09:00–17:00 Window

The golden rule of Net Billing economics: every kWh consumed from solar panels during 09:00–17:00 (rather than from WAPDA grid) avoids PKR 45–62/kWh. Every kWh exported during this same period earns only PKR 10.69/kWh. The self-consumption multiplier is:

  • High-consumption households (slab 5+): self-consumption value = PKR 62/kWh vs export value = PKR 10.69/kWh → 5.8× multiplier for self-consumption over export
  • Low-consumption households (slab 2): self-consumption value = PKR 30/kWh vs export value = PKR 10.69/kWh → 2.8× multiplier

Anti-Arbitrage Clause: What It Means in Practice

Under NEM 3.0, NEPRA prohibits "arbitrage" — importing cheap electricity (off-peak, PKR 22–28/kWh) and exporting it during peak to earn the avoided-cost credit on peak import. The DG licence condition states the export pattern must correlate with genuine solar generation. DISCOs with AMI meters can detect this pattern. Penalty: revocation of DG licence. This means battery systems must be configured to charge from solar only, not from grid — a key BESS configuration requirement.

Sizing Rule for Net Billing Era

Right-size for self-consumption, not generation. Under NEM 2.0, you could oversize to export and receive 1:1 credit. Under Net Billing, exporting more than ~20% of generation significantly degrades ROI. The optimal system size covers 80–100% of your peak-hour (09:00–17:00) load, with a small battery for morning and evening shoulder hours. See our mounting structure guide for roof-area planning.

8. Battery Storage Pairing Strategy for Net Billing Era

Under Net Billing, battery storage becomes a strategic asset rather than a luxury. The economics are simple:

  • Store solar surplus (would otherwise export at PKR 10.69/kWh) in battery
  • Discharge at evening peak (17:00–22:00) to displace WAPDA import at PKR 45–62/kWh
  • Net arbitrage value per kWh stored: PKR 34–51/kWh (value of displacement minus avoided export credit)
  • Battery payback contribution: A 5kWh usable battery shifting 4kWh/day = PKR 49,640–74,460/year value → contributes PKR 350,000–520,000 over 7-year BESS cycle life

NEPRA anti-arbitrage compliance: Battery charge source must be documented as solar-only. Configure the battery management system (BMS) to prohibit grid charging (or limit to emergency-only with time-stamp logging). If your DISCO audits export patterns, grid-charged-then-exported batteries violate DG licence conditions.

9. Seven Mistakes Pakistani Applicants Make in Net Metering Applications

  1. Buying equipment before feeder capacity check. If the feeder is full, no amount of money or documentation gets your application approved. Always submit a written feeder inquiry first (Step 1 above). We see applicants spending PKR 800,000–1,500,000 on equipment only to be told feeder capacity is exhausted.
  2. Using a non-IEC-62116 inverter. Several Chinese inverter brands sold at Hafeez Centre and electrical markets carry CE marks but no IEC 62116 type-approval documentation. DISCO inspectors are increasingly checking this at commissioning. A failed commissioning means reinstalling the inverter — costing time, and potentially the LOI validity window expiring.
  3. Earthing resistance >5Ω at inspection. The single most common commissioning failure. Soil resistivity in Punjab and Sindh plains is moderate to good, but sandy soils (Lahore outskirts, Karachi coastal) can be high-resistivity. Bentonite-treated earth pits or extended rod lengths resolve this. Budget PKR 15,000–40,000 for a properly installed earthing system — do not cut this cost.
  4. Missing the LOI validity window. The DISCO issues an LOI typically valid for 90–120 days. If your installation is delayed (panel delivery, roof work, etc.) and the LOI expires, you restart the application — paying fees again and waiting months more. Accelerate installation once the LOI arrives.
  5. Oversizing under Net Billing assumptions from NEM 2.0 installers. Many established installers still size systems to maximise generation (NEM 2.0 logic). Under Net Billing, an 8kW system on a 300-unit household bill will export 50–60% of generation at PKR 10.69/kWh — making the "extra" panels barely economical. Right-size to your daytime load.
  6. No SPD or incorrect SPD type on AC side. DISCO inspection requires Type 2 SPD (40kA) on the AC output side of the inverter. Type 1 SPD is required if there is no separate lightning protection system (LPS) bonded to the building. See our SPD guide for correct specification.
  7. Assuming DG licence transfer is automatic on property sale. The DG licence is tied to the metre consumer number, not the property deed. When selling a property with a solar system, the buyer must apply for licence transfer — and if the feeder is now at capacity, the buyer may not qualify. This is a real-estate complication to disclose and plan for.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I still get NEM 2.0 (1:1 unit banking) in 2026?
No. NEPRA's April 2023 notification (NEPRA/R/TRF-434/2023) made Net Billing mandatory for all new DG applications. Existing NEM 2.0 licence holders retain their grandfathered status for the remaining term of their 7-year licence. Once a NEM 2.0 licence expires, renewal is on Net Billing terms.
Q: My DISCO is taking 6+ months to process my application. What can I do?
NEPRA's DG Regulations require DISCOs to process complete applications within 60 days. File a formal complaint with NEPRA (nepra.org.pk → Complaints → DG/Net Metering) citing the submission date and DISCO reference number. NEPRA has directed multiple DISCOs to clear backlogs. Include copies of all submission receipts.
Q: Can I export to the grid without a DG licence?
No — this is technically possible (some inverters do export without DISCO notification) but illegal under NEPRA regulations. DISCOs' AMI systems increasingly detect unauthorised DG. Penalties include disconnection and fines. Always obtain a DG Generation Licence before energising a grid-tied system.
Q: Will solar help during load-shedding?
Standard grid-tied inverters (without battery) shut down during load-shedding by design — this is the anti-islanding protection working correctly (to protect DISCO linemen). A hybrid inverter with battery continues to power the home during outages. See our hybrid inverter guide for complete specs.
Q: Is there a minimum system size for Net Metering?
NEPRA regulations specify no minimum system size. However, practical minimum is approximately 1kW (below which DISCO application overhead exceeds economic benefit). Most DISCOs process residential systems down to 1kW, though some DG cells have informal minimums of 3kW.
Q: How does Net Metering affect my electricity bill?
Your bill now shows two registers: import kWh (billed at UR-1 stepped rates) and export kWh (credited at PKR 10.69/kWh avoided-cost rate). The credit offsets your import charges. If credit exceeds import in a month, the surplus carries forward as a PKR balance on your account (not refunded as cash). If annual credit exceeds annual import, NEPRA allows the DISCO to pay out the surplus at avoided-cost rate annually — but this situation is rare with a correctly sized system.
