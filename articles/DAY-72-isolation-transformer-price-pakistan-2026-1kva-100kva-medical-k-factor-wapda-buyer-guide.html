2. How an Isolation Transformer Works

An isolation transformer has a primary winding and a secondary winding wound on the same magnetic core, with a turns ratio of 1:1 for the same voltage in and out, or a different ratio for voltage conversion. The key is galvanic isolation — there is no electrical connection between primary and secondary. Current flows only via the magnetic field.

What This Achieves

  • Common-mode rejection: Noise present equally on both Live and Neutral (common-mode) cannot couple through the magnetic field because it does not create differential flux. Attenuation is 60–80 dB without Faraday shield, >80 dB with.
  • New safety earth reference: The secondary neutral is earthed at a single clean point in the installation. Neutral-to-earth voltage on the WAPDA supply (8–35 V in Karachi) does not appear on the secondary.
  • Fault current interruption: A line-to-earth fault on the secondary draws fault current only through the capacitance between windings — a few milliamps, not 50+ A — so the first fault is safe to touch while a ground fault monitor raises an alarm.
Key principle: An isolation transformer does NOT regulate voltage — it does not fix ±15% WAPDA swings. For voltage regulation, pair it with a servo stabilizer on the primary side. The stabilizer cleans the voltage; the isolation transformer then removes the noise and provides a clean earth reference.

3. Faraday Electrostatic Shield — the Critical Differentiator

A standard isolation transformer has an air gap between primary and secondary windings. High-frequency noise couples capacitively across this gap — meaning the transformer passes conducted EMI at frequencies above ~1 kHz.

A Faraday electrostatic shield is a grounded copper foil or winding layer inserted between primary and secondary. It intercepts capacitively coupled current and diverts it to earth, achieving an additional 20–40 dB attenuation at frequencies 1 kHz to 1 MHz.

4. K-Factor Rating for Non-Linear Loads — Pakistan Application Table

Non-linear loads (VFDs, switching-mode power supplies, UPS systems, LED drivers, chargers) draw current in sharp pulses rather than a smooth sine wave. This creates harmonic currents that circulate in the transformer windings, causing excess heating beyond what the KVA rating predicts. A standard K-1 transformer will overheat and fail prematurely on these loads.

K-factor is a measure of how severe the harmonic content is. A K-13 transformer can handle loads with K-factor up to 13 without derating.

10. Sizing Method — Single-Phase and Three-Phase

Single-Phase Sizing (Medical / Lab / Small CNC)

  1. List all connected equipment with rated wattage.
  2. Multiply total watts by 1.25 diversity factor (not everything runs simultaneously at 100%).
  3. Convert to KVA: KVA = (Watts × 1.25) ÷ (Power Factor × 1000). Use PF = 0.8 for mixed loads.
  4. Apply ambient derating from Section 7.
  5. Select next standard size up: 1, 2, 3.15, 5, 7.5, 10, 15, 20 KVA.

Example (operating theatre, Lahore):

  • Anaesthesia machine: 800 W
  • Surgical light (LED): 500 W
  • Patient monitor × 2: 300 W
  • Diathermy unit: 400 W (intermittent — use 50% demand)
  • Total demand: 800 + 500 + 300 + 200 = 1,800 W
  • KVA at PF 0.8: 1,800 ÷ (0.8 × 1000) = 2.25 KVA
  • 45 °C derating (Lahore summer): 2.25 ÷ 0.88 = 2.56 KVA
  • Select: 3.15 KVA medical isolation transformer

Three-Phase Sizing (CNC Panel / Industrial)

  1. Sum the rated kW of all 3-phase loads (motors, VFDs, servo drives).
  2. Apply demand factor: 0.75 for typical mixed CNC machine tool load.
  3. Divide by system power factor (typically 0.85 for VFD loads with line reactor).
  4. Multiply by 1.5 for harmonic current demand (K-13 load profile).
  5. Apply ambient derating.
  6. Select next standard size: 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 63, 75, 100 KVA (3-phase).

13. Installation & Earthing Requirements

Critical Earthing Rules for Isolation Transformers in Pakistan

Most Common Pakistan Mistake: Connecting the secondary neutral to the same earth bar as the WAPDA neutral. This defeats the entire purpose of the isolation transformer — the WAPDA common-mode noise and neutral-to-earth voltage flows straight through to your equipment. The secondary neutral MUST be earthed at a separate, dedicated electrode.
  • Primary side: Standard WAPDA earth connection (connect to existing earth bar in incoming panel). Install SPD Type 2 on primary terminals — see our SPD guide.
  • Faraday shield terminal: Run a dedicated 4 mm² green-yellow wire to a clean earth electrode that is NOT shared with the WAPDA system neutral. Minimum 2 m separation from WAPDA earth electrode.
  • Secondary neutral: Earth at one point only — the secondary distribution board earth bar, connected to the same clean earth electrode as the Faraday shield.
  • Secondary PE: All equipment chassis PE connects to the secondary earth bar.
  • Earth electrode specification: See our Earthing System Design Pakistan 2026 guide for proper electrode sizing and soil treatment.

Installation Location and Ventilation

  • Mount on a vibration-damping rubber pad (50 Shore A) — dry-type transformers transmit 50 Hz hum through floor and walls
  • Minimum 200 mm clearance on all sides for natural convection; 400 mm above for forced-air exhaust
  • For non-IP-rated (open ventilated) units: keep away from metalworking dust, coolant mist, and condensation risk areas
  • Temperature monitor: thermistor winding temperature probe recommended for K-rated units >20 KVA

14. Frequently Asked Questions — Pakistan Buyers

Q: Does an isolation transformer regulate voltage?

No. A 1:1 isolation transformer passes whatever voltage WAPDA supplies. If WAPDA drops to 190 V, the secondary delivers 190 V (minus the small resistance drop). For voltage regulation, place a servo stabilizer upstream of the isolation transformer.

Q: Can I use an isolation transformer with my solar inverter?

Only on the WAPDA (grid) input side before the inverter, not on the inverter output. Modern transformerless solar inverters already provide isolation. Installing an isolation transformer on the inverter output creates unwanted earth loops that can trip the inverter's GFDI (Ground Fault Detection Interrupter).

Q: What happens to the first earth fault on an isolation transformer secondary?

With a properly installed IT system (medical) with IMD: an alarm sounds, power continues, and the fault must be found and corrected before a second fault occurs. In a standard industrial installation without IMD, a single line-to-earth fault on the secondary draws only capacitive leakage current (5–30 mA) — the equipment keeps running but the fault should be found promptly.

Q: Why does my isolation transformer run warm / smell of varnish when new?

Varnish cure-off smell for the first 50–100 hours of operation is normal. If a transformer runs hot (>60 °C case surface) at rated load in 40 °C ambient, it is either undersized, the K-factor rating is inadequate for the actual load, or ventilation is blocked. Check all three before assuming a defect.

Q: Can I mount an isolation transformer outside in Pakistan?

Only with IP65 weatherproof enclosure, canopy shelter from direct monsoon rain, and Class H insulation for temperature range. Standard IP54 units are suitable for covered outdoor (under canopy) but not direct rain exposure. All terminal connections must be made in a weatherproof way.

Q: What current transformer should I use for metering an isolation transformer?

See our Current Transformer CT Price Pakistan 2026 guide. Use a Class 0.5S metering CT on the primary (WAPDA) side for energy billing, and a Class 1 protection CT on the secondary for overcurrent relay.
