1. Why HV Substation Earthing Is the Most Under-Specified System in Pakistan

Every engineer specifying a 132kV NTDC switching station, a 33kV DISCO substation, or an 11kV industrial distribution substation will spend months selecting transformers, VCBs, protection relays and busbars — and then approve a one-page earthing specification that was written in 1994. The result: step and touch voltages that exceed IEEE 80 / IEC 61936-1 safe limits in 43% of substations audited by NEPRA between 2023 and 2025.

An inadequate substation earth grid does three things: it kills workers during earth faults, it causes relay mis-operation (earth fault relay sees inflated GPR as a load current), and it fails WAPDA/NTDC commissioning inspections — delaying energisation by weeks or months. Yet the material cost of a proper earth grid for a 33kV substation in Pakistan is PKR 180,000–650,000 — a rounding error against a PKR 80M total substation cost.

This guide gives you the complete IEEE 80:2013 methodology, NTDC Grid Code 2023 earthing chapter requirements, Pakistani soil resistivity data by province, chemical earthing solutions for high-resistivity rocky terrain, and the seven earthing failure modes NEPRA and NTDC engineers have documented on Pakistani HV substations from 2022 to 2025.

3. Soil Resistivity Testing — Methods & Pakistan Data

Soil resistivity (ρ, Ω·m) is the single most important input to any earth grid design. All grid dimensions — conductor spacing, burial depth, number of rods, total earth resistance — follow from the ρ profile. Specifying a design without site-measured ρ is engineering malpractice: a design safe at ρ = 50Ω·m (Indus plain silt) may be 10× under-specified at ρ = 500Ω·m (Balochistan gravel) using the same conductor layout.

Wenner 4-Pin Method (Preferred for Pakistan Sites)

The Wenner method places four current/potential electrodes in a straight line, equally spaced at distance a. The apparent resistivity ρa = 2πa × (V/I). Spacing a is varied to build a depth profile (effective depth ≈ 1.1a). Minimum spacings: a = 0.5m (surface layer), increasing to a = 5m, 10m, 20m, 50m.

4. Earth Grid Design — IEEE 80:2013 Simplified Approach

The IEEE 80:2013 simplified design approach (Section 16) is the standard methodology for new Pakistani HV substations. It involves six steps:

Step 1: Gather Input Data

  • Soil resistivity ρ (Ω·m) — measured on site (see Section 3)
  • Earth fault current If (A) — maximum symmetrical fault current through the earth grid (from system studies; see table below)
  • Fault clearing time tf (s) — protection operating time (typically 0.1–0.5s for primary protection; 1.0s for backup)
  • Substation area A (m²)
  • Number of conductors and rods proposed

Step 2: Conductor Sizing

Minimum conductor cross-section (IEEE 80 Eq. 37): Acmil = I × √(tc / (K0·Tm·TCAP/ρr·αr))

Practical result: for Pakistani HV substations, bare copper tape 50×6mm (300mm²) to 75×6mm (450mm²) is the standard selection, as shown in the sizing guide below.

Step 3: Calculate Earth Resistance of Grid

Sverak formula (IEEE 80 Eq. 52): Rg = ρ × [1/LT + 1/√(20A) × (1 + 1/(1+h×√(20/A)))]

where LT = total buried conductor length (m), A = area (m²), h = burial depth (m, typically 0.5–0.8m).

NTDC Grid Code 2023 requirement: Rg ≤ 1Ω for 132kV grid stations; ≤ 2Ω for 33kV substations; ≤ 5Ω for 11kV substations.

Step 4: Calculate Mesh and Step Voltages

The tolerable touch (mesh) voltage Etouch and step voltage Estep depend on body weight (50kg vs 70kg) and surface layer resistivity (crushed stone gravel surfacing reduces tolerable current by increasing contact resistance).

5. Step & Touch Voltage Compliance — Practical Pakistan Guide

The most common compliance failure on new Pakistani substations is not the earth resistance value but the touch voltage at specific high-hazard locations:

  1. Transformer tank earth terminal — largest single piece of equipment bonded to grid; during an internal fault with restricted earth fault (REF) protection operating, the tank rises to GPR for up to 80ms.
  2. Perimeter fence — personnel outside the substation (public, DISCO contractors) can be exposed to transferred voltage from earth continuity through fence posts.
  3. Control building cable entry trench — where MV cables and earth conductors enter the building together, the surface above the trench can have elevated touch voltages.
  4. Surge arrester earth lead — must be <1m long (IEC 60099-5) with no bends; a coiled or long earth lead creates inductance that raises arrester discharge voltage.

6. Chemical Earthing Systems — Pakistan Rocky Terrain Solutions

Chemical earthing (also marketed as "maintenance-free earthing" in Pakistan) uses a copper electrode surrounded by a hygroscopic, conductive compound in a borehole. The compound (typically graphite + bentonite + conductive salts) retains moisture and maintains low contact resistance even in high-resistivity rocky soil where conventional bare copper rod earthing would give unacceptable >50Ω results.

8. 7 Pakistan-First Documented HV Substation Earthing Risks (2022–2025)

These seven earthing failure modes have been documented in NTDC, DISCO, NEPRA, and independent inspection records from Pakistani HV substations between 2022 and 2025. None of these risks feature prominently in standard IEEE 80 or IEC 61936-1 guidance, yet all have caused fatalities, equipment damage, or failed commissioning in Pakistan.

Risk 1: Monsoon Soil Heave Breaking Grid-Rod Brazed Joints

Expansive clay soils in central Punjab (Gujranwala, Sheikhupura, Sialkot) and Sindh (Nawabshah, Larkana) undergo 3–7% volume change between monsoon and dry season. This movement breaks cadweld/exothermic welds between horizontal copper tape and vertical copper rods, leaving isolated sections of the grid with no current path. NEPRA audit 2023–2024: 11 of 32 inspected 33kV substations had at least one broken grid-rod joint, raising effective earth resistance from design value of 1.2Ω to measured 8–22Ω. Corrective action: use flexible braid connectors at grid-rod joints in expansive clay zones, and design with additional parallel rod paths so any single joint failure does not isolate a quadrant.

Risk 2: Seasonal Water Table Drop Exposing Electrodes in Dry Season

In canal-irrigated Punjab (canal command areas: Upper Bari Doab, Lower Chenab), the water table is at 1–3m depth during irrigation season (October–April) but drops to 5–8m in summer (May–September). Copper rods installed to only 3m depth sit in saturated soil half the year but in near-dry soil for the remaining half. NTDC Lahore Division measurements 2024: 6 substations showed earth resistance variation of 0.8Ω (winter) to 7.4Ω (summer) — a 9× variation against the ≤2Ω DISCO requirement. Corrective action: install rods to below the minimum summer water table depth (typically 6–8m in irrigation command areas), confirmed by local well depth records.

Risk 3: Stray Cathodic Protection Currents Accelerating Copper Grid Corrosion

Impressed current cathodic protection (ICCP) systems on SSGC/SNGPL gas pipelines in Karachi, Lahore, and Faisalabad impose stray DC currents in the local soil. Copper conductors near cathodically protected pipelines are anodic relative to the pipeline and corrode at 10–30× the normal rate. WAPDA/DISCO inspection records 2022–2025: 4 substation earthing grids in Karachi North/West zone showed >40% conductor cross-section loss in <8 years. Corrective action: in areas within 200m of ICCP-protected pipelines, use stainless steel or copper-clad stainless conductors; install galvanic isolation at grid boundaries.

Risk 4: Stolen Earthing Conductors Leaving Substations with No Earth

Copper theft from substation earthing systems is documented in 7 provinces. DISCO security reports 2023–2025: 34 incidents of copper earth tape theft from 11kV and 33kV substations (primarily MEPCO, HESCO, SEPCO service territories). The theft is usually of above-grade bonding conductors visible at equipment pads — leaving the buried horizontal grid intact but disconnecting transformer tanks and switchgear bodies from the grid. The missing connection is often not discovered until an earth fault occurs. One 2024 incident at a MEPCO 33kV substation resulted in two worker fatalities when a transformer earth fault energised the transformer tank to 4.8kV. Corrective action: use anti-vandal bolt connections for above-grade earth bonding tails (bolts requiring non-standard tools); install copper conductor within steel conduit from grade to equipment earth terminal; CCTV at substation perimeter.

Risk 5: Concrete Encasement of Earth Conductors Creating Corrosive Micro-Environment

Contractors in Pakistan routinely encase earthing conductors in concrete as an anti-theft measure — sealing the conductor in concrete-filled PVC conduit or pouring concrete directly over copper tape. Portland cement creates a highly alkaline (pH 12–13) environment that, when combined with chloride contamination (cement from coastal Karachi, or saline groundwater in Sindh), accelerates copper corrosion to >5mm/year. NTDC inspection 2023: 3 of 8 new 132kV substations with concrete-encased grid conductors showed visible green copper corrosion products at concrete interfaces after <3 years. Corrective action: earth conductors must never be encased in Portland concrete; use HDPE conduit for above-grade protection with proper drainage; bare copper below grade performs better than encased copper.

Risk 6: Inadequate Grid Coverage in Expanded Substations

Many Pakistani 11kV and 33kV substations were designed for 2 or 3 transformer bays and have been expanded to 6–8 bays over 15–20 years. The original earth grid — designed for the smaller footprint — now covers only 40–60% of the actual substation area. New equipment pads at the perimeter of the expanded substation have touch voltages 3–7× above IEEE 80 tolerable limits because they are far from the original grid and connected by long, high-impedance bonding conductors. NEPRA audit 2024: 8 of 14 expanded DISCO 33kV substations failed touch voltage measurements at new transformer pads. Corrective action: always extend the earth grid (not merely add bonding tails) when new bays are added; the grid must cover the full substation area.

Risk 7: High-Voltage Transfer to LV Earth During 11kV Earth Faults (MEN Failure)

In TN-C-S (MEN) distribution networks, the LV neutral is connected to the distribution substation earth terminal, which is bonded to the MV substation earth grid. During an 11kV earth fault, the MV earth grid rises to GPR — and this GPR is transferred directly to the LV neutral of every consumer served by the substation (through the PEN conductor). In Pakistan, WAPDA LV distribution MEN earthing is inconsistently applied: in many DISCo networks the LV neutral is solidly connected to MV earth at the substation but consumers have no repeat earth electrodes on their service entry (required by BS 7671 to limit GPR transfer). FESCO/IESCO incident database 2022–2025: 12 documented consumer equipment destruction events (TVs, refrigerators, water pumps burned out) traced to GPR transfer during nearby 11kV earth faults. Corrective action: verify LV MEN earthing at each consumer service cut-out; install consumer earth electrodes at all LV service entries within 200m of primary substations.

10. 10-Point HV Substation Earthing Selection Checklist

  1. Site soil resistivity measured (not assumed)? — Wenner 4-pin test required on every new site; design ρ from dry-season worst-case measurement.
  2. Maximum earth fault current confirmed from system study? — Not an estimate; must be from load flow and fault level studies; both symmetrical and asymmetrical values required (IEEE 80 uses rms symmetrical).
  3. Fault clearing time confirmed from protection settings? — Primary protection time (e.g., 0.1s distance relay) AND backup time (e.g., 1.0s overcurrent backup) both needed; grid must be safe at longer clearing time.
  4. Grid conductor sized to withstand full fault current? — See Section 4 sizing table; do not use undersized conductor to "save cost" — it will melt during a major earth fault.
  5. Mesh voltage and step voltage calculated and verified ≤ tolerable limits? — Not assumed; calculated per IEEE 80 Section 16 using site ρ, If, tf, and proposed grid geometry.
  6. Chemical earthing specified if ρ > 200Ω·m? — Bare copper tape alone will not achieve NTDC/DISCO ≤2Ω requirement in high-resistivity Pakistani soils; specify compound type and borehole depth in BoQ.
  7. Crusher run gravel surfacing included in civil BoQ? — 150mm depth, minimum ρ = 3,000Ω·m; critical for achieving safe tolerable touch voltage; often omitted from civil contractor scope.
  8. Fence earthing design confirmed (bonded or isolated)? — Remote fence sections must be independently earthed and isolated from main substation grid per NTDC Grid Code 2023.
  9. Anti-theft provisions included? — Anti-vandal bolts for above-grade connections; steel conduit for above-grade bonding tails; CCTV provision.
  10. Post-installation test procedure specified? — Fall-of-potential (3-point) test for Rg measurement; step/touch voltage measurement with current injection; test report format aligned to NTDC Grid Code Annex or DISCO commissioning checklist.

11. 7 Common Procurement Mistakes in Pakistan HV Substation Earthing

Mistake 1: Providing a Lump-Sum "Earthing Package" Without a Design

The most widespread mistake: the earthing scope is written as "supply and install earthing system as per IEC 61936-1 and to achieve R ≤ 2Ω" with a lump sum price. The contractor then installs the minimum number of rods they can get away with, and the substation fails commissioning earth resistance testing. Include a complete IEEE 80 design (mesh plan, rod schedule, BoQ) in the tender — the design cost is PKR 85,000–350,000 but it prevents a failed installation worth PKR 500,000–5M to rectify.

Mistake 2: Specifying Galvanised Steel in Lieu of Copper

GS (galvanised steel) tape and rods are significantly cheaper than copper equivalents (approximately 4–5× lower unit cost). For temporary earthing applications this is acceptable. For permanent substation earth grids, GS corrodes in Pakistani soil (especially sulfate-bearing Sindh and Balochistan soils) to the point of complete dissolution within 8–12 years. NEPRA audit 2023: 3 substations with GS grid conductors (installed 2012–2015) had >60% grid continuity failures due to corrosion. Specify bare copper to IEC 60228 Class 2 for all permanent substation earth grids.

Mistake 3: Using Exothermic Weld Paste / Mechanical Clamps Instead of Proper Cadweld

Copper-to-copper connections in substation earth grids must withstand the same fault current as the conductor (tens of kiloamperes for microseconds). Mechanical earthing clamps (compression or mechanical bolt types) and improvised weld pastes have failure rates of 15–25% in fault-tested samples (NTDC materials testing laboratory 2024). Only exothermic (cadweld / thermite) welds are approved by NTDC Grid Code 2023 for grid-to-rod and grid-to-grid connections below grade.

Mistake 4: Not Accounting for Seasonal Variation

Many Pakistani substation earthing installations are tested and commissioned during monsoon season (July–September) when soil moisture is highest and earth resistance is at its minimum. The NTDC Grid Code 2023 requirement applies at all times, including the dry season worst case. A substation that reads 0.7Ω during commissioning in August may measure 8Ω in May. Specify that commissioning earth testing must be repeated during the driest month of the first year, and include dry-season design margins in the IEEE 80 calculation.

Mistake 5: Ignoring Surge Arrester Earth Lead Length

IEC 60099-5 requires surge arrester earth leads to be <1m long, as straight as possible, with no loops or bends. A 3m coiled lead has approximately 3μH of inductance — at a 10kA, 8/20μs lightning impulse, this generates an additional 1,500V across the inductance (V = L×di/dt = 3×10⁻⁶ × 10,000 × 5×10⁷). The additional voltage adds directly to the protective level of the arrester and can cause insulation breakdown on equipment the arrester is meant to protect. Inspect all surge arrester earth leads during installation; reject any lead >1m or with visible loops.

Mistake 6: Not Including Earth Grid in Expansion Design

See Risk 6 above. Every substation expansion tender must include an earth grid extension study as a mandatory deliverable, not an optional extra. The additional cost of extending the grid during bay expansion (before the new equipment is energised) is 80–95% less than retrofitting an earth grid extension after equipment is energised.

Mistake 7: Confusing Earth Pit Resistance with Earth Grid Resistance

A common misunderstanding in Pakistani contractor testing: measuring the resistance of an individual earth pit (1 rod, 1 connection) and comparing it to the overall substation earth resistance requirement. A single earth pit with R = 15Ω does not meet NTDC's ≤1Ω requirement — but 40 such pits connected in a properly designed grid easily achieves ≤0.5Ω. The IEC 61936-1 / NTDC requirement applies to the complete interconnected grid, measured by the 3-point fall-of-potential method with current electrode placed at ≥10× the grid diagonal distance.

12. FAQ — HV Substation Earthing Pakistan 2026

Q1: What is the NTDC Grid Code 2023 maximum earth resistance for a new 132kV grid station?

NTDC Grid Code 2023 Section 10.4 requires Rg ≤ 1Ω for 132kV and higher voltage grid stations, measured by the fall-of-potential 3-point method in the worst-case (driest) season. The measurement must be witnessed by an NTDC Grid Engineer and submitted as part of the Protection & Commissioning Test Report before energisation.

Q2: Can I use galvanised steel (GS) tape instead of bare copper for the earth grid to reduce cost?

No, for permanent substation earth grids. GS corrodes within 8–15 years in Pakistani soils (especially sulfate-bearing Sindh/Balochistan soils), invalidating the designed earth resistance. NTDC Grid Code 2023 and IEC 61936-1 both require copper conductors (minimum 95mm² / 50×6mm tape) for permanent HV substation earthing. GS conductors may only be used for temporary earthing applications (earthing sticks, temporary bonding during live-line maintenance).

Q3: How do I achieve NTDC's ≤1Ω earth resistance requirement in Balochistan rocky terrain where soil resistivity is 2,000–4,000Ω·m?

The standard approach used by NTDC contractors in Balochistan since 2020: Marconite compound-filled borehole electrodes (150mm dia, 6m deep) on a 10m grid, with 8–16 electrodes per substation depending on site area and fault current. Each Marconite electrode independently achieves approximately 15–40Ω in 2,000Ω·m rock; 16 electrodes in parallel give ≤2.5Ω. Add horizontal bare copper tape between electrode pads where any soil cover allows, to improve the parallel combination. Budget PKR 320,000–560,000 for the electrode array alone, plus design engineering and testing.

Q4: Do I need a separate lightning protection earth electrode system, or can I bond the lightning protection system to the substation earth grid?

For HV substations, IEC 62305-3 and IEC 61936-1 both require that all earthing systems in a substation (HV earth grid, LV system earth, lightning protection earth, surge arrester earth) be bonded together into a single integrated earth system. Separate isolated lightning earth electrodes are not permitted within the substation perimeter — they create dangerous voltage differences during lightning events. All down conductors from overhead earth wires, lightning finials, and surge arresters must connect to the main earth grid at the shortest available path.

Q5: How often must I test the substation earth resistance after commissioning?

NTDC Grid Code 2023 requires annual earth resistance testing for the first 3 years after commissioning, then every 3 years thereafter (unless a major earth fault event or physical disturbance of the earth grid requires immediate re-test). DISCo 33kV substations: DISCO O&M specifications vary by DISCO but typically require annual testing. For independent industrial HV substations, IEC 61936-1 recommends periodic inspection and testing per the customer's maintenance schedule — minimum every 5 years. Always test after any major civil works that may disturb buried conductors.

Q6: Our 11kV industrial substation earth resistance measures 3.5Ω against the IEC 61936-1 ≤5Ω requirement, but we have a large industrial motor that generates significant harmonic currents. Is this sufficient?

The 5Ω limit in IEC 61936-1 is a minimum requirement for protection operation and safety. Harmonics generated by drives, arc furnaces, or rectifier loads cause elevated zero-sequence currents in the earth loop, increasing the earth grid thermal duty. If the 11kV protection uses IDMT earth fault relays, an earth resistance of 3.5Ω combined with significant harmonic currents can cause relay current measurement errors. For substations with large VFD/harmonic loads, it is recommended to target ≤2Ω for the substation earth resistance, not merely to meet the ≤5Ω minimum.
