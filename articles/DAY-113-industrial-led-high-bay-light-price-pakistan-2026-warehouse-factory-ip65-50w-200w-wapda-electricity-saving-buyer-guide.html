7 Pakistan-Specific Factors That Change Your High Bay Selection

1. WAPDA Load-Shedding Drives Backup Sizing — LED's Lower Wattage Cuts Generator Cost

Pakistan's summer 2026 load-shedding schedule runs 8–12 hours per day for industrial C2/D2 consumers outside major cities (NEPRA Grid Code Schedule 6). A warehouse running 100×400W HPSV fittings needs a 50–60 kVA generator for lighting alone. The same illuminance delivered by 100×150W LED fittings needs only a 20–25 kVA generator—reducing generator capital cost by 40–50% (PKR 450,000–800,000 saving on a Cummins/Perkins set), plus daily diesel savings at PKR 95–185/kWh generation cost. Specify LED for all new warehouse projects where generator backup is required; the payback stacks generator capex, diesel, and tariff savings simultaneously.

2. Pakistan 45–50°C Ambient — Driver Thermal Derating Is Mandatory

Most LED driver datasheets specify Ta (ambient) = 40°C for rated output. Pakistani industrial rooftop/ceiling temperatures in May–August reach 45–52°C in Lahore, Multan, and Faisalabad (PMD 2023 rooftop thermal surveys). Without explicit 50°C derating verification, an LED driver running at Ta=50°C will reduce its output by 10–20% and cut its rated lifespan (L70) from 50,000 hours to 30,000–35,000 hours. Always request the driver's derating curve from the supplier and confirm the L70 at Ta=50°C. Drivers rated to Ta=65°C (e.g., Meanwell HLG series with IP67 rating) are mandatory for suspended bay luminaires in non-air-conditioned Pakistan warehouses.

3. Karachi Coastal Corrosion — IP65 Is the Minimum; IP66 + Marine Grade Aluminium Required Inland Sea Wind Zones

Karachi's saline sea-wind corridor (DHA–Clifton–SITE) introduces chloride deposition rates of 80–120 mg/m²/day (SITE Association corrosion data 2022). Standard IP20 industrial high bay fittings fail within 6–9 months in these zones: driver PCB corrosion, housing pitting, and LED module oxidation. Minimum specification for Karachi sites within 5 km of the sea: IP66 rating (directional water jet proof), ADC12 die-cast aluminium housing with Class 3 powder coat (minimum 60 µm), conformal-coated LED driver PCB, and stainless-steel mounting hardware. Verify IP rating is IEC 60529 certified (not just marked), and confirm the driver enclosure IP is stated separately from the optical compartment—many fittings have IP65 optics but IP44 drivers.

5. Faisalabad Textile Mill Lux Requirements — Warp Breakage Inspection Standard

Faisalabad's ring-spinning and weaving mills (which account for 40–45% of Pakistan's textile export capacity) face a specific photometric requirement not always understood by general electrical contractors. The Occupational Safety & Health Ordinance (Pakistan, 1966) Clause 17 sets minimum 150 lux for general factory areas, but ILO recommendations (Lighting in the Workplace, 2002) adopted by Punjab Labor Department circulars 2019 specify 500 lux minimum on the working plane for warp breakage detection in weaving departments. Uniform illumination (UO ≥ 0.6) is equally critical—dark zones cause inspectors to miss warp ends, increasing fabric defect rates. LED high bays with secondary optical redistribution (bi-optic or anti-glare diffuser) achieve UO 0.7–0.85 vs conventional HPSV at 0.35–0.45. Specify: minimum 500 lux at 1m above floor, UO ≥ 0.6, CRI ≥ 80 for accurate colour grading of fabric lots.

6. Halal Food Processing — CRI, Flicker Index & Colour Temperature Requirements

Pakistan's Halal Food Authority (PNHAC) requires food processing facilities to comply with GMP lighting standards. Camera-based quality inspection lines (FMCG, poultry processing, bakery packaging) require: (a) Colour rendering index CRI ≥ 90 to detect discolouration defects, (b) Colour temperature 4000–5000K neutral white to match inspector expectations of product colour, and (c) Flicker index <0.1 and flicker percentage <10% at 100 Hz to prevent strobe effect on high-speed conveyor cameras (typically 30–120 fps). Standard LED drivers with basic PWM dimming produce flicker index 0.3–0.5 at 100 Hz—failing camera inspection lines. Specify LED fittings with flicker-free drivers certified to IEEE PAR 1789-2015 (or equivalent EN 61547 flicker annex) for all food inspection environments. Note: NEPRA 230V ±10% voltage variation in Pakistan can affect PWM dimming frequency—verify driver input range 176–264V.

7. PSQCA PS 4778:2022 Certification — Pakistan's LED Performance Standard

PSQCA notified PS 4778:2022 (LED luminaires for general lighting purposes) in March 2022, requiring mandatory certification for LED luminaires above 50W sold in Pakistan. The standard mandates LM-80 lumen maintenance data, independent photometric testing to IES LM-79, and minimum initial luminous efficacy ≥100 lm/W for LED high bays. As of 2026, enforcement through SRO 991(I)/2022 applies to locally manufactured and imported fittings. Sourcing fittings without PS 4778 certification exposes buyers to SRO fines of PKR 50,000–500,000 per import consignment under PSQCA Act 2009. Insist on a PSQCA Product Certification Mark (PCM) on the carton and verify on the PSQCA online registry before placing bulk orders.

7 Common Mistakes When Buying LED High Bays in Pakistan — and Their PKR Cost

  1. Buying economy-grade to save upfront (PKR 1,500–4,000 per fitting cheaper): Economy drivers fail in 12–18 months at Pakistan temperatures. Replacement cost including labour in a 10m-high warehouse (boom lift hire PKR 8,000–15,000/day) makes the "saving" a 5–8× net loss. Always calculate total cost of ownership over 5 years.
  2. Matching wattage to old lamp wattage (1:1 swap): HPSV 400W → LED 400W is gross over-specification. Correct equivalent is LED 150–180W. Over-specification wastes PKR 3,000–7,000 extra per fitting with no benefit.
  3. Ignoring driver input voltage range: WAPDA supply dips to 185V in summer peak. A 220V±10% driver cuts out at 198V. Fit 176–264V range drivers or add a servo stabilizer (PKR 35,000–80,000) per circuit.
  4. No photometric simulation before bulk order: Over- or under-ordering by 20–30% fittings is common without DIALux simulation. 30 excess fittings at PKR 15,000 each = PKR 450,000 waste.
  5. Single-source import without Pakistan warranty support: LED driver failures need matching driver replacement. A China-direct fitting with no local stock requires 6–8 week import; warehouse in darkness costs PKR 50,000–500,000/day in lost productivity.
  6. No surge protection on LED circuits: Pakistan WAPDA overhead LT line switching transients 2–8kV destroy unprotected LED drivers. Install SPD Type 2 on every LED DB/feeder. SPD cost: PKR 3,500–8,000 per 3-phase panel — negligible vs driver replacement cost.
  7. Specifying DALI dimming without verifying WAPDA supply compatibility: DALI control gear requires clean 230V supply. On WAPDA feeders with 8–18% THDv (Faisalabad/Karachi industrial zones), DALI commissioning failures are common. Use 0–10V analogue dimming as the more robust Pakistan option.
