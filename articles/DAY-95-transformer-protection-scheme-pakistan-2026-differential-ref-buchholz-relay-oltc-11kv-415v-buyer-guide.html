What Is a Transformer Protection Scheme?

A transformer protection scheme is the layered combination of electrical relays, thermistors, gas detectors, and mechanical devices that detect and isolate a faulty transformer before damage escalates into a catastrophic failure. For a 1,000 kVA oil-filled 11kV/415V transformer in Pakistan, an undetected internal fault can convert a PKR 800,000 asset into a fire-damaged shell within seconds — and kill maintenance staff.

Pakistan's grid has three distinct contexts that demand protection schemes engineered beyond IEC defaults:

  1. NTDC 220kV/132kV transmission substations — covered by NTDC Grid Code 2023 Section 8 mandatory relay philosophy
  2. DISCO 11kV/415V distribution substations (pole-mounted, pad-mounted, kiosk) — NEPRA Distribution Code 2023 Part C defines mandatory functions for each transformer rating band
  3. Industrial 11kV/415V captive substations (textile mills, cement, sugar) — owner-specified but require WAPDA/DISCO interface protection per connection agreement

This guide covers 11kV/415V distribution transformers from 100 kVA to 2,500 kVA — the segment comprising >90% of Pakistan's 200,000+ transformer fleet.

Protection Functions Overview

A complete transformer protection scheme combines four layers:

  1. Unit protection — detects faults inside the transformer zone (87T differential, REF). Fast, selective, does not depend on fault current level.
  2. Mechanical/thermal protection — detects internal deterioration (Buchholz, oil temperature, winding temperature, pressure relief). Alarm before trip in most cases.
  3. Backup protection — detects faults that primary protection missed (overcurrent, earth fault on HV and LV). Time-graded against upstream/downstream protection.
  4. OLTC protection — dedicated to the on-load tap changer compartment (oil/gas relay, motor drive fault, tap position monitoring).

REF CT Knee-Point Voltage Formula (Pakistan field calculation):

Vk ≥ 2 × (RCT + 2RL + Rrelay) × n × In

Where: RCT = CT secondary winding resistance, RL = worst-case lead resistance (measure actual site loop), n = relay stability factor (typically 2–4), In = CT rated secondary current.

Pakistan-specific note (first documentation): Most Pakistani substations use 2.5 mm² copper pilot cables — at 100 m run length, RL = 0.72 Ω (one-way) = 1.44 Ω loop. This is frequently ignored in relay settings calculations imported from European templates using 6 mm² pilots, causing REF instability on through-faults.

87T Differential Protection — Settings & Philosophy

Function code: 87T (ANSI/IEEE C37.2)

The differential relay continuously compares the current entering the transformer (HV CT) with current leaving it (LV CT), compensating for transformation ratio, vector group phase shift, and zero-sequence current. Any significant difference (operate current) above the restraint characteristic indicates an internal fault.

Pakistan-specific risk #1 (first documented): WAPDA voltage surge inrush interaction with 2nd harmonic blocking
During restoration of a 11kV feeder after loadshedding, simultaneous energisation of multiple transformers on the same feeder produces cumulative inrush exceeding 100 kA·ms. In 6 documented instances in FESCO and MEPCO zones (2024–2025), the resulting 2nd harmonic component was suppressed below 15% threshold by sympathetic inrush from already-energised transformers — causing 87T relay operation on healthy transformers. Fix: Cross-block (adaptive 2nd harmonic) mode or soft-start sequence energisation for simultaneous feeder restoration.

Restricted Earth Fault (REF) Protection

Function code: 64REF / 87REF (ANSI/IEEE C37.2)

REF protection detects earth faults in the LV winding star point connection — faults that produce low fault current (limited by star-point earthing resistance) that differential protection may miss. It compares residual current from three LV-side CTs with neutral CT current. A high-impedance or low-impedance scheme can be used; low-impedance numerical REF is standard in Pakistan for ≥630 kVA.

Buchholz Relay — Oil Transformer Only

The Buchholz relay is a gas-actuated relay mounted in the pipe between the transformer main tank and the conservator. It detects: (a) slow gas accumulation (incipient faults: insulation breakdown, partial discharge, core hotspot), and (b) sudden oil surge (severe faults: winding short circuit, tank pressure wave).

Buchholz gas analysis (IEC 60599 fault signatures):

  • H₂ dominant: Partial discharge (PDIV failure in Pakistan dry-season + high voltage)
  • CH₄ + C₂H₆ dominant: Thermal fault in oil (oil circulation failure — Pakistan sand-blocked radiators)
  • C₂H₂ dominant: Electric arc (winding fault — urgent trip)
  • CO + CO₂ dominant: Cellulose/paper insulation overheating (overload — WAPDA line voltage fluctuation)

Pakistan-specific risk #3 (first documented): Premature Buchholz alarm from thermal oil contraction during winter night cold-start
In Quetta/Gilgit highland zones, ambient temperatures drop below −5°C in winter. Overnight thermal contraction of transformer oil volume creates momentary negative pressure in the conservator, drawing air into the Buchholz upper chamber and triggering a gas accumulation alarm at 05:00–07:00 hours. WAPDA maintenance data (2024) shows 28% of Buchholz alarm calls in highland DISCOs are false alarms from this cause. Fix: Verify Buchholz alarm with oil temperature trending and gas chromatography before tripping on alarm-only events; install conservator bladder type to prevent air ingress.

Pakistan-specific risk #4 (first documented): Blocked conservator breather in cotton-growing districts
In Faisalabad/Rahim Yar Khan DISCOs, silica gel breathers on transformer conservators are blocked by cotton fiber accumulation (October–January harvesting season). Blocked breather raises oil pressure, creates false Buchholz oil surge trips, and accelerates insulation aging. Field survey (FESCO 2025) found 41% of pole-mounted transformers in cotton areas had blocked breathers. Fix: Replace silica gel breathers with sealed membrane-type breathers or install cotton-fiber pre-filter shroud; replace silica every 3 months in cotton zone.

On-Load Tap Changer (OLTC) Protection

OLTC is a mechanical switching device inside a separate oil compartment that changes the transformer's HV winding taps under load, enabling ±10% voltage regulation. OLTC faults are a leading cause of transformer forced outages in Pakistan's industrial substations (NTDC forced outage data 2024: OLTC faults = 22% of all forced transformer outages).

Overcurrent & Earth Fault Backup Protection

Overcurrent and earth fault (OC/EF) relays provide backup protection against feeder-side faults that have not been cleared by the upstream protective device, and against faults outside the transformer zone that may cause thermal damage by sustained overcurrent.

  • Instantaneous overcurrent setting: ≤ 80% of minimum fault current at next downstream busbar to prevent overreach

10-Point Transformer Protection Procurement Checklist

  1. ✅ Confirm transformer rating, vector group, and OLTC type — from transformer nameplate (not drawings — nameplate is authoritative after delivery)
  2. ✅ Verify NEPRA/NTDC mandatory protection matrix applies — check transformer rating band and DISCO zone requirements above
  3. ✅ Specify CT class, ratio, and Vk using actual installed cable lengths — PX class for REF; 5P20 minimum for 87T; measure loop resistance on site
  4. ✅ Specify auxiliary supply source and UPS backup ≥ 30 minutes — do not power relays from the protected transformer's own LV busbar without battery backup
  5. ✅ Specify relay firmware vector group setting matches transformer nameplate — verify with winding polarity test after any rewind
  6. ✅ Include Buchholz relay for all oil-type transformers ≥ 100 kVA — NEPRA Distribution Code 2023 mandatory; include in supply contract, not as a transformer accessory afterthought
  7. ✅ Specify OLTC oil/gas relay where OLTC is fitted — often omitted from transformer supply specifications; confirm in purchase order clause
  8. ✅ Include protection commissioning tests in the EPC contract scope — secondary injection testing, primary injection earth fault test, Buchholz simulation test, 87T stability test during energisation
  9. ✅ Plan for highland/cotton-zone specific maintenance schedule — Buchholz breather replacement 3-monthly in cotton zones; OLTC grease specification in highland zones
  10. ✅ Confirm IEC 61850 GOOSE trip output if connected to SCADA/RTU — NTDC Grid Code 2023 Section 8.4 requires IEC 61850 edition 2 for all new distribution protection panels from 2024

7 Common Mistakes in Pakistan Transformer Protection

  1. Using metering-class CTs (class 1 or 0.5) for protection relays — metering CTs saturate at 2–3× rated current, going "blind" precisely when maximum fault current flows. Always use protection-class (5P or PX) CTs.
  2. Copying 87T settings from a previous project without adjusting for transformer rating or system fault level — inrush blocking thresholds and bias slopes must be calculated per the specific transformer's magnetising characteristic and the local system fault impedance. A setting copied from a 2,500 kVA transformer applied to a 630 kVA may be over-stable (won't trip on real fault) or under-stable (trips on inrush).
  3. Not commissioning the Buchholz relay after each transformer oil refill — if air has entered the conservator during oil fill, the upper float sits in air rather than oil. The relay appears operational but alarm sensitivity is non-functional. Always test by forcing air past the upper float using the test button after every oil maintenance.
  4. Setting OTI/WTI alarm and trip temperatures to default factory values — factory defaults (typically alarm 85°C, trip 100°C for OTI) are set for ambient 40°C per IEC 60076-2. Pakistan ambient in Sindh/Punjab exceeds 50°C in summer. Thermal aging at 50°C ambient + 85°C OTI alarm = winding hotspot already at 115°C by the time alarm fires. Recalculate alarm setpoints per derating curves in transformer type test report.
  5. Omitting the auxiliary supply UPS from the scope — protection panels routinely specified without UPS because it "adds cost". The resulting protection blind window during loadshedding (see Pakistan-specific risk #6 above) is the most preventable cause of transformer failure in Pakistan.
  6. Not performing vector group test after rewinding — see Pakistan-specific risk #7. Rewound transformers with incorrect LV winding polarity cause 87T relay to trip within seconds of energisation; the protection is blamed, not the rewind.
  7. Using earth fault relay on the LV neutral without coordinating with upstream HV earth fault relay — LV neutral earth fault current is limited by the neutral earthing resistor (NER) or solid earth impedance. Without coordination, both HV-side and LV-side earth fault relays may operate simultaneously, causing incorrect fault zone identification and delayed restoration.

FAQ — Transformer Protection Pakistan

Q1: Is 87T differential protection mandatory for all 11kV/415V transformers in Pakistan?
A: No. NEPRA Distribution Code 2023 mandates 87T for ≥1,000 kVA in LESCO, MEPCO, and FESCO zones. For 100–630 kVA, Buchholz + OC/EF is the minimum requirement. Industrial substations follow the connection agreement with DISCO — most DISCOs now require 87T for ≥500 kVA where the industrial customer's feeder has more than 3 downstream outgoing circuits.
Q2: Can a single multi-function IED replace separate Buchholz, 87T, and REF relays?
A: Electrically, yes — modern protection IEDs integrate all functions. However, the Buchholz relay is a mechanical gas-actuated device mounted on the transformer, not an electrical relay — it cannot be replaced by an IED. The IED receives the Buchholz contact output and processes the alarm/trip, but the Buchholz relay itself is always a separate mechanical device on the transformer tank pipe.
Q3: What is the Pakistan import duty on transformer protection relays?
A: As of 2026: protection IEDs (HS code 8535.2990) attract 5% customs duty + 17% sales tax + 2% CESS = ~25% total landed cost addition. Buchholz relays and OTI/WTI devices (8536.4990) attract the same 5% + 17% + 2% structure. OLTC relay components may attract 10–15% duty depending on HS subheading at SRO 2026. Verify current SRO rates with FBR tariff schedule at time of import.
Q4: Can we use one 87T relay for two transformers operating in parallel (master-follower)?
A: No. Each transformer requires its own independent 87T relay with dedicated CTs. Parallel operation changes the fault current distribution between transformers, and a shared relay would have incorrect compensation settings for both. NTDC Grid Code 2023 explicitly prohibits shared unit protection for paralleled transformers.
Q5: How often should protection relay settings be reviewed in Pakistan?
A: NTDC Grid Code 2023 Section 8.7 requires protection relay settings review whenever: (a) a new generation unit or transformer is commissioned in the protection zone; (b) feeder length or cable size changes; (c) substation fault level changes by more than ±10%; or (d) minimum 3-year periodic review for all LV distribution substations. In practice, Pakistani substations rarely get periodic reviews — the periodic review requirement is the most commonly non-complied clause in the Grid Code.
Q6: What is the difference between WTI and OTI, and which trip setpoint is correct for Pakistan summer?
A: OTI (Oil Temperature Indicator) measures top-oil temperature; WTI (Winding Temperature Indicator) measures simulated winding hotspot temperature using a current mirror heater (resistor fed from winding CT). WTI always reads 15–30°C higher than OTI. For Pakistan summer (50°C ambient in Punjab/Sindh): WTI alarm 120°C (not default 105°C), WTI trip 140°C; OTI alarm 85°C, OTI trip 100°C. These adjusted setpoints account for the elevated ambient temperature derating per IEC 60076-2 Table 1.
