What Is an OT SIEM — and Why Is It Different from IT SIEM?

A Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platform collects, normalises, correlates, and alerts on log and event data from across an infrastructure. IT SIEMs (Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel, IBM QRadar, LogRhythm) are designed for TCP/IP hosts: Windows servers, Active Directory, firewalls, email gateways. They understand RFC 5424 syslog, Windows Event Log, CEF/LEEF, and JSON over HTTPS.

OT devices speak different languages. A Siemens S7 PLC does not emit Windows Event Log entries. A WAPDA-connected protection relay sends GOOSE and SV messages over IEC 61850. A Modbus RTU field device has no IP stack at all. An OT SIEM must therefore include:

  • OT-aware protocol parsers: Modbus, DNP3, IEC 61850, EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, BACnet, IEC 60870-5-104
  • Passive network tap / span-port capture: OT devices cannot tolerate active scans; passive packet inspection is mandatory
  • Asset inventory engine: auto-discovers PLCs, HMIs, RTUs, historians from traffic (not from active probing)
  • Anomaly/behaviour baselines: flags when a PLC suddenly issues a firmware-write command outside of a maintenance window
  • IT/OT event correlation: links a suspicious AD login from IT with a simultaneous write to PLC tag in OT — the classic lateral-movement chain

The IEC 62443 Zone & Conduit Model and SIEM Placement

IEC 62443-3-3 defines Security Levels (SL 1–4) for Industrial Automation and Control Systems. In the Purdue Reference Model — which the standard uses as a reference architecture — a SIEM collector sits at the boundary between Level 2 (Control) and Level 3 (Operations), with a forwarding agent at the DMZ that feeds normalised events up to the enterprise SIEM at Level 4 (Business).

If you shipped an OT firewall with IEC 62443 zone segmentation and a Zero-Trust VPN for remote maintenance, your next architectural step is a SIEM that ingests events from both the OT firewall and the VPN gateway — plus direct passive taps on the OT network segments. Without the SIEM, those security controls generate logs nobody reads.

CERT-PK Incident Reporting — What OT Operators Must Know

CERT-PK (Computer Emergency Response Team Pakistan), operated under the Ministry of IT and Telecom, requires organisations operating Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) to report cybersecurity incidents within 24 hours of detection. The National Cyber Security Policy 2021 and the subsequent NCA Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) guidelines extend this obligation explicitly to SCADA/ICS environments in:

  • Power generation and distribution (WAPDA, NEPRA-licensed IPPs, DISCOs)
  • Water treatment and municipal utilities
  • Oil & gas processing and pipeline SCADA
  • Large-scale manufacturing (pharmaceutical GMP, textile export factories with EU/US buyer cyber-audits)
  • Telecom core networks

An OT SIEM enables CERT-PK compliance by providing the event timeline, affected asset list, and attack vector reconstruction that the NCA incident report template (NCA-IRT-001) requires. Without a SIEM, operators must reconstruct incidents from fragmented PLC alarm logs, historian CSV exports, and firewall ACL logs — a process that typically takes days, missing the 24-hour window.

CERT-PK Incident Reporting Workflow with OT SIEM

  1. Detection: SIEM correlation rule triggers (e.g., "PLC firmware write outside maintenance window" + "concurrent AD lateral movement alert")
  2. Triage (0–2 hrs): OT SOC analyst reviews asset context from SIEM asset inventory; confirms scope (which PLCs, which process line)
  3. Containment (2–6 hrs): Isolate affected OT network segment via firewall policy push (automated playbook or manual); preserve logs
  4. Initial notification to CERT-PK (≤24 hrs): Submit via cert.gov.pk portal; include: incident timestamp, affected systems, attack vector (preliminary), impact assessment, and containment action taken. SIEM provides all required fields.
  5. Full investigation & RCA: SIEM forensic export; NCA-IRT-001 final report within 72 hrs for Tier-1 CII organisations

Sizing an OT SIEM for Pakistani Industrial Sites

Key Sizing Parameters

  • Assets under management (AUM): Total count of PLCs + HMIs + RTUs + EWS + network devices. Most OT SIEM vendors license by AUM count.
  • Events per second (EPS): OT environments are low-EPS compared to IT — typically 50–500 EPS for a mid-size factory. Use 10% of asset count × 2 as a rough EPS estimate.
  • Log ingestion GB/day: Relevant for Splunk/Sentinel (ingestion-priced). Passive OT network capture is high-volume; use selective filtering to avoid unnecessary ingestion cost.
  • Retention period: NCA requires 12 months; NEECA energy audit logs should be retained 3 years; textile buyer compliance audits often require 5 years.

OT SOC Staffing — The Pakistan Challenge

The biggest barrier to OT SIEM adoption in Pakistan is not platform cost — it is staffing. An OT SOC analyst needs three competencies simultaneously: IT security (SIEM operation, threat hunting), OT/ICS engineering (understanding PLC ladder logic, process context), and Pakistani regulatory familiarity (CERT-PK, NCA, WAPDA SCADA standards). This combination is rare.

Practical staffing models for Pakistani manufacturers in 2026:

  • Shared SOC with an MSSP: A Managed Security Service Provider (MSSP) operating an OT-capable SOC (there are 3–4 in Pakistan as of 2026) provides 24×7 monitoring as a managed service. PKR 1.4M–5.6M/yr for remote SOC-as-a-service. SIEM platform may be included. Best for sites that cannot hire in-house OT analysts.
  • Hybrid (in-house L1 + MSSP L2/L3): In-house shift operators monitor SIEM dashboard during working hours; MSSP provides after-hours coverage and advanced investigation. Reduces MSSP cost to PKR 840K–2.8M/yr while building internal OT cyber capability.
  • In-house OT SOC: Viable only for large industrial groups (textile conglomerates, fertiliser plants, power utilities). Minimum viable team: 2× OT SOC analysts + 1× OT security architect. Training: SANS ICS515, Dragos Academy, or CISA ICS training programmes (online).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an OT SIEM mandatory in Pakistan for all manufacturers?

Not universally mandatory. CERT-PK and NCA reporting obligations apply to Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) operators — primarily power, water, oil & gas, large pharmaceutical, and telecom. However, textile and food manufacturers exporting to the EU or US face increasing cyber-audit requirements from buyers (EU NIS2 supply-chain provisions, CTPAT cyber requirements), making OT SIEM effectively mandatory for export-oriented factories.

Can I use Microsoft Sentinel as an OT SIEM in Pakistan?

Yes — Microsoft Sentinel combined with Microsoft Defender for IoT (OT sensor) is one of the most cost-effective OT SIEM options for sites already using Azure or Microsoft 365. The Defender for IoT sensor is deployed on-premises (passive tap), and events are forwarded to Sentinel via the cloud connector. Data residency: Sentinel can store logs in the UAE Azure region (closest to Pakistan, low latency). Approximate cost: PKR 1.4M–4.2M/yr depending on ingestion volume.

What is the minimum OT SIEM setup for a small Pakistani textile factory?

A minimum viable OT SIEM for a small factory (20–50 OT assets) in 2026: deploy Wazuh SIEM (open-source, free licence) on a local server or VPS; install Wazuh agents on all HMI and engineering workstation Windows PCs; set up a passive network tap on the OT LAN core switch and run Zeek with Modbus/DNP3 parsers to capture L0–L2 traffic; forward all events to Wazuh Indexer. Estimated total cost: PKR 280K–560K/yr (server hardware or VPS + integration labour). This achieves approximately 70% of commercial SIEM visibility at 10% of commercial SIEM cost.

How do I report a PLC ransomware incident to CERT-PK?

Report via cert.gov.pk (online portal) or email to cert@moitt.gov.pk within 24 hours of detection. Required fields: organisation name + sector, date/time of detection, affected systems (make/model of PLCs, SCADA version), attack vector (ransomware, lateral movement from IT), estimated impact (production downtime, data encrypted), and containment actions taken. CERT-PK will assign an incident ID and may deploy an ICS-CERT response team for Tier-1 CII incidents. Keep your SIEM evidence chain intact — do not wipe infected systems before forensic collection.

What is WAPDA's requirement for substation SIEM and cybersecurity monitoring?

WAPDA and NTDC substations with SCADA/EMS (Energy Management System) are classified as CII under Pakistan's National Cyber Security Policy 2021. NTDC's Grid Code (Revision 7, 2024 draft) references NCA ECC controls for SCADA systems. In practice, WAPDA DISCOs are in early stages of OT SIEM deployment as of 2026 — most are conducting SIEM proof-of-concept projects under USAID and GIZ power sector support programmes. Vendors active in the WAPDA OT security space include Dragos, Fortinet, and local MSSP partners.

How long does OT SIEM deployment take in a Pakistani factory?

A typical OT SIEM deployment for a medium-sized Pakistani manufacturer (50–150 OT assets) takes 8–16 weeks: 2 weeks for passive asset discovery and log source scoping; 2–4 weeks for SIEM platform deployment and log ingestion configuration; 2–4 weeks for correlation rule tuning and false-positive reduction; 2–4 weeks for SOC analyst training and playbook development. The critical path constraint in Pakistan is typically availability of certified OT-security integrators — there are fewer than 10 firms nationally with ICS/SCADA security deployment experience as of 2026.
