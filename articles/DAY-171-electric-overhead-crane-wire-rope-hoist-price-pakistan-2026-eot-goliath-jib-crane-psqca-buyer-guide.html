1. Why Every Pakistani Factory Needs to Know This

Pakistan's industrial heartland — the textile mills of Faisalabad, the steel rerolling units of Lahore's industrial estates, the cement plants of Cherat and Bestway, the sugar mills of Punjab, and the growing auto-assembly units of Karachi — all share one common material-handling challenge: moving heavy loads safely, repeatedly, and cost-effectively.

Electric overhead cranes and wire rope hoists are the workhorses of this effort. A single 10-tonne EOT crane can replace 15–20 manual workers per shift for heavy lifting, reduce workplace injuries by over 60%, and cut cycle times in steel fabrication, die shops, and transformer assembly from hours to minutes.

Yet crane procurement in Pakistan is plagued by three problems:

  1. Underpowered or wrong duty class — buying a light M2-duty crane for a steel plant that needs M5.
  2. Missing safety components — no emergency stop contactor, no overload limiter, no PSQCA inspection.
  3. Incorrect electrical installation — standard contactors used instead of crane-duty contactors, improper VFD selection for hoist motion, missing brake resistors.

This guide cuts through the confusion with verified PKR prices, correct component selection, and a 20-point commissioning checklist drawn from IEC 60204-32 (Electrical Equipment of Machines — Cranes), FEM 1.001, and PSQCA PS:4756.

2. Types of Electric Overhead Cranes

2.1 Electric Overhead Travelling (EOT) Crane — Single & Double Girder

The most common type in Pakistani industry. Bridges span across a bay; the crab (hoist trolley) traverses along the bridge; the whole assembly travels along runway rails mounted on columns.

  • Single-girder EOT: Capacity 0.5T–20T, span up to 20m. Hoist runs on the bottom flange of the bridge beam (underhung). Lower cost, restricted headroom.
  • Double-girder EOT: Capacity 5T–500T, span up to 35m. Hoist sits on top of two parallel bridge beams. Greater lift height, better headroom. Standard for Pakistani steel mills, cement plants, power stations.

2.2 Goliath Crane (Gantry Crane)

Runs on ground-level rails — no overhead runway structure required. Used in Pakistani shipyards (Karachi), precast concrete yards, power plant switchyards, and heavy fabrication shops. Capacities: 5T–500T.

2.3 Jib Crane (Wall-Mounted / Pillar-Mounted)

Fixed to a wall or floor pillar. Swing radius 2–8m. Capacities 0.25T–10T. Very popular in Pakistani die shops, maintenance bays, and machine tool areas where a full EOT crane is uneconomical.

2.4 Monorail Hoist System

Single I-beam rail; hoist travels in one axis only. Used in production lines, assembly areas, boiler rooms. Covered in detail in the Day 172 Chain Hoist guide.

2.5 Explosion-Proof Crane (Ex-rated)

Required in petroleum refineries, gas plants, petrochemical units, and grain storage facilities in Pakistan. All electrical components (motors, control panels, limit switches) must be rated Ex d (flameproof) or Ex e (increased safety) per IEC 60079. PKR premium over standard: 2.5–4×.

6. VFD Crane Control — Speed, Anti-Sway & Energy Saving

Standard contactors give two speeds (high/low) for crane motion. A Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) unlocks infinitely variable speed from 0–100%, with significant benefits for Pakistani industrial operations.

6.1 Crane-Specific VFD Requirements

Not every VFD is suitable for crane hoist motion. The hoist (vertical lifting) axis has unique demands that generic VFDs cannot meet safely:

  • Brake chopper (dynamic braking resistor): Mandatory for hoist. When lowering a load, the motor regenerates energy. Without a brake chopper and resistor, DC bus voltage spikes trip the VFD. Standard pump VFDs often lack a built-in chopper.
  • Load encoder feedback (closed-loop vector): Prevents load slip on power failure. The encoder reports motor speed; the VFD holds torque at standstill while the mechanical brake engages.
  • Anti-sway logic: Advanced crane VFDs include trajectory planning algorithms that ramp traverse speed to minimise pendulum swing of suspended loads — critical in steel slabs handling and coil storage.
  • Safe torque off (STO): IEC 61800-5-2 STO input for safety contactor-free emergency stop. Mandatory for SIL 2 crane safety circuits.
  • Mechanical brake release output: 24V DC relay output that energises the electromechanical brake solenoid synchronised with motor torque build-up. Prevents load drop on start.

7. PSQCA PS:4756 & Safety Requirements in Pakistan

PSQCA (Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority) PS:4756 is Pakistan's national standard for electric overhead travelling cranes. Key requirements:

  • Load testing: Static overload test at 125% SWL; dynamic test at 110% SWL before commissioning.
  • Limit switches: Upper and lower travel limit switches on hoist (mandatory). Long-travel and cross-travel buffer stop switches on EOT bridges.
  • Overload protection: Load cell or slip clutch preventing lifting > 110% SWL. Electronic overload limiter preferred for steel mills.
  • Emergency stop: Mushroom-head E-stop on pendant control AND at both bridge ends. Latching type per IEC 60947-5-5.
  • Earthing: Continuous earth bonding of all crane metalwork to building earth. Rail joints bonded. Runway conductors earthed.
  • Annual inspection: Under Pakistan Factory Act 1934, all lifting equipment in registered factories must be inspected by an approved examiner annually with Form 15 certification.

8. Crane Electrical: Contactors, Protection & Emergency Stop

The crane control panel is the electrical heart of every EOT crane. It must be engineered specifically for crane duty — standard motor starters are inadequate.

8.1 Crane-Duty Contactors vs Standard AC Contactors

Crane duty involves frequent starts/stops — typically 60–300+ operations/hour in steel plant applications. Standard AC-3 rated contactors (designed for 1,500,000 operations) are suitable only for light crane duty (M3). Heavy crane duty (M5+) requires:

  • AC-4 rated contactors (plugging and inching duty) — higher contact pressure, silver alloy contacts
  • IEC 60947-4-1 utilisation category AC-4
  • Minimum mechanical endurance: 10,000,000 operations
  • Oversized by 1.5–2× ampere rating vs motor FLA for crane duty

8.2 Motor Protection for Crane Duty

Standard thermal overload relays are marginally suitable for crane motors. For M5+ duty, use electronic motor protection relays with:

  • Class 20 or Class 30 trip curve (slower trip for high-inertia crane motors)
  • Phase-loss protection (critical — single phasing on hoist motor drops load)
  • Thermistor PTC input (motor winding temperature)
  • Memory function (retains thermal image through brief power cuts — common in Pakistan)

8.3 Emergency Stop Wiring (IEC 60204-32 Compliant)

The emergency stop circuit for a crane must meet IEC 60204-32 Category 1 stop (controlled stop, then power removed):

  • Pendant E-stop (NC contact) in series with master contactor coil circuit
  • End-of-travel limit switches in series with respective directional contactors
  • Safety relay monitoring the E-stop loop for contact welding
  • Visual indicator (pilot lamp) for safe-to-operate status on pendant
  • ESTOP reset only by deliberate twist-and-release action — never automatic

8.4 Cable Routing — Festoon vs Conductor Bar

Power and control cables to the travelling bridge must accommodate continuous movement. Use cable management systems appropriate for the crane type:

  • Festoon cables on C-track: H07RN-F 5-core 2.5mm² rubber cables, 10% extra length, minimum 300mm bend radius. PKR 185–420/metre installed.
  • Conductor bar (busbar system): 3-phase + earth copper/aluminium conductor in PVC housing. Current collector shoe picks up supply. Preferred for high-duty, long-span cranes. PKR 1,200–2,800/metre installed (4-conductor system).

9.2 Civil/Structural Requirements

  • Runway beam deflection: Maximum L/750 (vertical), L/1000 (lateral) under full crane load — per FEM 1.001.
  • Column corbels: Minimum M25 concrete with anchor bolts per structural engineer's design. No field-welded corbels without PE certification.
  • Rail joints: Maximum 1mm gap, lipped joint or welded. Rail ends drilled for 20mm bolts, fish-plated for conductor bar cranes.
  • End-of-runway buffers: Hydraulic shock absorbers for cranes > 10T or bridge speed > 60 m/min. Spring buffers for smaller/slower cranes.

10. Twenty-Point Commissioning Checklist

Complete every point before first load test. Record in the crane commissioning register (required for PSQCA and Factory Act certification).

  1. ✅ Structural inspection: Bridge girder, end carriages, hoist body — no visible cracks, weld defects, or deformation. NDT (magnetic particle or dye penetrant) on all principal welds for M5+ cranes.
  2. ✅ Wire rope inspection: Check for broken wires, kinks, corrosion, wear. Replace if >10% of wires broken in one lay length (per IEC 4309-1).
  3. ✅ Hook & safety latch: Hook throat opening ≤ 10% above nominal. Safety latch operational — load will not dislodge under vibration.
  4. ✅ Hoist drum: Rope wound neatly, minimum 2 dead turns retained on drum at lowest hook position. Fleet angle ≤ 2°.
  5. ✅ Electromechanical brake: Clearance 0.3–0.8mm (manufacturer spec). Brake torque ≥ 125% of rated hoist torque. Brake pads ≥ 50% thickness remaining.
  6. ✅ Upper limit switch: Tested unloaded — hook stops >300mm below top of drum.
  7. ✅ Lower limit switch: Tested unloaded — hook stops >500mm above floor level.
  8. ✅ Long-travel and cross-travel limits: Both end-stops triggered and verified before first run.
  9. ✅ End buffers: Both ends of runway and bridge. Buffer springs or hydraulic units correctly installed.
  10. ✅ Earthing continuity: <0.5Ω between crane metalwork and building earth point. Measured with 10A DC earth tester, not simple continuity buzzer.
  11. ✅ Insulation resistance: ≥1 MΩ at 500V DC between each phase conductor and earth (hoist motor, travel motors, control wiring).
  12. ✅ Emergency stop test: Pendant E-stop cuts power within 0.5s. All motions stop. E-stop ring latches. Machine does not restart until deliberate twist-release.
  13. ✅ Overload limiter calibration: Tested at 100% SWL (lifts freely) and 110% SWL (refuses to lift — alarm or contactor drop-out).
  14. ✅ VFD parameter verification (if fitted): Motor nameplate data entered. Ramp times set per crane manufacturer. Brake release timing verified. Encoder feedback loop closed. Dynamic braking resistor connected.
  15. ✅ Control pendant: All motion directions correct (no reversed hoist direction). Buttons labelled in Urdu and English. Pendant cable strain relief secure.
  16. ✅ Radio remote control (if fitted): Deadman pushbutton functional. Transmitter battery level indicator operational. Control zone tested (no phantom commands).
  17. ✅ Static load test: 125% SWL suspended for 10 minutes. No permanent deformation. Rope and hook inspect post-test.
  18. ✅ Dynamic load test: 110% SWL cycled through all motions (hoist up/down, bridge travel both directions, crab traverse both directions). No abnormal vibration or noise.
  19. ✅ Lubrication: Wire rope dressed with rope lubricant (Molykote HSC or equivalent). Travel wheel bearings greased. Open gears lubricated. All grease nipples identified and labelled.
  20. ✅ Documentation: Commission report signed. As-built wiring diagram attached to crane panel. Maintenance logbook started. PSQCA Form submitted (or Form 15 under Factory Act 1934).

12. FAQ — Electric Overhead Crane & Hoist Pakistan 2026

What is the price of a 2-tonne electric wire rope hoist in Pakistan 2026?

A 2-tonne electric wire rope hoist with single-speed 4m/min lift ranges from PKR 85,000–145,000 for imported units. Variable-speed VFD-controlled models with 6m lift are PKR 165,000–280,000. Prices vary by duty class (M3 vs M5) and headroom (low-headroom vs standard).

Is PSQCA certification mandatory for overhead cranes in Pakistan?

PSQCA PS:4756 covers design and safety requirements for electric overhead travelling cranes in Pakistan. While mandatory enforcement varies by industry, steel mills, cement plants, and NTDC/WAPDA substation projects require PSQCA-certified lifting equipment. All cranes must also comply with OSHA-equivalent Pakistan Factory Act 1934 inspection requirements.

What SWL rating do I need for a 5-tonne overhead crane?

SWL (Safe Working Load) is the rated capacity. For a 5-tonne EOT crane, the structural components are designed to FEM 1.001 or ISO 4301/1 duty class M4–M5 for typical industrial use. Always add a 25% dynamic factor for Pakistani road-vibration-sensitive installations.

Can a standard VFD be used for crane hoist motion?

Not all VFDs are suitable for crane hoist motion. Hoist duty requires a crane-rated VFD with: (1) brake chopper built-in, (2) load encoder feedback for closed-loop torque control, (3) anti-sway logic, (4) built-in safety contactor relay output for mechanical brake release. Generic pump/fan VFDs lack these features.

What cable festoon system is used for EOT cranes in Pakistan?

EOT cranes in Pakistan typically use either festoon cable systems (trailing cables on C-track or I-beam) or conductor bar (busbar) systems. For spans under 20m with moderate duty, festoon cable (H07RN-F flexible rubber cable) is preferred. For high-duty or long-span cranes, conductor bar systems with current collectors are more reliable and lower-maintenance.
