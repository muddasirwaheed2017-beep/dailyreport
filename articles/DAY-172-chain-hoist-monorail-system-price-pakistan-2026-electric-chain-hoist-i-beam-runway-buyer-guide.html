1. What Is an Electric Chain Hoist & Monorail System?

An electric chain hoist is a motor-driven lifting device that uses a roller-link chain and hook block to raise, hold, and lower loads. The hoist body contains a gear reducer, motor (typically 0.4–7.5 kW), electromagnetic brake, and upper limit switch. It is suspended from an end-carriage trolley that runs along a bottom-flange I-beam — the monorail runway.

The monorail is the simplest form of overhead material handling: a single beam, suspended from the ceiling structure or from a floor-mounted gantry frame, over which the hoist travels in one axis. This arrangement is enough to serve a single machine, a loading bay, or a maintenance aisle at a fraction of the cost of a full electric overhead (EOT) bridge crane.

Typical Applications in Pakistan

  • Textile mills: Loading bales into mixers, handling loom beam sections, die changes in weaving sheds
  • Automotive workshops: Engine removal, gearbox swaps, body panel handling
  • Steel fabrication: Positioning structural sections on CNC plasma/flame-cutting tables
  • Cold storage & food processing: Side-of-carcass handling, pallet stacking in deep-freeze aisles
  • Pharmaceutical: Moving IBC vessels and cleanroom equipment in ISO-classified areas
  • Power generation: Generator set rotor extraction, transformer oil tank removal

Capacity range in Pakistan: 0.5T (500 kg), 1T, 2T, 3T, 5T, 10T. Capacities above 10T typically step up to wire rope hoists and EOT bridge cranes.

2. CD1 vs MD1 vs Wire Rope: Which Type Do You Need?

CD1 — Single-Speed Chain Hoist

The CD1 is the standard workhorse for light and medium duty (FEM M3). It operates at a single hoisting speed (typically 2–8 m/min depending on capacity). The trolley drive is usually manual push (up to 2T) or motorised (3T and above). Price is the lowest of all electric hoist types — making it the default choice for maintenance bays and small workshops where the hoist is used fewer than 100 cycles per shift.

When to choose CD1: Intermittent use, single-speed precision is acceptable, budget is the primary driver, capacity ≤5T.

MD1 — Double-Speed Chain Hoist

The MD1 adds a slow-speed (creep) mode for precision load positioning — critical for assembly lines and machining centres where the hook must land within ±10 mm. Typical speeds: fast 4–8 m/min, slow 1–2 m/min. The motor has two sets of windings (Dahlander or separate winding) switched by a contactor. MD1 suits FEM M4–M5 duty and is preferred wherever the hoist runs ≥200 cycles per shift.

When to choose MD1: Precision placement required, high cycle rate, automotive or electronics assembly, FEM M4–M5 application.

Wire Rope Hoist (CD/MD type with drum)

For capacities above 5T or lift heights above 12 metres, a wire rope hoist (reeved drum with steel wire rope) replaces the chain mechanism. Wire rope hoists are more compact relative to their capacity, offer higher lift heights (up to 30+ metres), and tolerate higher duty classes (FEM M6–M8). These are the standard for EOT bridge cranes — see the separate EOT crane & wire rope hoist guide for details.

3. FEM Duty Class M3–M5: Matching the Hoist to the Workload

The FEM (Fédération Européenne de la Manutention) and ISO 4301 duty class system defines a hoist's design life in terms of load spectrum and total hours of operation. Undersizing the duty class is the leading cause of premature hoist failure in Pakistan — especially in textile mills where maintenance bays run three shifts.

FEM ClassISO 4301 GroupTotal Design HoursLoad SpectrumTypical Application
M2M2400 hLight (0.125)Rarely used maintenance hoists
M3M3800 hLight (0.125)General maintenance, workshops, 1-shift use
M4M41,600 hModerate (0.25)2-shift production, textile finishing
M5M53,200 hModerate-Heavy (0.5)3-shift continuous production, steel fabrication
M6M66,300 hHeavy (1.0)Foundry, steel mill, wire rope hoist required

How to Calculate Your Duty Class

Step 1: Estimate daily hoist hours (motor-on time). Step 2: Multiply by 300 working days × 10-year design life. Step 3: Estimate the average load as a fraction of rated capacity (load spectrum factor Kp). Plot on the ISO 4301 matrix. When in doubt, go one class higher.

Pakistan-specific note: WAPDA load-shedding causes frequent stop-restart cycles that accelerate brake wear and thermal stress on motor windings. For hoists in regions with >4 h/day load-shedding, specify M5 when M4 would otherwise suffice, and ensure the motor has Class F insulation minimum.

4. Monorail Runway & I-Beam Design Guide

The runway is the structural spine of the monorail system. Undersized runway beams cause excessive deflection, hoist trolley bind, and — in worst cases — beam flange cracking. Oversized beams waste steel and inflate civil costs. Use this guide to get the sizing right the first time.

4.1 Beam Profile Selection

For Pakistani markets, three I-beam profiles are standard:

  • IPE (European I-beam, equal flanges): IPE-160 to IPE-360. Best for new installations — wide, flat flange suits hoist trolley wheels. Available from PSML and imported from China/Turkey.
  • UB/UC (British Universal Beam): UB-152 to UB-305. Common on older structures built under British standards — often found in legacy textile mills.
  • HEA/HEB (European wide-flange): HEA-140 to HEA-220. Where headroom is critical (low-ceiling mezzanines), the shorter profile height saves 30–50 mm.

Dynamic load factor: Always multiply the static lifted load by φ₂ (dynamic factor from ISO 4302). For smooth hoist motion, φ₂ ≈ 1.1–1.2. For fast-start hoists or class M5, use φ₂ = 1.3.

4.3 End Stops and Buffer Bumpers

PSQCA and ISO 15011 require end stops at both ends of the runway to prevent the trolley running off the beam. Standard end stops are bolted flange plates. For high-speed trolleys (>20 m/min), add rubber bumper blocks rated for the kinetic energy at full travel speed.

4.4 Runway Joints and Alignment

When the runway exceeds 6 m (single beam section), a splice joint with fishplate bolted connection is used. The flange gap at the splice must not exceed 2 mm to prevent trolley wheel impact. Runway alignment tolerance: ±3 mm lateral over any 2-metre length. Misalignment causes wheel-flange contact and accelerated trolley bearing wear — the most common cause of trolley replacement in Pakistani facilities.

5. Festoon Cable System for Hoist Power & Control

The festoon cable system supplies 3-phase power and control signals to the travelling hoist along the runway. Proper festoon design prevents cable wear, trip hazards, and the nuisance faults (intermittent phase loss) that plague many Pakistani installations.

5.1 Cable Type Selection

  • Power cable: LAPP ÖLFLEX CRANE or equivalent — reeling-grade PVC, minimum 15-million flex cycles rating. For high-temperature areas (near furnaces or foundry), use silicone-sheathed cable.
  • Control cable: 7-core or 12-core festoon cable for pendant pushbutton connections. Use screened cable if VFD is fitted (to suppress EMI).
  • Cross-section: For 3T/2.2 kW motor at 50-metre runway: 3-core 2.5 mm² power + 7-core 1.0 mm² control. For 10T/7.5 kW at 50 m: 4-core 6 mm² + 12-core 1.5 mm².

5.2 Festoon Rail and Trolley Carriages

The festoon rail is a C-channel (C-25 or C-32 to DIN 3015) mounted below the runway beam. Cable is clipped into plastic festoon trolley carriages spaced at 500–800 mm. The carriages slide along the C-channel rail as the hoist travels. Cable loop length between carriages must equal approximately 1.5× the carriage spacing to allow slack without binding. For cable routing outside the festoon zone, use ladder tray or perforated tray to the main panel.

5.3 Pendant Pushbutton Station

The standard pendant for a 2-motion hoist (hoist up/down + trolley travel left/right) uses a 4-button or 6-button pendant station with an emergency-stop mushroom head. The pendant housing must be IP55 minimum for outdoor or dusty Pakistani environments. Pendant cable must be strain-relief anchored — cable must not carry mechanical load. For 10T+ hoists, a radio remote-control pendant (Autec/Hetronic type) eliminates trip hazards in busy bays.

6. Electrical Panel, Motor Protection & Control Circuit

The hoist control panel houses the main isolator, motor starter contactors, overload relays, limit switch circuit, and low-voltage control transformer. For Pakistani industrial sites where 3-phase supply voltage fluctuates ±15% (WAPDA 400 V ± tolerance), correct panel design prevents nuisance tripping and motor burnout.

6.1 Main Components

  • Main isolator: 16A–63A TP MCB or MCCB with door-interlock facility
  • Hoist motor starter: Reversing contactor pair (Up + Down) with mechanical and electrical interlock. Use AC-3 rated contactors — see the contactor & overload relay guide for sizing.
  • Thermal overload relay: Set to 110–115% of hoist motor FLC. See the thermal overload relay guide for bimetal vs electronic selection.
  • Trolley motor starter: Second reversing pair for motorised trolley travel (Left + Right)
  • Control transformer: 400 V / 24 V DC or 42 V AC. Low-voltage control is mandatory under PSQCA lifting gear standards.
  • Emergency stop relay: Safety relay with monitored reset. See the industrial safety relay guide for SIL-rated selection.
  • Motor protection relay (MPR): For hoists ≥5T in 3-shift use, add an MPR for phase-failure, undercurrent (chain break), and thermistor protection. See the motor protection relay guide.

6.2 VFD Option for Precision and Energy Saving

Installing a variable frequency drive (VFD) on the hoist motor eliminates the mechanical brake shock at start and stop, extends brake pad life, enables infinitely variable creep speed, and reduces peak demand current by up to 60% at start. For a 3T/2.2 kW hoist running 200 cycles/shift, adding a VFD saves approximately PKR 48,000/year in energy and reduces brake replacement frequency from 2×/year to 1×/3 years.

VFD selection note: Use a heavy-duty (HD) or crane-duty VFD with Safe Torque Off (STO) function. The hoist brake must still be mechanically held — never rely solely on VFD torque to hold a suspended load.

7. PSQCA, Factories Act 1934 & Safety Devices

Lifting equipment in Pakistan is regulated under the Factories Act 1934 (Sections 33–36), the PSQCA Lifting Gear Standards (PS:4756 for overhead cranes, PS:5048 for chain blocks), and in some provinces by the Punjab Factories Rules 1978. Non-compliance can result in Factory Inspector stop-work orders, insurance voidance, and criminal liability in the event of an accident.

7.1 Mandatory Safety Devices

  • Upper limit switch: Cuts hoist motor and applies brake before chain/hook over-travels. Must be a positive-opening contact (IEC 60947-5-1 annex K).
  • Overload limiter: Trips hoist if load exceeds 110–125% of rated capacity. Mandatory for hoists ≥1T under PS:5048.
  • Chain guide and chain bucket: Prevents chain jamming and wrapping around drum; collects slack chain.
  • Electromagnetic brake: Spring-applied, power-released brake that holds the load on power failure. Minimum static holding torque = 1.5× rated load torque.
  • Low-voltage control: 24 V DC or 42 V AC control circuit for operator safety. Standard under PSQCA lifting gear guidelines.
  • Emergency stop: Pendant E-stop cuts all hoist and trolley motion immediately.

7.2 Proof Load Test & Inspection Certificate

Before commissioning, the hoist and runway must undergo a proof load test at 125% of rated capacity (SWL). The test report must be retained on-site. PSQCA requires re-inspection at minimum annual intervals. The inspection must be conducted by a competent person (certified rigger/crane inspector) and the results recorded in a Lifting Equipment Register — a legal document under Section 36 of the Factories Act.

9. 18-Point Commissioning & Safety Checklist

Use this checklist before handing over any new or refurbished chain hoist / monorail installation to the plant. File the signed checklist in the Lifting Equipment Register.

  1. ☐ Structural inspection: Runway beam section verified against design load calculation. Deflection ≤ L/750 under test load.
  2. ☐ Beam alignment: Lateral straightness within ±3 mm per 2-metre length. No kinks at splices.
  3. ☐ End stops installed: Positive end stops at both runway ends. Buffer bumpers fitted if trolley speed >20 m/min.
  4. ☐ Hoist mounting check: Suspension lug/hook bolted or welded correctly. Lock-wired if bolted.
  5. ☐ Chain inspection: No twisted links, kinks, corrosion or wear >10% of original diameter. Properly lubricated.
  6. ☐ Chain bucket fitted: Collects slack chain and correctly anchored to hoist body.
  7. ☐ Hook latch operational: Spring-loaded latch closes fully, no binding. Swivel rotates freely.
  8. ☐ Electromagnetic brake: Static holding test at 125% SWL with power off. Load must not drift. Brake pads within wear limit.
  9. ☐ Upper limit switch: Tested at no-load — hoist stops and brake applies before hook reaches body. Trip point at minimum 50 mm clearance.
  10. ☐ Overload limiter calibration: Verified at 110% SWL (hoist trips) and 100% SWL (hoist runs). Certificate from calibrated test weights.
  11. ☐ Control transformer: Output voltage 24 V DC or 42 V AC confirmed under load. Earth connection verified.
  12. ☐ Emergency stop: Pendant E-stop opens control circuit within 0.5 s. Positive-opening safety relay reset only from pendant, not auto-restart.
  13. ☐ Direction markings: Pendant buttons labelled Up/Down/Left/Right in Urdu and English. Cross-check: pressing Up raises load.
  14. ☐ Motor insulation resistance: ≥1 MΩ at 500 V DC Megger test for new motor before energising.
  15. ☐ Festoon cable inspection: No kinks or abrasion points. Strain relief at both ends. Carriage spacing uniform. No cable dragging on ground at any trolley position.
  16. ☐ Earth continuity: <1 Ω from hoist body to main earth bar. Metal runway beam bonded to PE.
  17. ☐ Proof load test: 125% SWL raised 300 mm and held for 10 minutes. No visible deformation, no abnormal noise, no brake drift. Test report signed by competent person.
  18. ☐ Operator training: At least one person per shift trained in safe use, pre-use inspection, and emergency procedure. Training record signed and filed.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the price of an electric chain hoist in Pakistan 2026?

What is the difference between CD1 and MD1 chain hoist?

CD1 (single-speed) chain hoists are cost-effective for light-to-medium duty applications (FEM M3). MD1 (double-speed) models offer a slow creep speed for precision positioning and fast speed for travel, making them suitable for heavier or more frequent use (FEM M4–M5). MD1 is preferred in automotive assembly, textile, and steel fabrication plants where load placement precision matters.

How do I size an I-beam for a monorail system?

I-beam sizing depends on hoist capacity, span between support columns, and dynamic load factor (typically 1.1–1.3). An IPE-200 beam handles up to 2T on a 4-metre span. For 5T on a 6-metre span, an IPE-270 or HEB-180 is typically required. Always have a structural engineer verify deflection limits (L/750 maximum) and flange width compatibility with the hoist trolley foot dimension.

What safety devices are mandatory for a chain hoist in Pakistan?

Under the Factories Act 1934 and PSQCA lifting gear standards: upper limit switch, overload limiter (110–125% FLC trip), chain guide and chain bucket, low-voltage control (24 V DC or 42 V AC), and emergency-stop provision. For overhead installations in multi-operator environments, anti-collision interlocks are recommended.

Can I add a VFD to an existing chain hoist?

Yes, in most cases. The existing motor must be checked for inverter compatibility (Class F insulation, thermistor protection). The hoist brake must remain electromechanically controlled — it must not be released by the VFD output but by a separate contactor controlled in parallel. Add a dV/dt filter on the VFD output if the cable run exceeds 20 metres. CNC Electric can supply the VFD, dV/dt filter, and integration panel as a kit.

What is the annual maintenance cost of a 2T chain hoist in Pakistan?

For M4 duty (2-shift), budget PKR 12,000–20,000/year for consumables (brake pads, chain lubrication, limit switch contacts) plus PKR 8,000–12,000 for the annual competent-person inspection and load test. Total: PKR 20,000–32,000/year for a 2T M4 hoist in normal textile or light-fabrication use.
