2b. En-Route / Corridor Charging (Intercity Fleet)

For intercity bus or logistics fleets, en-route charging stations require 150–350 kW DC chargers with <30 min charge windows. Pakistan's motorway network has gaps — Lahore–Islamabad M-2 has 2 existing DC fast charge points (2026). Fleet operators must plan:

  • Range planning: 250–350 km range modern EV bus → 1 charge stop per motorway segment
  • Gridconnection: 11 kV supply via NTDC/LESCO at expressway rest stops
  • OCPP roaming: OCPI 2.2.1 protocol for inter-operator roaming (not yet mature in Pakistan)
  • Backup: Diesel generator with ATS as EVSE backup for load-shedding resilience

2c. Workplace / Hub Charging (Corporate Fleets)

Corporate offices with 20–50 EVs typically install 7–11 kW AC chargers in car parks. Key design consideration: office peak load (09:00–17:00 HVAC + equipment) coincides with LESCO peak tariff. OCPP scheduling must shift charging to off-peak (22:00–06:00) or use solar generation (10:00–15:00) to minimise cost.

3a. OCPP Backend Software Options (Pakistan Fleet Context)

  • Open-source (SaaS self-host): Steve (Siemens), OCPP-J server on cloud VM — zero license cost, requires IT team, used by some Pakistani CPOs
  • Commercial SaaS: Charge Point Management Systems (CPMS) — typically USD 1–3/charger/month; foreign currency cost is a concern at PKR 280+ exchange rate
  • On-premise appliance: Industrial PC or IIoT gateway with embedded CPMS — suitable for high-security or low-connectivity sites; see IIoT Gateway Guide for hardware
  • Integrated EMS: CPMS embedded within facility Energy Management System — best for ISO 50001 compliance + NEECA reporting

3b. Pakistan-Specific OCPP Deployment Challenges

  • 4G reliability: OCPP-J (WebSocket) preferred over OCPP-S (SOAP) on cellular — SOAP breaks on mobile NAT/firewall
  • Load shedding: UPS on OCPP gateway mandatory — 4-hour backup minimum; charger session data must be cached locally during outages and synced on reconnect
  • Static IP scarcity: Use DDNS or cloud MQTT bridge; most cellular SIMs use CGNAT (no inbound connections)
  • Currency risk: Cloud CPMS priced in USD — budget for 10–15% PKR depreciation annually

4. Load Management & Demand Response for EV Fleets

4a. Dynamic Load Management (DLM) Principles

DLM prevents the fleet depot from exceeding the LESCO sanctioned load (the kW value on the connection agreement). Exceeding sanctioned load triggers disconnection or penalty charges. The OCPP backend monitors real-time grid consumption and distributes available capacity across charging EVs.

4b. LESCO Demand Charge Trap — The Fleet Operator's Critical Risk

LESCO's EV-3 tariff includes a fixed demand charge of PKR 850/kW/month on the sanctioned load. A fleet depot with 500 kW sanctioned capacity pays PKR 425,000/month fixed — regardless of actual usage. Without DLM, uncoordinated charging creates simultaneous peak demand, forcing a higher sanctioned load than necessary. DLM can reduce required sanctioned capacity by 40–60%, saving PKR 170,000–255,000/month in fixed charges for a 500 kW nominal fleet.

5b. V2B Implementation — Technical Requirements

To implement V2B at a Pakistani corporate fleet depot today:

  1. Bidirectional EVSE: Charger must support ISO 15118-20 and have AC/DC bidirectional power electronics — not all 22 kW chargers support this. Expect 30–50% cost premium over uni-directional.
  2. Compatible EV OBC: Vehicle's on-board charger must support V2B — most 2025–2026 Chinese EV models (BYD Han, Atto 3) support V2B on CCS2. Check manufacturer specification.
  3. Energy Meter + OCPP: ISO 15118-20 session with OCMF-signed energy reports for billing/accounting.
  4. Building EMS integration: V2B command dispatch via EMS when building demand exceeds threshold — avoids manual operation.
  5. WAPDA net-metering: V2G (grid export) requires separate NEPRA approval; V2B (behind-the-meter only) does not require grid export approval.

V2B payback example (20 × BYD Atto 3, 50 kWh battery, Karachi office, KESC tariff):
Peak shaving 100 kW × 4h daily at PKR 48/kWh peak rate = PKR 19,200/day → PKR 480,000/month in peak cost avoided. Bidirectional charger premium: ~PKR 150,000 × 20 units = PKR 3M one-time. Payback: ~6 months.

11. Pakistan-Specific Fleet Charging Pitfalls

  1. Under-specifying the transformer: A 200 kW fleet depot often needs a 400 kVA transformer, not 250 kVA, due to PF and inrush. LESCO transformer waiting list: 3–6 months. Order early.
  2. Missing Type B RCCB: Specifying Type A RCCB to save PKR 5,000/circuit fails PSQCA PS:5201:2023 inspection. EV OBCs produce DC smooth fault current that Type A cannot detect — fire/shock risk.
  3. OCPP 1.6J without DLM: Old OCPP 1.6J backends lack proper Smart Charging profile support. Upgrade to 2.0.1 before scaling the fleet — migration is painful and expensive at 50+ chargers.
  4. No load shedding plan: OCPP sessions interrupted by load shedding corrupt energy records and trigger billing disputes. Fit UPS on OCPP gateway + configure charger local cache (OCPP 2.0.1 supports offline transactions).
  5. Ignoring kVAr penalties: LESCO imposes PF penalty below 0.85 — EV chargers idle between sessions draw low PF. Auto-switching capacitor bank pays for itself in 2–4 months.
  6. Skipping earthing verification: Pakistani depot floors (Lahore sandy soil, Karachi made-up land) have highly variable soil resistivity. Test before commissioning; > 5 Ω requires remediation before LESCO will energise the connection.
  7. Currency risk on imported EVSE: Nearly all 22+ kW chargers are imported (mostly China). At PKR 280+ exchange rate, a USD 2,000 charger costs PKR 560,000. Budget contingency 15% for currency movement.
  8. PSQCA customs clearance: Uncertified EVSE held at port for testing — 30–90 days. Confirm PS:5201:2023 conformity mark before ordering. Add 6–8 weeks minimum to delivery timeline for first-time imports.

13. FAQs

How many EV chargers does a 50-vehicle fleet depot need in Pakistan?

A 50-vehicle fleet typically needs 15–25 chargers using overnight charging with smart load management. With 7–8-hour charging windows and staggered schedules, 1 charger per 2–3 vehicles suffices. OCPP dynamic load management keeps peak demand below the LESCO sanctioned load, avoiding costly capacity upgrades.

What is the WAPDA/LESCO EV fleet tariff in Pakistan 2026?

LESCO EV-3 fleet tariff (approved 2024): off-peak (22:00–06:00) PKR 24.5/kWh, shoulder (06:00–18:00) PKR 32/kWh, peak (18:00–22:00) PKR 48/kWh. Fixed demand charge PKR 850/kW/month of sanctioned capacity. Fleet operators with OCPP smart charging can schedule 80%+ of charging in off-peak, reducing effective per-kWh cost to PKR 25–28.

Is V2G (vehicle-to-grid) available in Pakistan 2026?

V2G is not yet commercially deployed in Pakistan but NEPRA's EV Policy 2026 amendments reference bidirectional charging pilots. V2B (vehicle-to-building) is technically feasible today using CCS2 bidirectional OBCs and ISO 15118-20 compliant chargers. Early adopters can achieve 15–20% reduction in peak demand charges. Full WAPDA grid export metering for V2G requires separate approval under NEPRA's net-metering framework.

What electrical connection is required for a 10-charger fleet depot in Pakistan?

A 10 × 22 kW AC charger depot (220 kW installed) with 50% simultaneity factor needs ~110 kW demand. LESCO requires a 3-phase 415V connection with 150–200A main incomer, kVAr compensation to PF ≥0.95, and a dedicated EV sub-panel with Type B RCCBs per IEC 62752. Transformer upgrade to 200 kVA is typically needed for a residential or light-commercial supply. Full connection application via LESCO's online portal with load schedule.

Can solar panels power an EV fleet depot in Pakistan?

Yes. A 100 kWp rooftop solar + 200 kWh LiFePO4 BESS can supply 60–70% of a 20-vehicle fleet's daytime charging needs in Pakistan. The key is OCPP-based smart scheduling: BESS charges from solar during 08:00–16:00, EVs charge from BESS + grid during evening hours at off-peak rates. ROI is typically 4–6 years at current petrol vs electricity differential (PKR 350/litre petrol vs PKR 24.5/kWh EV-off-peak).
