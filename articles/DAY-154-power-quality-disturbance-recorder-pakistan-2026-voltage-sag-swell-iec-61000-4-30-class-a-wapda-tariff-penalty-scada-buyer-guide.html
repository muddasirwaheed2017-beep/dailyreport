1. What Is a Power Quality Disturbance Recorder?

A Power Quality Disturbance Recorder (PQDR) — also called a Power Quality Analyser with extended logging, a Power Disturbance Monitor, or simply a PQ Recorder — is an instrument that continuously monitors one or more electrical supply points and stores timestamped records of every deviation from nominal voltage, frequency, and waveform quality. Unlike a basic multimeter or even a standard power quality analyser, a PQDR is designed for unattended long-term deployment — typically seven to thirty days at a minimum, and often permanently installed at the utility incomer or critical production feeder.

The core value proposition in Pakistan is threefold:

  1. Evidence — a Class A PQDR produces time-stamped, court-admissible voltage sag records that can be presented to WAPDA, LESCO, FESCO, MEPCO, IESCO, or HESCO when disputing damage claims or penalty billings caused by utility-side disturbances.
  2. Root-cause analysis — by correlating sag/swell events with production stoppage timestamps from your MES or SCADA historian, you can pinpoint whether downtime is caused by the utility, your own network, or equipment malfunction.
  3. Compliance — IEC 61000-4-30 Class A measurement methodology is the internationally recognised standard; NEPRA's Power Quality Regulations (2022) reference IEC 61000-4-30 as the measurement method for PCC (Point of Common Coupling) assessments.

4. Why Pakistani Industrial Facilities Need a PQDR Right Now

Pakistan's grid operates under exceptional stress. The five conditions that make a PQDR mandatory for any facility consuming more than 500 kVA:

  1. Chronic voltage sags from load shedding transitions — WAPDA's rotating load shedding, still occurring in 2026 despite solar additions, creates 20–80 sag events per week on many 11 kV feeders. Without a Class A record, your insurance claim for a PKR 3.5 million VFD is rejected.
  2. NEPRA Power Quality Regulations 2022 — Under Regulation 7, industrial consumers connected at 11 kV or above can formally request PQ assessments. A PQDR gives you the counter-measurement capability to validate (or rebut) DISCO's own measurements.
  3. IEEE 519-2022 compliance pressure — EU and US textile buyers are now requiring IEEE 519-2022 harmonic compliance in their vendor audits. A PQDR that logs THD-V and THD-I per-harmonic order provides the data for your compliance report.
  4. High cost of unrecorded events — A 200 ms voltage sag that trips 20 VFDs on a weaving or spinning line causes PKR 180,000–450,000 of scrap and restart cost. Without a timestamped record, you cannot prove the sag came from the utility side vs your own factory.
  5. ISO 50001 EnPI data quality — Your Energy Management System energy performance indicators lose validity if the sub-meter data is contaminated by unrecorded interruptions. A PQDR timestamps every interruption so the EMS can exclude incomplete measurement intervals.

5. Using PQDR Evidence to Dispute WAPDA / DISCO Tariff Penalties

This is the highest-ROI use case for a Class A PQDR in Pakistan. The workflow:

5.1 Penalty / Damage Scenario Setup

  • LESCO / FESCO bills you for "power factor penalty" under their PF tariff — but your factory PF was 0.95+ when a 11 kV sag caused your capacitor bank protection to trip, dropping PF momentarily.
  • WAPDA refuses to compensate for equipment damage during a supply interruption, claiming it was your in-premises fault.
  • DISCO performs a PQ assessment and claims your facility injects harmonics above the PCC limit — you need counter-evidence.

5.2 Documentation Chain from PQDR to Formal Complaint

  1. Install Class A PQDR at the 11 kV / LT incomer, synchronised to GPS UTC time ±20 ms. Install a second PQDR at the same location if the event is likely to become a legal matter (dual independent evidence chain).
  2. Capture the sag/swell/interruption event — PQDR records: RMS profile (10-cycle intervals), waveform capture (at least 100 ms pre-trigger, 500 ms post-trigger), THD-V at time of event, event start/end timestamp UTC.
  3. Cross-reference with production data — Export PQDR event CSV. Compare timestamps with your MES/SCADA stoppage log and motor trip logs (from your MCC IIoT gateway event buffer).
  4. File formal complaint with NEPRA under Section 7(3) of the NEPRA Power Quality Regulations 2022, attaching the PQDR PDF/CSV report and a loss quantification statement signed by a licensed electrical engineer.
  5. DISCO response — DISCO must respond within 30 days. With Class A data, most DISCOs settle the PF penalty waiver administratively rather than proceed to tribunal.

Pakistan-specific note: Always verify the GPS antenna can receive signal through the factory roof or ceiling. 7. Channel Configuration for Pakistani Industrial Sites

7.1 11 kV Incomer (High-Voltage Side — via VT/CT)

  • Voltage: 3 × VT secondary (110V or 100V phase-to-neutral) — verify VT ratio and burden before connecting PQDR inputs.
  • Current: 3 × CT secondary (1A or 5A) — confirm accuracy class ≥ 0.5S for energy and ≥ 5P20 for protection events.
  • PQDR input range: 100V / 1A or 100V / 5A — most Class A recorders support both via jumper selection.
  • Auxiliary DI channels: 4–8 digital inputs — connect to: DISCO auto-recloser trip output, main incomer trip relay, generator ATS status, capacitor bank contactor feedback. These DI timestamps appear on the same event timeline as voltage events, enabling root-cause correlation.

7.2 400V LT Feeder (Low-Voltage Side — Direct or via CT)

  • Voltage: 3 × direct input up to 690V L-L (most Class A recorders are 300V or 480V direct; use a step-down VT for 690V).
  • Current: Rogowski coil clamps (1A–10,000A) for quick installation without CT disconnection — suitable for surveys. For permanent installation, solid-core or split-core CTs with rated burden ≤ 2.5 VA are preferred.
  • Temperature channel (optional): PT100 input on some models — monitors transformer winding temperature correlation with voltage disturbances.

8. Memory, Storage and Recording Modes

A PQDR recording at 512 samples/cycle (50 Hz) across 3 voltage + 3 current channels + 4 DI channels generates approximately 18–25 MB per day of compressed aggregated data (10/12-cycle RMS, 10-min values, event waveforms). For a 30-day campaign at a site with high sag frequency (50+ events/day), budget 2–4 GB of storage.

High-speed transient 1 MHz transient capture, peak detection Lightning, surge, capacitor switching spike identification High — 10–50 MB per event

Best practice for Pakistan: Enable all continuous modes always. Set triggered waveform capture for sag threshold 90% and swell threshold 110% with 200 ms pre-trigger / 1,000 ms post-trigger. High-speed transient capture is high-storage — enable only during targeted investigations (lightning season June–August, or after a VFD failure).

9.2 COMTRADE File Format — Portable Event Archive

Class A PQDRs store waveform captures in COMTRADE format (IEEE C37.111-2013), the universal protection and power quality event archive format. COMTRADE files consist of a .CFG configuration file + .DAT waveform data file. Any power quality software (commercial or open-source) can open these. For Pakistani factory owners: when requesting PQDR data for a WAPDA dispute, specifically request COMTRADE .CFG/.DAT exports plus the PDF or HTML summary report. The COMTRADE file is the primary legal evidence; the PDF is the human-readable presentation.

10. PQDR in an ISO 50001 Energy Management System

Your ISO 50001 EMS measures energy performance indicators (EnPIs) using sub-meter kWh data. However, EnPI calculations become invalid when:

  • A voltage sag causes production to stop mid-interval — the sub-meter records partial production kWh but the production system records zero output for that batch.
  • A short interruption (auto-recloser, 300 ms) causes all meters to reset, creating a gap in the 10-min aggregation interval.
  • A voltage swell causes transformer saturation, drawing reactive power spikes that inflate kVAh billing without producing useful work.

Integrating the PQDR's event timestamps into the EMS resolves these issues by allowing the EnPI calculation engine to:

  1. Flag EnPI intervals that contain a PQ event as "invalid measurement intervals" — excluded from the EnB (Energy Baseline) regression.
  2. Quantify energy wasted during voltage sag recovery (motor re-acceleration current surge, production restart energy).
  3. Report the total "PQ loss kWh" per month as a separate energy conservation measure (ECM) opportunity in the NEECA Energy Audit report.

The PQDR → historian → EMS integration is therefore not optional for ISO 50001 Level 3 (automated real-time EnPI dashboards) — it is the data quality backbone of the entire measurement and verification chain.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a power quality analyser and a power quality disturbance recorder?

A power quality analyser is primarily a handheld or portable instrument used for one-off measurements, surveys, and troubleshooting — typically deployed for hours to a few days. A power quality disturbance recorder (PQDR) is designed for permanent or long-term unattended installation (weeks to months) with large local storage, continuous triggered waveform capture, and network connectivity for real-time SCADA integration. All PQDRs perform the same measurements as a PQ analyser, but their key advantage is timestamped event logging over extended periods — which is what makes the data admissible as legal evidence in WAPDA / DISCO disputes under NEPRA Power Quality Regulations 2022.

Which IEC 61000-4-30 class is required for a NEPRA power quality dispute in Pakistan?

Class A is required for formal disputes and contractual power quality assessments under NEPRA Power Quality Regulations 2022. Class A measurement uncertainty (±0.1% of Udin), mandatory 10/12-cycle aggregation, and ±20 ms UTC timing accuracy are what differentiate Class A data from Class S. Class S data may be used as supplementary evidence but is insufficient as primary evidence in a formal NEPRA complaint filing against a DISCO. Always confirm the instrument carries an accredited IEC 61000-4-30 Class A certificate (IECEE CB or equivalent), not just a datasheet claim.

How long should I monitor before filing a WAPDA voltage sag complaint?

A minimum of one continuous week of Class A PQDR data is required by NEPRA regulations; one month is strongly recommended. This establishes statistical validity — a one-week campaign might capture a good or bad week atypically. For SARFI (System Average RMS variation Frequency Index) calculations per IEEE 1564-2014, a minimum 12-week dataset gives statistically significant percentile values. For immediate damage claims (equipment failure during a specific event), even a 24-hour dataset is sufficient provided it captures the event's timestamp and waveform, plus a minimum 1-hour baseline before and after the event.

Can a PQDR connected at the 400V LT bus be used to prove a voltage sag originated from WAPDA's 11 kV network?

Yes, with the right analysis. The 11 kV/400V transformer has a typical impedance of 4–6% — a sag originating on the 11 kV side will appear at the 400V LT bus at approximately the same per-unit residual voltage (minus the transformer impedance drop). Key indicators that the sag originated from the utility 11 kV network: (a) the sag affects all three phases simultaneously (utility fault); (b) the sag duration matches DISCO auto-recloser clearing time (80–150 ms for single-shot reclosing); (c) a simultaneous DI input from the 11 kV incomer protection relay trip confirms the source. For highest evidentiary value, deploy a Class A recorder both at the 11 kV PCC (via VT) and the 400V LT bus simultaneously — the synchronised timestamps (GPS) prove both sides saw the same event.

What is SARFI and how does NEPRA use it for power quality benchmarking?

SARFI (System Average RMS variation Frequency Index) is defined in IEEE 1564-2014 as the number of voltage sag/swell/interruption events per year that exceed a threshold, averaged across all monitored sites. SARFI-90 counts events where voltage drops below 90% of nominal; SARFI-70 below 70%; SARFI-50 below 50%. NEPRA's Power Quality Regulations 2022 benchmark DISCOs against SARFI-90 targets. If WAPDA/LESCO exceeds the SARFI-90 threshold for your supply zone, you have a basis for a formal PQ complaint. Your PQDR's Class A data calculates your site-specific SARFI indices, which you can compare against NEPRA's publicly published DISCO performance data.

Does a PQDR replace a power quality analyser for IEEE 519 harmonic compliance testing?

A Class A PQDR with harmonic measurement up to H50 and 10-min aggregation can fully replace a portable analyser for IEEE 519-2022 harmonic compliance surveys, and provides a superior dataset because it captures 95th percentile THD-V and THD-I values over a week or more — which is exactly what IEEE 519-2022 requires for PCC compliance assessment (95th percentile, 10-min values over one week). A portable analyser used for a few hours may capture an atypically clean or dirty period. For the initial active harmonic filter sizing survey, a 7-day PQDR campaign at the PCC is the gold standard for Pakistan's variable production schedules.
