5. Conduit Bodies (LB / LL / LR / T / C) — When and How to Use

Conduit bodies are the Pakistani electrician's tool for making 90° direction changes in rigid conduit without bending the conduit itself, and for providing cable pull access on long runs. They are NOT junction boxes — IEC 61386 prohibits using conduit bodies as wire splice points.

5.2 Pull Rope Removal Rule (Critical for Pakistan Industrial Sites)

After pulling cable through a conduit body, the pull rope/string MUST be removed. Leaving rope inside a conduit body is a documented cause of moisture wicking in Pakistan outdoor installations (rope acts as a wick, drawing water through conduit body and into the enclosure). LESCO inspectors in Lahore's industrial zones have flagged this as a recurring installation defect in 2024–2025 industrial connection audits.

5.3 Conduit Body Fill Capacity — IEC 61386 Pakistan Requirement

Cable fill in a conduit body must not exceed 40% of the internal cross-section. For three 4mm² cables (each ~8.7mm² cross-section), total fill = 26.1mm². A 20mm LB conduit body has ~314mm² cross-section → 40% = 125mm² → maximum ~14 cables of 4mm². For larger cables, upsize the conduit body or use a junction box instead.

6. Pakistan-Specific Failure Modes — Conduit Fittings

6.1 Thermal Expansion — PVC Conduit Fittings in Pakistan Summers

Pakistan rooftop PVC conduit surface temperature in June–August: 60–70°C (black conduit in direct sun). PVC thermal expansion coefficient: 0.08mm/m/°C. For a 10-metre outdoor conduit run:

  • ΔT = 60°C (from 10°C winter night to 70°C summer day)
  • Expansion = 10m × 0.08mm/m/°C × 60°C = 48mm
  • Without an expansion coupling, this 48mm movement pulls slip couplings apart and stresses junction box entries

Rule: Install PVC expansion couplings every 6 metres on outdoor rooftop solar conduit runs. GI conduit expansion (0.011mm/m/°C) requires expansion couplings every 30 metres outdoors. Refer to our Conduit Price Pakistan guide for conduit type selection by application.

6.2 Karachi Salt Fog — Coastal Conduit Fitting Corrosion

Karachi's coastal industrial zones (SITE, Port Qasim, Bin Qasim) experience salt fog deposition rates of 60–120 mg NaCl/m²/day (ASTM B117 Salt Fog Class S3–S4). Electroplated zinc GI fittings corrode through in 12–18 months in these environments. Specifications for Karachi coastal installations:

  • Outdoor GI fittings: Hot-dip galvanised (HDG, ≥45µm coating) or specify SS 304/316 locknuts and bushings
  • Junction boxes: Polycarbonate IP65 or SS 316 — not painted steel or electroplated GI
  • Saddles: SS 316 or nylon — not GI in salt fog zones

6.3 WAPDA/DISCO Metering Board — Missing Bushing Failure

The most common conduit fitting defect cited in LESCO's 2025 SQ&P electrical installation inspection report: conduit entering meter board without a bushing. Without a bushing, the sharp cut edge of a steel or PVC conduit abrades the cable insulation at the entry point. Over months of thermal cycling and vibration, insulation breaks down → earth fault → MCB trip or fire. The fix costs PKR 20–50 (one bushing). The failure cost ranges from PKR 50,000 (rewiring) to PKR 2.5M+ (fire). Bushings are mandatory at every conduit-to-enclosure entry under IEC 60670-1.

6.4 Rodent Intrusion — Open Conduit Ends

A documented failure mode in Pakistan residential and agricultural installations: conduit ends left open at junction boxes, pull boxes, or unused knockouts. Rodents (rats, mice) enter through open conduit ends and nest inside, chewing cable insulation. A 2024 survey of agricultural pump control panels in Punjab found rodent damage in 23% of panels inspected, with open conduit ends as the root cause in 71% of cases. Install end caps or conduit bushings on every conduit end, including unused knockouts. Cost: PKR 6–25 per fitting.

6.5 NEPRA Net Metering Inspection — Outdoor Junction Box Defects

NEPRA's 2024 Net Metering Inspection Framework (enforced by LESCO, MEPCO, FESCO, IESCO, HESCO) requires all outdoor solar PV string connection enclosures to be:

  • Minimum IP65 (not IP54)
  • UV-resistant material (polycarbonate preferred; PVC acceptable if UV grade specified)
  • Labelled with DC voltage warning per IEC 60417-5036
  • Accessible without tools for DISCO metering inspection

Installations failing these criteria have been refused net metering connection in LESCO territory (2024–2025 inspection cycle). See our RCCB Type A vs Type AC guide for solar protection device requirements and our Earthing System Design guide for PV system earthing requirements.
