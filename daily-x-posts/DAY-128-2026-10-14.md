## Primary Post

At Pakistan's current industrial tariff (Rs 38/kWh blended), the payback on an IE3 vs IE1 motor upgrade is 2–8 months.

IE1 to IE3 on a 37 kW motor = Rs 270,000/year saved. Motor costs Rs 500,000 new. Payback: 22 months — and that's the big one.

For a 5.5 kW motor? Rs 40,000/year saving. IE3 premium: Rs 20,000. Payback: 6 months.

NEECA MEPS (SRO 612/2022) now mandates IE2 minimum for new motors above 0.75 kW. IE1 at import = customs detention risk.

## Thread Post 1

Biggest hidden cost in Pakistani factories: motors running at 40% load.

42% of installed motors in Pakistan run below 50% nameplate load (NEECA 2024 survey of 340 facilities).

At 40% load, an IE3 motor runs at ~80–82% actual efficiency instead of its rated 91%.
Reactive power draw also spikes — PF drops to 0.65–0.70.
NEPRA demand charge penalty adds Rs 18,000–24,000/month per motor.

Fix: correctly sized motor + capacitor bank. Don't oversize "for future use."

## Thread Post 2

Pakistani motor rewinding: three tiers, three very different outcomes.

Tier 1 (EASA-affiliated, Karachi/Lahore): core test before/after, VPI varnish, <0.5% efficiency loss
Tier 2 (established local shops): variable, 1–3% loss common
Tier 3 (bazaar-grade): open-flame stripping, 3–6% loss typical

On a 37 kW motor at Rs 38/kWh: 3% extra loss = Rs 81,000/year extra electricity.
Tier-3 rewind at Rs 90,000 + Rs 81,000/year = more expensive than new IE3 in under 2 years.

Always specify Tier 1 for motors above 11 kW.

## Thread Post 3

VFD vs capacitor bank: not competing — both.

VFD = real kWh savings on centrifugal pumps/fans (cube law: 80% speed = 51.2% power)
Capacitor bank = kVAr reduction = NEPRA demand charge saving (NOT kWh)

A 75 kW motor at PF 0.78 → corrected to 0.95 saves Rs 18,000–24,000/month in demand charges alone.
Capacitor bank cost: Rs 35,000–55,000. Payback: under 3 months.

Use both. They're cumulative, not alternatives.
