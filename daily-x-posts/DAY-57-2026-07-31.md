## PRIMARY POST

Bus duct sizing mistake that's costing Pakistani factories millions:

Using IEC-rated current at Pakistan ambient.

IEC 61439-6 rates bus duct at 35°C.
Pakistan factory floors run at 45–50°C in summer.

The derating factor at 45°C = 0.94.

A "1600A" bus duct actually delivers 1504A at Pakistan ambient.

If your load is 1500A and you bought 1600A-rated BTS — you have 4A of headroom.

**1/7**

Bus duct vs wiring duct — the naming confusion that causes project failures:

- Bus duct (busbar trunking): sealed metallic power distribution, 400A–4000A, runs between panels
- Wiring duct: open plastic trough for control cables inside panels

Not the same. Not similar. Different product categories entirely.

We see this mix-up in Pakistan specs every month.

**3/7**

Load-shedding is destroying Pakistani bus duct joints silently.

4–14 thermal cycles per day.
Each cycle: expand → contract → expand.
Bolted joints loosen over 3–5 years.

Loose joint → resistance rises 800–2000% → localised heating → fault.

Fix: IR thermography every year (PKR 15,000–35,000).
Ignore it: PKR 80,000–350,000 fault repair.

**4/7**

WAPDA SDO inspection — 6 bus duct check items:

1. IP54+ certificate (IEC 60529) — reject without it
2. Icw fault level rating ≥ transformer fault current
3. Hanger spacing ≤ 1500mm
4. Earth bond at both ends + every 25m
5. Fire barriers at all wall/slab penetrations
6. Expansion joints on rising mains (every 30m)

Missing any = delay 2–6 weeks + rework PKR 8,000–1,500,000

**5/7**

Plug-in bus duct: the infrastructure play for Pakistani manufacturers who change machine layouts.

Adding a machine via plug-in tap-off box: 2–4 hours.
Adding the same machine via new cable run: 2–4 days.

Pakistani textile and auto-assembly plants reconfigure lines for export orders. Plug-in BTS makes relocation PKR 15,000 vs PKR 60,000+.

---

**6/7**

Aluminium bus duct in Pakistan — acceptable but conditional.

15–20% cheaper than copper.
One hard rule: bi-metallic compression lugs at ALL copper-to-aluminium interfaces.

Without bi-metallic lugs: galvanic corrosion → resistance rise → fire.

Most experienced Pakistani contractors still specify copper.
The PKR 15–20% premium is worth the reduced risk.
