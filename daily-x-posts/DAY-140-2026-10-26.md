## Thread 1 — The Single-Channel Problem

Pakistan panel shops wire E-stops like this:
NC contact → contactor coil → done

If that NC contact welds (common in dusty Karachi, humid Faisalabad):
- Button still feels "pressed"
- Machine still runs
- Operator assumes they're protected

They are not.

A dual-channel SRU detects contact welding. A standard relay does not.

## Thread 2 — Stop Category 0, 1, 2

EN 60204-1 says HOW a machine must stop:

🔴 Cat 0: Immediate power removal (grinders, presses)
🟡 Cat 1: Controlled decel, then power off (VFD-driven textile ring frames)
🟢 Cat 2: Controlled stop, power stays on (CNC tool change, robot programming)

Most Pakistan machines need Cat 0.
Textile winding machines need Cat 1 — or you break spindles every E-stop.

## Thread 3 — The EDM Wiring Pakistan Engineers Skip

EDM = External Device Monitoring

It reads the feedback contacts of your safety contactors.
If a contactor sticks closed → EDM prevents reset → system stays safe.

Cost to wire EDM: 2 extra conductors, PKR 500 labour.
Cost of missing a welded contactor: one operator injury, plant shutdown, 6-figure PSEB fine.

## Thread 4 — Safety Light Curtain Distance Calculation

Press brake Pakistan spec error:
Budget-market setup total response time: 120 ms
Safety distance required: 240 mm minimum

Most Pakistan press brakes have guard posts at 80 mm.
At 120 ms, a hand moving 2,000 mm/s travels 240 mm into the closing zone before the brake stops.

Formula: S = K × (t1 + t2) + C
K = 2,000 mm/s | Calculate this BEFORE installing, not after the accident.
