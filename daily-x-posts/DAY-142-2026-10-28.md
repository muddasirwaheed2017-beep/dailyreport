**Draft 1 (thread opener — educational hook):**

Pakistan engineers: specifying a 10,000 PPR encoder when 1,024 PPR is enough costs you 60–80% extra AND can overflow your PLC counter.

10,000 PPR × 3,000 RPM ÷ 60 = 500 kHz pulse rate. Most Pakistan PLC high-speed counters max out at 100 kHz.

Result: missed counts → wrong position → crash.

**Tweet 2:**

Incremental vs Absolute encoder — the Pakistan decision in one question:

"Does your machine need to know position IMMEDIATELY on power-up after a WAPDA outage, WITHOUT running a homing cycle?"

✅ YES → Absolute encoder (costs 40–60% more, but prevents crashes on restart)
❌ NO → Incremental encoder (cheaper, simpler)

---

**Tweet 3:**

Hollow shaft vs solid shaft encoder for Pakistan motor retrofits:

Solid shaft → needs coupling + bracket → coupling misalignment = #1 encoder failure cause in Pakistan field

Hollow shaft → slides over motor shaft → no coupling → installs in 15 min not 45 min → eliminates the failure mode

For retrofit: hollow shaft almost always wins.

**Tweet 4:**

Karachi humidity + Faisalabad textile dust = optical encoder destroyer

The optical disc fogs, corrodes, fills with dust.

Solution: IP67 magnetic encoder. No optical path = nothing to corrode or clog.

Slightly lower max PPR (10,000 vs 100,000+) — which you don't need for 95% of Pakistan industrial applications.
