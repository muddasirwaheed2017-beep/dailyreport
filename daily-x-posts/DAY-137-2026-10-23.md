### Thread 1 — The Minimum Pressure Trap

Pilot-operated solenoid valve arrived. Working fine.

Six months later: cylinder randomly won't extend during peak production.

Root cause: compressed air pressure dropping to 0.8 bar during peak demand.

Pilot-operated valves need 1.5–3 bar minimum to open.

Below that: valve won't open, regardless of electrical signal.

Direct-acting valve = works from 0 bar. No minimum pressure requirement.

Specify direct-acting whenever Pakistan compressed air pressure consistency is uncertain.

### Thread 2 — 5/2 vs 5/3 for Pakistan Load-Shedding

5/2 monostable (spring-return): cylinder always retracts to defined position on power loss.
→ WAPDA trips → cylinder retracts → safe for operator to clear jams.

5/3 pressure-centre: cylinder locks mid-stroke on power loss.
→ Load-shedding → cylinder locked in unknown mid-position.
→ Worker tries to clear jam → cylinder is locked with full air pressure.
→ Power restores → machine cycles on operator.

For Pakistan industry: 5/2 monostable is safer default.
Use 5/3 only where mid-stroke locking is explicitly required AND interlocked.

### Thread 3 — BSP vs NPT Thread Trap

Pakistan pneumatic infrastructure: BSP (G) threads — 55° Whitworth flank.
Many Chinese solenoid valve imports: NPT threads — 60° American flank.

The threads partially engage. Look OK on assembly.
Under 6 bar compressed air: slow leak. Over months: metal fretting, permanent leak.

Fix: Verify "G" thread designation on specification sheet BEFORE ordering.
BSP ≠ NPT. PTFE tape is not a fix for the wrong thread standard.
