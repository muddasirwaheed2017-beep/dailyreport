### Personal / Long-Form Post

**A 75kW motor failed in a Lahore pharmaceutical plant last October. The protection was "correct" on paper. Here's why it still failed — and why Pakistan's WAPDA supply makes this a common problem.**

The protection package:
- Thermal overload relay ✅
- Phase failure relay ✅
- Earth leakage relay ✅

Three protection devices. All correctly rated. All working.

The motor failed anyway.

**What happened:**

WAPDA was delivering 4.8% voltage unbalance on the plant's 11kV feeder — typical for a Lahore industrial zone during summer peak load. IEC 60034-26 says 4% voltage unbalance produces approximately 32% additional rotor heating. The thermal OL relay measured current — not rotor temperature. Current looked normal. Phase failure relay threshold was set at 30% asymmetry — a common factory default. 4.8% didn't register.

Over 3 months, the rotor insulation degraded silently. Then a winding-to-frame fault developed. The earth leakage relay should have caught it — but the CBCT had a termination issue and wasn't triggering correctly.

Result: complete motor failure. 75kW motor rewind: PKR 185,000. Shutdown: 19 hours. Lost production: PKR 195,000. Total: PKR 380,000.

The solution that would have prevented this: a mid-range digital motor protection relay with negative-sequence current measurement. Cost: PKR 22,000. Installed in the same DIN rail space as the thermal OL relay. With a negative-sequence threshold correctly set at 20% (corresponding to ~4.5% voltage unbalance), it would have tripped the motor weeks before the winding fault developed.

**The Pakistan-specific context that most guides don't address:**

Pakistan WAPDA industrial feeders in textile cities and food processing zones regularly show 2–5% voltage unbalance in summer, caused by uneven loading across the 11kV distribution network. This is not a fault — it's the normal operating condition of Pakistan's grid under peak load. IEC motor standards assume a maximum of 1% voltage unbalance for full-rated operation.

The combination of WAPDA asymmetry + Pakistan 45°C ambient (IEC motors rated to 40°C) + load-shedding thermal cycling (4–6 motor starts per day instead of the IEC design basis of 2) means Pakistan motors operate under significantly more stress than IEC ratings account for.

**Three things that make digital motor protection essential for Pakistan above 22kW:**

**1. Negative-sequence current protection**
Measures the actual asymmetry in the current waveform, not just voltage. Catches 2–5% WAPDA unbalance and trips before it becomes destructive. Set threshold at 20–25% for WAPDA industrial feeders.

**2. Thermal memory across load-shedding**
Bimetallic thermal OL relay: resets to zero when WAPDA cuts power. Digital relay: stores thermal model in NVRAM. When supply restores, the relay knows the motor was at 70% thermal capacity before the interruption — and will trip earlier if the motor is immediately overloaded on restart. Critical for Pakistan plants running through WAPDA load-shedding cycles.

**3. PTC thermistor input for Pakistan 45°C motor rooms**
For motors in unventilated Pakistan compressor rooms, textile departments, or outdoor pump sets in summer, a PTC thermistor directly measures winding temperature. Current can look perfectly normal while the winding is approaching thermal damage. The thermistor trips when the winding actually gets too hot — independent of what the current is doing.

**7 Pakistan motor protection mistakes (with repair costs):**

1. Setting OL at 125% FLC "because the motor runs hot" — PKR 55,000–200,000 rewind cost
2. Bimetallic relay for Class 30 loads (crushing mills, sugar rollers) — PKR 40,000–120,000 damage
3. Ignoring WAPDA 2–4% asymmetry — PKR 100,000–350,000 motor failure every 18–36 months
4. No lockout after phase failure trip — auto-restart on WAPDA restoration causes single-phase start
5. Trip contact wired NO instead of NC — motor runs unprotected if relay supply fails
6. No CBCT for earth fault on Pakistan motors (monsoon moisture ingress risk)
7. Wrong trip class for application — either nuisance trips or too-slow overload detection

**When the 3-device combination is still fine:**
- Motors below 22kW in rural areas with stable WAPDA supply
- Agricultural tube-well pumps where cost is the primary constraint
- Non-critical loads where production shutdown is tolerable

**When you need a digital relay:**
- Any motor above 22kW on a Pakistan WAPDA industrial feeder
- Any motor in a pharmaceutical, food processing, or continuous-process plant
- Any motor in a room above 45°C (compressor rooms, outdoor summer applications)
- Any MCC with PLC/SCADA integration
