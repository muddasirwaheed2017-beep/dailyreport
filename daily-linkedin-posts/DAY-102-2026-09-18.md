## 7 battery failure modes Pakistan electrical engineers must know before the next loadshedding crisis

Pakistan's grid environment turns a 5-year VRLA battery into an 18-month replacement cycle. Here's why capacity testing every 12 months is no longer sufficient — and the 7 failure patterns you're likely missing.

**1. PSOC sulfation from loadshedding cycling**
Short outages (2–4 hours) don't allow full recharge. After 6 months of chronic partial-state-of-charge cycling, hard lead sulfate crystals form that permanently block plate surface area. Capacity falls to 60% before impedance even moves. Impedance tests miss this. Only a full discharge test catches it.

**2. Temperature derating in summer battery rooms**
IEC 60896-21 uses 25°C as reference. A battery room in Sindh/South Punjab at 42°C in July has a correction factor of 0.91. A battery delivering 83% raw capacity is actually at 75.5% corrected — below the 80% replacement threshold. How many facilities are running failed batteries thinking they passed?

**3. Generator AVR instability → over-voltage charging**
Older Cummins/Perkins sets with unstable AVRs create 10–15% output voltage variation. During generator supply, UPS charger voltage spikes above 2.35 V/cell. One over-charge event at 2.45 V/cell permanently damages VRLA electrolyte. Detection: swollen battery case, electrolyte dry-out. Prevention: over-voltage cutout at UPS rectifier input.

**4. Parallel string imbalance from generator switchover transients**
When generator connects to UPS with even a 5V residual voltage difference, the surge unevenly loads parallel battery strings. After 2–3 years, strings diverge in SoH. A failing string shifts load to healthy strings, accelerating their degradation. Test each string individually — never test aggregate strings.

**5. Harmonic plate erosion from old thyristor rectifiers**
Pre-2010 UPS units with thyristor rectifiers draw battery current with high harmonic content (THD >20%). This causes micro-cracking of positive plate active material. Capacity fades before impedance increases. Impedance testing will not catch this — only annual full discharge tests will. Solution: IGBT rectifier replacement or line reactor at UPS input.

**6. Temperature gradient string imbalance**
In a poorly ventilated battery room, upper shelf batteries run 10–12°C hotter than lower shelf batteries. Lower-shelf batteries cycle deeper, degrade faster. After 3 years, you have a heterogeneous string: some cells at 95%, adjacent cells at 65%. A single impedance scan across the string reveals the gradient pattern. Directional fan installation costs PKR 8,000–15,000. A battery string replacement costs PKR 85,000–450,000.

**7. Flooded cell water quality sulfation (older WAPDA installations)**
Rural DISCO substations still using flooded vented lead-acid batteries: if topped up with high-TDS tube-well water instead of deionised water, chlorides and sulfates contaminate the electrolyte, attacking positive grid material. SG stays low despite full charge. Use only DI water ≤0.1 µS/cm conductivity.

**The takeaway:**

In Pakistan's loadshedding environment, the standard IEEE 1188 annual capacity test interval is a minimum baseline. High-criticality facilities (hospitals, data centres, NTDC/WAPDA substations) should test every 6 months.

And every test must apply the IEC 60896-21 Annex A temperature correction. A single correction oversight in a 45°C battery room turns a passing result into a failed one.
