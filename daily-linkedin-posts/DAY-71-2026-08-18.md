**Why Pakistani buyers are getting servo stabilizers wrong — and paying PKR 25,000–150,000 for it**

After 71 days of documenting Pakistan's electrical buying patterns, voltage stabilizers are the product category with the most dangerous spec mismatches.

Three issues we see repeatedly:

**1. The ±20% trap**
Standard international stabilizer datasheets spec ±20% input range: 176V–264V. Pakistan's WAPDA feeders in Lahore, Peshawar, and rural Punjab routinely drop to 155V–165V in summer. When input falls below the stabilizer's minimum, the unit bypasses — your load runs unprotected at dangerously low voltage. The Pakistan-correct specification is 150V–270V (±26% on 230V nominal).

**2. The AC inrush sizing failure**
A 1.5-ton split AC draws 6–7A running. Compressor starting inrush: 20–26A for 0.5–1.5 seconds. We see buyers install 2 KVA stabilizers (rated ~9A continuous) on 1.5-ton ACs. The servo motor stalls on every AC start, overheats, and burns within 18–24 months. Correct rule: 1.5 ton AC → 4 KVA minimum.

**3. The solar inverter placement mistake**
Installing a servo stabilizer on the OUTPUT of a solar inverter creates a feedback conflict between the stabilizer's control loop and the inverter's PWM regulation. The correct placement is on the WAPDA INPUT to the inverter — protecting the inverter's charging circuit. Inverter output is already regulated.
