**Why Pakistani factories keep burning out their Ethernet switch ports — and how to stop it**

After assessing dozens of factory automation projects across Punjab and Sindh, the single most common (and preventable) network failure we see is a consumer Gigabit switch installed in a PLC control cabinet.

Here's what happens:

1. **Summer hits.** The cabinet reaches 58°C inside. The consumer switch — rated to 40°C — throttles, reboots, or fails.

2. **WAPDA fires a transient.** Grid switching events in Lahore and Karachi industrial estates couple through 80m Ethernet cable runs into the RJ-45 magnetics. The TVS diodes absorb it once, twice — then the port is dead.

3. **A forklift clips a cable.** On a bus topology, every device downstream is offline. On a ring topology with MRP, traffic reroutes in 20ms and no PLC sees a fault.

The industrial-grade alternative costs PKR 45,000–120,000 instead of PKR 5,000–12,000 for a consumer switch. The first prevented 2-hour production stop pays back the difference.

**What to specify for Pakistan:**
- DIN-rail mount, 24 VDC (matches PLC panel power)
- Temperature rated -40°C to +75°C, fanless
- IEC 61000-4-5 Level 3 surge immunity (2kV port isolation)
- MRP (PROFINET) or RSTP (general) ring redundancy
- VLAN/QoS managed for multi-device networks
