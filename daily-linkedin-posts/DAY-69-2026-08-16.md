**The most common ATS mistake in Pakistan — and why it's a safety issue, not just an electrical one.**

With load-shedding hitting 8–18 hours per day in Pakistani cities this summer, automatic transfer switches have become standard infrastructure for commercial and industrial sites.

But more than 65% of ATS installations in Pakistan have a fundamental problem: they're 3-pole units on a WAPDA TN-C-S supply system.

Here's why that matters:

**Pakistan's WAPDA system is TN-C-S.** The neutral and earth (PEN conductor) are bonded at your service entrance, then separated into N and PE inside your building.

When you use a 3-pole ATS and transfer to generator:
→ The generator provides power through the three phase conductors
→ But the neutral remains connected to WAPDA's system
→ Your generator's neutral is floating relative to your local earth
→ Neutral-to-earth voltage can rise to dangerous levels
→ Downstream RCCB protection may fail to operate correctly

**The fix is simple: always specify 4-pole ATS.** The 4th pole breaks the neutral. When on generator supply, the neutral bonds locally to your generator earth electrode — creating a proper, safe system.

---

**For solar net metering sites, there's an additional NEPRA requirement most installers miss:**

NEPRA Grid Code Amendment 4 (2023) requires that your ATS prevent the solar inverter from energising the WAPDA feeder when supply is absent (anti-islanding).

The consequence isn't just a fine (PKR 50,000–250,000). An energised "dead" feeder puts WAPDA linemen at risk.

A 3-source ATS controller (WAPDA → Solar → Generator priority) with an inverter inhibit interlock is the NEPRA-compliant solution.
