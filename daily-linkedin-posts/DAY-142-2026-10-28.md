🔄 **The Pakistan Encoder Mis-Specification Problem — And How to Fix It**

After working with Pakistani industrial engineers across textile, cement, and food processing sectors, I keep seeing the same three encoder mistakes. Here's how to avoid them.

**Mistake 1: Over-specifying PPR**

A 10,000 PPR encoder on a standard VFD application costs 80% more than a 1,024 PPR unit — and can exceed your PLC high-speed counter's frequency limit (10,000 PPR × 3,000 RPM ÷ 60 = 500 kHz, while many PLC modules max out at 100 kHz). Result: missed counts, wrong position, unexpected trips.

VFD speed feedback: 1,024 PPR is almost always sufficient.

**Mistake 2: Optical encoders in Pakistan's industrial environments**

Karachi's 85% coastal humidity and Faisalabad's textile dust destroy optical encoder discs within months. The solution isn't more frequent cleaning — it's switching to IP67 magnetic encoders. No optical path, no disc to fog or clog. The slightly lower PPR ceiling rarely matters for standard industrial applications.

**Mistake 3: Incremental encoder on a gearbox linear axis**

After a WAPDA power cut, an incremental encoder has no idea where it is. On a vertical axis or a long-travel gearbox drive, homing into an obstruction causes machine damage. The rule: any linear axis with a gearbox → multi-turn absolute encoder, preferably Wiegand (battery-free) for remote Pakistan installations.
