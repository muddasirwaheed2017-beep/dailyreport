**Is your electrical panel MCB actually 6kA — or just labelled that way?**

PSQCA's 2023 market surveillance found that 41% of MCBs sold in Pakistani wholesale markets labelled "6kA" could not actually clear a 6kA fault current without catastrophic failure.

This is a life-safety issue — not just a technical detail.

Pakistan's electrical environment imposes five unique stresses on MCBs that international selection guides ignore completely:

**1. DISCO fault level variability**
K-Electric's Karachi urban Zone 1 has a prospective short-circuit current (PSCC) of 8–12kA. A "6kA" MCB that actually fails at 3kA will explode — not trip — in a Karachi urban panel.

**2. Load shedding thermal cycling**
4–16 switching operations per day in load-shedding areas vs ~5 per year in international design assumptions. The bimetal strip in a cheap MCB will drift its calibration after ~5,000 cycles, causing either nuisance trips (too sensitive) or failure to trip (fire hazard).

**3. 45°C+ summer ambient temperature**
IEC 60898-1 calibrates MCBs at 30°C. At 45°C, you must derate continuous current rating by ~20%. A 32A MCB in a Karachi summer panel is effectively only a 27A device. Most Pakistani installers ignore this.

**4. Solar PV DC circuits**
DC arcs do not self-extinguish. An AC MCB installed on a solar string will sustain a DC arc until it catches fire. Use IEC 60947-2 / UL 489B rated DC MCBs for all solar DC circuits — this is one of the most dangerous mistakes in Pakistan's solar boom.

**5. Counterfeit market**
17 importers/manufacturers were found selling counterfeit PSQCA-marked MCBs in Pakistan in 2024. Always demand a third-party test certificate (KEMA, DEKRA, SGS, Bureau Veritas) with a lot-traceable number.
