**The most dangerous number in a Pakistani factory is the voltage reading on the MCC incomer.**

Not because the number is wrong — it's usually correct. Because it's *incomplete*.

A voltmeter reads L1=398V, L2=402V, L3=407V and reports three correct measurements. What it cannot tell you is the negative-sequence voltage unbalance factor: in this example, 0.76% — acceptable by IEC standards.

But move that same reading to L1=388V, L2=402V, L3=410V — a spread that looks "close enough" — and the unbalance factor is 3.2%. Per IEC 60034-26, that translates to approximately 25% additional rotor heating on every motor connected to this feeder. Run for 12 months at 125% of rated temperature, and motor insulation life drops by 50–60%.

WAPDA industrial feeders in Punjab and KP routinely reach 3–5% voltage unbalance during peak load-shedding bypass periods, when single-phase loads dominate shared LT networks. We documented this in our Day 52 guide on motor protection relays (IEC 60034-26 data on 4% unbalance = 32% rotor heating). The natural question that followed: how do you actually *measure* this?

**The instrument gap in Pakistan industrial maintenance**

Most Pakistani industrial maintenance departments own:
- Clamp meters (True RMS, reads current and basic PF)
- Multimeters (reads voltage accurately)
- Insulation testers (reads winding integrity after the damage is done)

What they typically don't own:
- A power quality analyser that computes negative-sequence voltage unbalance per IEC 61000-4-30
- A PQ analyser that separates displacement PF (fixable by capacitors) from distortion PF (not fixable by capacitors — active harmonic filter required)
- A PQ analyser that identifies the 5th harmonic current injection from each VFD — the parameter needed for detuned capacitor bank design to avoid resonance

The result: capacitor banks installed based on basic PF readings fail within 18 months because 5th harmonic resonance wasn't assessed. Motor protection relay negative-sequence thresholds are set by guesswork rather than by measured worst-case WAPDA unbalance. WAPDA power factor surcharges continue because nobody separated displacement PF from distortion PF before buying capacitors.

**Four tiers — what you actually need vs what you think you need**

🔴 **Entry clamp-on with PQ (PKR 8,000–25,000):** THD% as a single number. No harmonic spectrum. No unbalance calculation. Adequate for quick field checks. Not adequate for engineering decisions.

🟡 **Mid-range Class S portable analyser (PKR 45,000–120,000):** Full harmonic spectrum to 50th order, negative-sequence unbalance %, displacement vs distortion PF, voltage dip/swell logging, data export. Covers 90% of Pakistani industrial use cases. This is the correct tier for motor protection relay settings, capacitor bank sizing, and energy audits.

🔵 **Professional Class A analyser (PKR 250,000–600,000):** IEC 61000-4-30 Class A compliant. Required for NEPRA disputes with WAPDA, insurance claims on motor failure caused by supply quality, DFI-submitted energy audits. Most factories do not need Class A — energy audit firms and large industrial groups do.

⬜ **Panel-mounted DIN rail PQ meter (PKR 15,000–80,000):** Continuous 24/7 monitoring at MCC incomer. Complements the portable analyser — survey with portable, monitor permanently with panel meter.

---

**WAPDA-specific measurement protocol**

For Pakistani factories deploying a PQ analyser for the first time:

1. Log for 7 days minimum at MCC incomer — WAPDA supply quality varies dramatically by time of day (evening peak 18:00–22:00 PKT is worst) and day of week
2. Review voltage unbalance trend — set motor protection relay negative-sequence threshold based on measured worst-case, not a nameplate assumption
3. Review harmonic spectrum — identify whether 5th harmonic is present before sizing any capacitor bank
4. Separate displacement PF from distortion PF — if distortion PF is significant, capacitor banks alone will not fix the WAPDA surcharge
5. Install permanent panel PQ meter for ongoing monitoring

The PKR 45,000–120,000 investment in a Class S portable analyser is recovered in:
- One avoided WAPDA PF surcharge month (typically PKR 40,000–120,000 for medium industrial consumers)
- One avoided motor rewind from running at 130% rated temperature (typically PKR 80,000–380,000)
- One correctly sized capacitor bank vs one resonance failure replacement (typically PKR 150,000–600,000 difference)
