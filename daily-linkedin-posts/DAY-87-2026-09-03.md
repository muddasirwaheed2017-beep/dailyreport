**The economics of solar storage in Pakistan have fundamentally shifted in 2026.**

Three months ago, a Pakistani solar buyer would ask: "Do I really need batteries?"

Today they ask: "How many kWh should I get?"

Here's why the conversation changed:

---

**The NEPRA export tariff cut changed the math.**

In July 2025, NEPRA reduced the buy-back rate from PKR 19.99 → PKR 10.69/kWh.

Meanwhile, WAPDA's UR-1 residential tariff (slab 3, Aug 2026) = PKR 53/kWh + fuel cost adjustment = PKR 60/kWh effective.

The arithmetic now favours storage decisively:
- Export a unit: earn PKR 10.69
- Store a unit and self-consume: save PKR 60.00

**That's a 5.6× premium for storage over export.**

A 10kWh LiFePO4 system at PKR 450,000–700,000 now pays back in 4–6 years — and lasts 10–13 years.

---

**But Pakistan's solar market has a knowledge gap that's costing buyers.**

We've seen it repeatedly in the field:

→ Batteries sized on RATED kWh, not USABLE kWh (ignoring 80% DoD)
→ LiFePO4 installed in unventilated metal containers — cells hitting 50°C in Sindh summers
→ BMS-inverter protocol mismatches (CAN vs RS485) causing SoC read errors
→ Grade B cells sold under Grade A pricing in Lahore and Karachi markets
