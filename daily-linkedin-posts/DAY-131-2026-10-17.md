## Post: Gear Reducer Selection for Pakistani Industry — Starting a New Cluster

**Why gear reducers fail early in Pakistan — and a practical 2026 buying guide**

We've just published Pakistan's most comprehensive gear reducer and gearbox buying guide at CNC Electric — and it starts with the three mistakes that destroy gearboxes in Pakistani plants within 2–3 years:

**1. Undersized service factor**
Pakistani industrial standard practice often uses SF 1.0 (smooth continuous). But flour mills jam and restart multiple times per shift, and LESCO/FESCO load-shedding delivers 4–8 restart cycles per day with locked-rotor current spikes. The correct SF for a Faisalabad flour mill is 1.50 (moderate shock) + 0.25 (load-shedding restarts) = **SF 1.75 minimum**.

**2. Wrong mounting position**
Worm reducers have specific oil fill/drain/vent plug positions for each mounting orientation (B3/B5/B6/B7). Receiving a B3-configured unit for a B6 vertical installation and not re-plugging the vent = oil starvation on the upper bearing = failure within 3,000 hours.

**3. Wrong lubricant viscosity for Pakistan's climate**
ISO VG 220 (standard for worm reducers) oxidises at 48°C Sindh summer ambient. Above 40°C, switch to VG 320 or VG 460 synthetic, and add a forced-air cooling fan for units at ratios above 60:1 (efficiency drops to 65–72%, all heat in the housing).
