**Is your DISCO bill charging you a hidden tax every month?**

For Pakistani industrial consumers, the reactive energy surcharge isn't a footnote — it's a significant operational cost that most facility managers underestimate.

Here's what the numbers look like for a 500 kW textile facility in Faisalabad running at power factor 0.73:

• Reactive energy surcharge (kVArh): ~PKR 655,000/month
• Demand billing uplift (kVA vs kW): ~PKR 175,000/month
• **Total monthly penalty: ~PKR 830,000**

An Automatic Power Factor Correction (APFC) panel — properly sized at 400 KVAR with detuned reactor-capacitor banks — corrects the PF to 0.94 and eliminates both charges. Installed cost: PKR 750,000–900,000. Payback: 8–10 months.

---

**Why Pakistani sites need detuned reactor-capacitor banks (not standard capacitors)**

Pakistan's industrial sector has seen aggressive VFD adoption over the past 5 years — particularly in textile, cement, and engineering. VFDs generate 5th and 7th harmonic currents. When standard (non-detuned) capacitor banks are energised in these environments, they can resonate with the bus inductance at exactly these harmonic frequencies, amplifying harmonic currents 5–10×.

The result: capacitor fuses blow within 2–6 months. Capacitor dielectric failure is common. In severe cases, transformer overheating follows.

The solution is detuned series reactors (p-factor = 7%), connected in series with each capacitor bank step. They shift the LC resonant frequency below the 5th harmonic (to ~189 Hz), so the bank presents high impedance to harmonic currents rather than amplifying them. Detuned banks are mandatory where VFD load exceeds 20% of total connected load — which describes most Pakistani industrial sites built after 2018.

**The generator interaction problem (unique to Pakistan's loadshedding context)**

This is the most frequently underestimated APFC failure mode in Pakistan.

During loadshedding, industrial sites switch to captive generation. Generator bus characteristics differ fundamentally from the WAPDA grid: the generator's short-circuit ratio is 3–5× lower, meaning reactive currents cause proportionally larger voltage swings.

If the APFC panel continues switching large KVAR banks at the same rate as in grid mode, it can push the generator terminal power factor into leading (capacitive) territory. The AVR senses rising voltage and reduces excitation — but if over-compensation is sustained, some AVRs fail to respond fast enough, causing terminal voltage overshoot to 110–115% Vn and triggering the generator's over-excitation protection trip.

The fix is straightforward: wire the generator auxiliary contact (52G) to the APFC controller's "generator mode" input. In this mode:
• Limit maximum switched KVAR to 50–60% of total installed KVAR
• Increase step switching interval to 30–60 seconds (vs 5–10 seconds on grid)
• Lock out further switching if PF exceeds 0.98 leading
• Add 60-second APFC activation delay after generator transfer

This configuration eliminates generator-APFC interaction trips.

**NEPRA PF penalty threshold: stricter than you think**

NEPRA's reactive energy surcharge triggers below 0.85 lagging — compared to India's 0.90 and Gulf states' 0.92. For D-category industrial consumers on some DISCOs, there's also a maximum demand penalty in kVA for consumers who sustain below-threshold PF for more than 3 months.

Target PF for Pakistani industrial APFC installations: 0.92–0.95 lag. Not unity — unity target causes PF controller hunting between leading and lagging. Not exactly 0.85 — too close to the penalty threshold with no margin for load variation.
