**Active Harmonic Filters in Pakistan: Why "Generator Island Mode" Is Non-Negotiable**

Pakistan's industrial facilities operate in a power environment unlike anywhere else in the world.

12–16 hours of daily loadshedding means every large manufacturer switches between WAPDA supply and diesel generator 1–3 times per day. This switching event completely changes the harmonic landscape:

📊 On WAPDA supply: source impedance ~0.05–0.08 Ω
📊 On diesel generator: source impedance ~0.4–0.8 Ω (8–15× higher)

Why does this matter for harmonic filters?

Standard active harmonic filters (AHF) assume a stiff, low-impedance supply source. Their current injection control loops are tuned for that assumption. When installed in Pakistan and the supply switches to generator, the high generator impedance causes the AHF control loop to interact with the AVR — the result is AHF oscillation, nuisance tripping, or generator instability.

**We have seen facilities where an AHF that worked perfectly on WAPDA became unusable after generator changeover.**

The solution: specify AHF units with a verified "Generator Island Mode" (GIM) or "Weak Grid" operating mode. This is a distinct control algorithm that reduces the current controller bandwidth and adds damping compensation for high-impedance sources. It is not a standard feature on all AHF products — it must be explicitly specified and verified with a test certificate.
