**Why thermography matters MORE in Pakistan than almost anywhere else:**

🌡️ **WAPDA restart transients amplify loose connections.** NTDC's 220 kV grid averages 4.2 voltage dip events per day during summer peak (May–August). Each restart cycle drives a current surge through every high-resistance joint in your facility. A joint running at ΔT = 15°C under steady-state load can spike to ΔT = 60°C during a WAPDA restart — that's NFPA 70B Priority 1 territory (repair immediately).

🌡️ **Pakistan's thermal budget is half of Europe's.** At 48°C ambient, a motor with ΔT = 70°C is running at 118°C surface — only 37°C below Class F winding limits. The same motor in a temperate climate has 65°C of margin. Thermography reveals which assets are approaching their absolute thermal limits, not just their relative ones.

🌡️ **NEECA SRO 1065(I)/2023 now requires documented thermographic evidence.** Large Energy Users (>500 MWh/year) submitting NEECA energy audits must include thermographic survey reports signed by an ISO 18436-7 Level II certified thermographer. Facilities with an existing programme reduce their audit compliance cost by 20–35%.

**The five mistakes I see repeatedly in Pakistani industrial thermography:**

1️⃣ Scanning panels under <40% load (Ramadan shifts, early mornings) — NFPA 70B minimum is 40% rated load. Light-load surveys miss Priority 2–3 findings.

2️⃣ Using default emissivity (0.95) on bare copper busbars — copper emissivity is 0.03–0.07. That 100°C busbar reads as 42°C on a default setting. Use black tape reference patches.

3️⃣ Comparing to ambient instead of a reference connection — in 48°C summer, everything looks "warm." Use ΔT methodology, not absolute temperatures.

4️⃣ Skipping post-repair verification images — without a before/after thermal pair, you have no documented proof the repair worked. NEECA auditors and insurance surveyors both ask for verification images.

5️⃣ Using Level I-only thermographers for NEECA audit documentation — SRO 1065 requires ISO 18436-7 Level II certification. Level I can collect data under Level II supervision, but cannot sign the report.
