**How much is WAPDA load shedding costing your bearings? (And how to quantify it before the motor fails)**

Most Pakistani factory maintenance managers I speak with can tell me the last time a critical motor tripped. Almost none can tell me how many stress cycles that motor had accumulated in the 6 weeks before it failed.

That's the gap vibration monitoring closes.

---

Here's what actually happens during a WAPDA load shedding restart:

When grid power is restored and your 90 kW pump motor re-energises across the line, it draws 6–8× rated current for 3–10 seconds. This isn't just an electrical event — it's a mechanical impulse. The bearing cage, outer race, and inner race experience a shock load that contributes to race fatigue and lubricant film breakdown.

In a typical Pakistani industrial facility running in LESCO/FESCO territory, that's 8–15 unplanned restarts per 24-hour period during summer peak season. Over 200 restarts per month, every month.

ISO 20816 (the successor to IEC 10816) gives you the measurement framework to track the cumulative effect:
- Zone A (≤1.12 mm/s velocity RMS): New or recently serviced machine
- Zone B (1.12–2.80 mm/s): Acceptable long-term operation
- Zone C (2.80–7.10 mm/s): Warning — investigate within 2 weeks
- Zone D (>7.10 mm/s): Danger — plan maintenance within 48 hours

A well-implemented vibration monitoring programme detects the progression from Zone A to Zone C over 4–8 weeks, giving your team a planned maintenance window instead of a 3-day emergency shutdown.

---

**The Pakistan-specific ROI calculation:**

In cement plants, a forced outage on an ID fan or kiln drive typically costs PKR 2–8 million per day in lost production and emergency spare parts. A 4-channel online CMS with wired IEPE accelerometers and ISO 20816 alarm zones costs PKR 390,000–880,000 installed.

That's a payback period of less than one avoided failure event.

For textile mills — where 50–200 motors run 22 hours/day — the cumulative bearing replacement savings over 12 months typically return 3–5× the initial investment. For sugar mills, where the 100–120 day crushing season means a mill drive bearing failure during peak operations is a potential PKR 5 million event, the case is even stronger.
