**Commissioning an electrical installation in Pakistan? Here's what the Electricity Inspector actually checks — and why getting the sequence wrong costs you the connection.**

After 125+ days writing technical electrical guides for the Pakistani market, the single most common question from contractors: *"Why did the EI reject my test certificate?"*

Here's the correct commissioning test sequence (IEC 60364-6 order — dead tests first, live tests last):

1. Visual inspection
2. CPC continuity (200mA low-resistance tester — null your leads first)
3. Ring-main continuity (if applicable)
4. **Insulation resistance** — 500V DC, all circuits dead, neutral lifted, SPDs removed
5. Polarity verification
6. Earth electrode resistance (3-pole FOP method)
7. → ENERGISE →
8. Earth fault loop impedance (Ze at origin, Zs at each circuit furthest point)
9. RCD trip-time test (IΔn and 5×IΔn per IEC 61008/61009)
10. Functional tests
11. PAT testing of portable appliances

**Why sequence matters:**
- IR test before energisation catches wiring faults before they become fires
- Loop impedance test requires live supply
- RCD test without loop impedance = incomplete — both go on the EI-7 certificate

**The most common Pakistan-specific failures I've documented:**

| Failure | Root Cause |
|---------|-----------|
| IR test fails whole installation | SPD / neon lamps left in during test |
| Zs exceeds MCB Zs limit | Undersized CPC on long 1.5mm² circuit |
| RCD doesn't trip within 300ms | S-type (time-delayed) RCD misapplied in socket position |
| Earth electrode Ra > 200Ω | Dry/rocky soil; single rod without bentonite compound |
| Motor IR < 0.5MΩ before first run | Storage in open yard during monsoon season |

**Pakistan's regulatory framework:**
- Pakistan Electricity Rules 1937 (Rules 44–50) — statutory requirement
- NEPRA Connection Regulations 2010 — DISCO service entry
- IEC 60364-6 — international verification standard increasingly cited by LESCO
- PSQCA PS 3646 — local wiring regulations
