**Why Pakistani automation engineers keep specifying the wrong compute platform — and how to fix it**

After working on SCADA and factory automation projects across Karachi, Faisalabad, and Lahore, I've seen the same mistake repeated: a commercial-grade PC running a 24/7 SCADA system.

It looks fine during commissioning. It fails within 18 months.

**The three Pakistan-specific failure modes that eliminate commercial hardware:**

**1. WAPDA voltage transients destroy commercial PSUs**
Grid restoration after load-shedding produces 270–310V AC spikes lasting 50–200ms. Commercial PC power supplies fail at the electrolytic capacitors within 50–200 such events. Industrial IPC power supplies are rated to 264V AC continuous with built-in transient suppression.

**2. Pakistan climate destroys fan-cooled hardware**
Faisalabad textile sheds: 70–90% RH year-round (cotton fiber + yarn moisture). Karachi coastal plants: 45–48°C ambient + 80–85% humidity June–August. Fan bearings corrode within 6 months. Fanless IPCs with aluminum conduction cooling eliminate moving parts entirely — and maintain IP65 rating that fan vents would destroy.

**3. Commercial SSDs corrupt SCADA historian data**
WAPDA outages are abrupt — no clean shutdown signal. Commercial SSDs without power-loss protection capacitors will corrupt any in-progress write. Industrial-grade CFast and SATA SSDs with PLP capacitors absorb the energy to complete the write before voltage collapses. This distinction is rarely specified and almost never discussed with Pakistani panel builders.

**The hardware watchdog timer — the most underspecified item in Pakistan:**
Any unattended IPC (remote pump station, DISCO substation, unmanned satellite office) needs a hardware watchdog timer that restarts the system within 60 seconds of OS freeze — independently of the OS. Software watchdogs are useless when the OS itself is frozen after a voltage event.
