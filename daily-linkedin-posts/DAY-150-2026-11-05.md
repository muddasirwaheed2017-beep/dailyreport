## Draft Post

**Most Pakistani factories are running their SCADA, PLCs, and ERP on the same flat network.**

I've seen it in textile mills, cement plants, and food processing units across Punjab and Sindh. The IT team installed a Cisco or other commercial firewall at the internet edge, assumed the job was done, and moved on.

That assumption is now a liability.

---

**What changed?**

Three things converged in 2026:

1. **NCA (National Cyber Authority)** issued a Critical Infrastructure Protection directive. Industrial operators in power, water, gas, and manufacturing are now within scope. CERT-PK incident reporting is mandatory above defined thresholds.

2. **EU NIS2 Directive supply chain provisions** — if you export textiles, food, or manufactured goods to the UK or EU, your buyer's cybersecurity questionnaire (CTPAT, BCI Sedex/SMETA) now includes OT/factory network questions. A flat-network SCADA system is an audit failure.

3. **Ransomware targeting OT specifically increased 87% in Asia-Pacific in 2025** (ICS-CERT data). Pakistani factories are not exempt.

---

**What IEC 62443 Zone & Conduit actually means in practice:**

The standard doesn't require a ₨50M project. For a mid-size Pakistani factory:

- **Level 0 (Field):** PLCs, sensors, drives — no internet, no IT crossover
- **Level 1 (Control):** HMI, DCS, SCADA — industrial network only
- **Level 2 (Supervisory):** Historian, MES — DMZ layer
- **Level 3 (Site operations):** ERP interface, remote access — IT network
- **Dedicated OT firewall** between L2 DMZ and IT — not your IT UTM

A dedicated industrial firewall (DIN rail, -40°C–+70°C, fail-open/fail-secure, Modbus/DNP3 deep packet inspection, 4G LTE OOB management) costs ₨185,000–₨320,000 for entry-level. That's the price of one WAPDA surge incident.
