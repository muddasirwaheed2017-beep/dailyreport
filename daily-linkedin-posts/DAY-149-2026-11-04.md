→ NEECA audit is coming up. The auditor wants 15-minute interval kWh/kg data for the past 12 months.

This is 100% preventable with a data historian.

**What a data historian actually does:**

A historian is a time-series database that stores every sensor reading — pressure, temperature, current, flow — with accurate timestamps and compression. It auto-calculates 15-minute energy summaries, peak demand, power factor, and specific energy intensity (kWh/kg) as continuous calculated tags. The NEECA dataset generates itself.

**The 2026 open-source stack for Pakistani SMEs:**

For a factory with 1,000–5,000 tags, you don't need OSIsoft PI (PKR 42–70 lakh for 1,000 tags):

• **InfluxDB 3.0** (free OSS) + Telegraf OPC-UA plugin — handles millions of writes/second
• **TimescaleDB** (free OSS) — full SQL, connects to Power BI and Grafana natively
• **Factry Historian** (AGPL) — GUI-based, OPC-UA native, no code required

Total cost for server + edge IPC + OPC-UA gateway: PKR 5–9 lakh. One avoided compressor failure pays for it.

**The WAPDA problem you must solve before anything else:**

Cloud-only historian architectures fail in Pakistan. WAPDA load-shedding creates internet outages. Without an on-premise edge historian on a UPS-backed industrial PC, every power cut is a data gap in your NEECA record.

Three-layer resilience design:
1. PLC internal circular buffer + OPC-UA Historical Access for backfill
2. Edge historian on UPS-backed fanless IPC (4-hour runtime)
3. IIoT gateway with 72-hour store-and-forward for remote sites

**The MES layer above the historian:**

Once you have the historian, the MES (Manufacturing Execution System) links process data to production orders, shifts, and product grades. For Pakistani textile exporters, MES is becoming mandatory: EU CBAM and buyer sustainability codes need per-metre or per-kg energy intensity at batch level. OEE below 60% is common in unmonitored Pakistani factories — MES reveals 15–25 percentage points of hidden losses.
