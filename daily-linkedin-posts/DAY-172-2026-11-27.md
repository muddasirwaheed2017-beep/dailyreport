Electric chain hoists are the most common lifting solution in Pakistani factories — and the most commonly mis-specified.

Three mistakes we see every week:

**1. Duty class underspec**
CD1 is rated FEM M3 (800 design hours). A textile mill running 3 shifts does 300+ cycles/day. That's M5 territory (3,200 h). Running M3 hardware at M5 duty means brake pads every 3 months and motor rewinds every 18 months. The fix: specify MD1 at FEM M5. The price difference is 30–40%. The maintenance saving is 2–3× that.

**2. I-beam sized for static load only**
The dynamic load factor (φ₂) for a fast-start hoist is 1.2–1.3. A 2T hoist starting quickly effectively loads the beam at 2.4–2.6T. Add the runway beam self-weight and the safety margin for deflection ≤ L/750. An IPE-200 is fine for 2T at 4-metre span. At 7-metre span you need IPE-240. We've seen IPE-160 beams with visible permanent set after 6 months of 2T use. A structural engineer review costs PKR 15,000. A beam collapse costs lives.

**3. Festoon cable installed without slack**
Cable must loop between carriages at 1.5× the carriage spacing. Too tight: carriages bind and the cable connector pulls out of the pendant. Too loose: cable drags on the floor and abrades. Use LAPP ÖLFLEX CRANE or equivalent reeling-grade PVC — standard flexible cable fails in 6–12 months on a hoist that moves 100 times/shift.
