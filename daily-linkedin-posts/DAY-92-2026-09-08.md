**Why Pakistani factories keep burning motors they just tested as "OK"**

I've been documenting Pakistan-specific electrical engineering data that doesn't exist anywhere else online. Today's topic: motor insulation testing (megger testing), and why the standard Pakistani approach fails.

**7 things Pakistani engineers get wrong about megger testing:**

**1. Using PS:596 500V test voltage**
Pakistan Standard PS:596 specifies 500V DC for motors up to 440V. IEEE 43-2013 (the correct reference) specifies 1000V for routine maintenance, 2000V for acceptance testing. At 500V, early insulation breakdown is invisible — the motor reads "excellent" and fails in 3 months.

**2. No temperature correction in summer**
Pakistan summer ambient 45-55°C drops insulation resistance by 4-8× compared to the IEEE reference temperature. A motor at 50°C winding temp needs its IR reading multiplied by 2 before comparing to the pass/fail table. Without this, you're comparing summer readings to winter baselines — meaningless.

**3. Testing dirty motors**
Cotton fiber from textile processes (Faisalabad/Karachi) absorbs moisture and reduces surface resistivity. Test result reads motor failure when it's just contamination. Clean first, test second. Always.

**4. No post-rewind baseline**
~60% of Pakistani motors have been rewound. Most rewind shops don't provide a post-rewind megger certificate. Without that baseline, you cannot trend the motor's insulation health — ever. 15-minute PI test at 1000V DC immediately after rewind, witnessed by your engineer or with a certificate, fixes this permanently.

**5. Not discharging VFD DC bus before testing**
VFD-driven motors have DC bus capacitors in the drive. Applying a megger without first discharging the DC bus risks damaging the megger, shocking the technician, and blowing the VFD's IGBT modules. Always verify zero DC voltage at VFD terminals before testing motor windings.

**6. Annual-only testing in high-risk environments**
Rural PESCO/TESCO/QESCO areas experience 3-14 load-shedding events daily. Each one generates switching transients that thermally cycle the winding insulation. Textile mill cotton dust, VFD common-mode voltage, outdoor storage during monsoon — all these mean quarterly minimum testing, not annual.

**7. Acting on single readings instead of trends**
A motor trending from 500 MΩ to 50 MΩ over four quarters is in trouble, even though 50 MΩ passes the minimum. Trend analysis, not point-in-time thresholds, is the correct basis for motor maintenance decisions. This requires: consistent test voltage, consistent temperature correction, consistent logging.
