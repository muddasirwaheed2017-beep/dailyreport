**3 instrumentation specification errors costing Pakistan industrial plants money in 2026**

After working through hundreds of Pakistan plant instrumentation designs, the same errors appear on project after project:

**Error 1: Switch vs Transmitter confusion**
A pressure switch gives you YES/NO at one setpoint — PKR 1,200–8,000. A pressure transmitter gives you a continuous 4-20mA signal proportional to process pressure — PKR 8,500–85,000. The wrong choice means either severe overspend (transmitter where a switch suffices) or a process that cannot be controlled or logged (switch where a transmitter is needed). Rule: if your PLC has an analogue input card and you need to trend, log, or continuously control based on pressure, you need a transmitter.

**Error 2: Wide-range SMPS not specified for 4-20mA loop supplies**
Pakistan WAPDA voltage sags to 170–180VAC during load-shedding peaks. An SMPS rated "220V ±10%" cuts out at 198VAC — below Pakistan reality. When your loop supply drops out, every 4-20mA transmitter on that supply reads 0mA. The PLC sees below the 4mA live-zero — spurious alarms, and potentially spurious protective trips. Specify 85–264VAC or 100–240VAC wide-range SMPS for every Pakistan instrument loop power supply. The cost difference is negligible; the reliability difference is significant.

**Error 3: Thermocouple extension cable — using copper instead of matched extension wire**
The most common Pakistan thermocouple wiring error: running copper multicore cable from the thermocouple head back to the panel (because it's cheaper than type-K extension cable). This creates a spurious second junction at every connection point. Result: systematic 5–15°C temperature offset that shifts with ambient temperature — the reading looks plausible but is wrong. Always use extension cable that matches your thermocouple type (K-type with K-type extension). This is not optional — it is fundamental thermocouple physics.
