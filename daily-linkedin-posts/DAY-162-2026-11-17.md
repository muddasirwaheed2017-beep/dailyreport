## Topic: Solar DC Protection — The Hidden Risk in Pakistan's Solar Boom

**A PKR 80,000 Inverter Failure. A Completely Avoidable Roof Fire. And the Same 10-Word Explanation Every Time.**

"The installer used an AC MCB on the DC side."

Pakistan added over 3,000 MW of distributed rooftop solar in 2025–2026. With that growth has come a parallel rise in inverter failures, warranty voids, and in a small but growing number of cases, electrical fires — most of them traceable to one root cause: DC protection devices that were either absent, incorrectly specified, or substituted with cheaper AC-rated equivalents.

---

**Why DC Protection Is Not Optional — The Physics**

Alternating current has 100 natural zero-crossings per second (50 Hz × 2 poles). An AC circuit breaker trips at one of those zero-crossings — the arc self-extinguishes because there's literally no current to sustain it.

Direct current has no zero-crossings. A fault current on a 600V DC PV string will sustain an arc indefinitely until the contact gap grows large enough to exceed the arc voltage. An incorrectly used AC MCB on a DC circuit doesn't trip cleanly — its contacts weld shut. The result is a sustained, invisible arc behind a panel or inside a conduit that continues until the insulation burns through.

This is why a DC MCB costs more than an equivalent AC MCB. The difference in price buys permanent blow-out magnets, larger contact gaps, and arc chutes specifically engineered for DC arc extinguishing. It's not a margin item. It's physics.

---

**The Four DC Protection Devices Every PV System Needs**

**1. DC MCB or MCCB — Overcurrent + Short-Circuit Protection**
- Must be rated specifically for DC voltage (IEC 60898-1 DC or IEC 60947-2)
- 500V DC for single-string systems; 1000V DC for multi-string or higher Voc
- Two-pole series wiring (2P-1) for 500V; four-pole series wiring (4P-1) for 1000V
- PKR 1,200–7,500 depending on rating

**2. gPV String Fuses — Reverse Current Protection (2+ Parallel Strings)**
- Protects individual strings against reverse current from healthy parallel strings
- IEC 60269-6 (gPV class — designed specifically for PV DC circuits)
- Sized at 1.25 × Isc per string; never more than 2 × Isc
- PKR 180–350 per fuse — the most cost-effective insurance item on any multi-string system
- Required in a combiner box for all systems with 2+ parallel strings per MPPT input

**3. DC Surge Protective Device (SPD Type II) — Lightning & Transient Protection**
- IEC 61643-11 (Type II, Class C) — protects against induced surges, switching transients
- Must be DC-rated; AC SPDs will fail immediately on DC circuits
- Installed at inverter DC input (most common) or at combiner box
- Always requires a backup fuse (sized per manufacturer datasheet) — without it, a failed SPD in short-circuit mode sustains a DC arc
- PKR 2,800–5,500 per unit
- Effectively mandatory for any system >10 kW or in Punjab/KPK lightning zones to maintain inverter warranty

**4. DC Load Break Isolator — Visible, Lockable Maintenance Disconnect**
- IEC 60947-3 (utilisation category DC-21B)
- Required by PEPCO/DISCO within 1.8m of every grid-tied inverter
- Must be IP65 weatherproof for rooftop installations; lockable in OFF position
- Allows DISCO linemen to safely isolate PV array before working on grid-side connection
- PKR 3,500–9,500 for outdoor IP65 unit

**The 2026 Compliance Landscape in Pakistan**

NEPRA SRO 892(I)/2015 (Net Metering Regulations) specifies inverter standards and protective relays, but does not explicitly itemise every DC protection component. This regulatory gap has been exploited — some installers omit DC SPDs and string fuses to cut PKR 10,000–15,000 per installation.

However:
- IEC 62109-1 (safety of power converters for PV) requires SPD and DC disconnect provisions on the inverter DC input
- Major Pakistani insurers now require DC SPD documentation on systems >10 kW as a condition of cover
- PEPCO/DISCO grid-tie approvals require a visible lockable DC disconnect diagram in the single-line diagram submission
- LESCO/IESCO field inspections have been increasing rejections for missing or uncertified DC protection

**The practical cost of compliance:** For a 10 kW on-grid system — DC MCB (PKR 4,000) + string fuses (PKR 2,000) + DC SPD (PKR 4,500) + DC isolator (PKR 5,000) = PKR 15,500 total. Against a PKR 25,000–80,000 inverter IGBT replacement and a voided warranty, the case writes itself.
